Abstract

In its own combinations with the CMB and supernovae, DESI DR2 prefers an evolving dark energy with w0 > −1 and wa < 0 over ΛCDM at 2.8–4.2σ (DESI Collaboration 2025). This record tests the dark-energy history of the author's framework, Mathematical Materiality (MM), against DESI DR2 BAO, compressed CMB distance priors (Chen, Huang and Wang 2019) and three supernova samples, and against the growth of structure. It then proposes a phenomenological hypothesis, read with MM by coherence: the late acceleration as a reading of the observer's clock that changes with the growth of cosmic structure. Every test was registered with SHA-256 fingerprints before its computation (knowing earlier results on the same data, as each registration states); additions decided after seeing results are labelled; failed predictions are reported. An independent AI system checked each block in one to nine rounds. It is an exploratory, preliminary study.

Before starting: the words you need

The universe expands. Distant galaxies move away from us. The expansion rate, H, says how fast this happens. It is measured in km/s/Mpc: kilometres per second for each megaparsec of distance. A megaparsec is a huge distance, more than three million light years.

The late acceleration. For a few billion years the expansion has not been slowing down: it has been accelerating. The most used explanation is a dark energy, a substance that pushes the expansion.

ΛCDM. It is the standard model of cosmology. It uses a cosmological constant, Λ: a dark energy that never changes. It also contains cold dark matter (CDM).

w, the equation of state. For a substance, w is the pressure divided by the energy density. For the cosmological constant w = −1, always. If w changes over time, dark energy evolves.

The CPL model. It is the most used way to write a w that changes: w(a) = w0 + wa (1 − a). Here a is the scale factor: it is 1 today and it was smaller in the past. w0 is today's value; wa says how much w changes.

Redshift, z. The light of distant galaxies is stretched during its journey, because the universe expands. This stretching is written z. The larger z is, the farther away and the further back in time we look.

The data used for the expansion.

  • DESI DR2 measures the baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO). They are a typical distance imprinted in the distribution of galaxies: they act as a cosmic ruler.
  • The cosmic microwave background (CMB) is the oldest light of the universe. Here it enters with three summary numbers, called distance priors.
  • Supernovae are stars that explode with a known luminosity. Their distance is obtained from their light. Here three samples are used: Pantheon+, Union3 and DES-Dovekie. Another sample, DES-SN5YR, serves only as a check.

For the growth of structures other data are used: SDSS, DESI DR1 and 6dFGS (section 8).

The growth of structures. At the beginning the universe was almost uniform. Then gravity gathered matter into galaxies and clusters. The linear growth factor, D, says how much the small differences in density have grown. σ8 measures how lumpy matter is today, on spheres of 8 h⁻¹ Mpc. fσ8 says how fast structures grow.

The χ² and the Δχ². The χ² (read chi-squared) is an error score: the lower it is, the better a model follows the data. Δχ² is the difference from ΛCDM. A negative Δχ² means that the model does better than ΛCDM.

σ. It is the typical error. A result at 3σ is three typical errors away from another.

Registering. A test is registered when it is written down and dated before doing the calculations.

The translations. The author writes his ideas in words, as premises. To do the calculations they must be translated into formulas. The translations are choices of whoever does the calculations. A negative outcome hits a translation, not the premise.

At a glance

The question. DESI (2025) combined its data with the CMB and with supernovae. In these combinations the CPL model, with an evolving dark energy, is preferred over ΛCDM, the standard model with a cosmological constant (w = −1). Can the late acceleration be read in another way? Can it be an effect of how time is read by an observer who lives inside growing structures, instead of an extra substance?

The idea in a few lines.

  • We take a universe of matter and radiation only, without dark energy. It is the bare universe.
  • The expansion rate read by the observer is higher than the bare one by a factor 1 + δ0 S(z).
  • S(z) is a tracer, that is, a measure, of the growth of cosmic structures in the whole universe. It is 1 today and tends to 0 if there are no structures.
  • Mostly S = [D(z)/D(0)]^n, where D is the linear growth factor.
  • The clock model is an expansion history different from that of the published MM 2.32. A way of translating the counts of 2.32 into this clock law has not yet been built.

An example from everyday life. If your watch runs slow without your knowing it, everything you measure seems faster to you. Here the idea is similar: the clock of those who are inside structures makes the expansion seem faster.

The data. The DESI DR2 BAO, the compressed CMB distance priors and one supernova sample at a time: Pantheon+, Union3, DES-Dovekie. Δχ² is relative to ΛCDM: negative is better. σ8 is the amplitude of the matter fluctuations today on 8 h⁻¹ Mpc.

How to read the main rows.

  • The published MM 2.32 always has w ≥ −1. With today's data it is in tension, at 2.1–3.1σ.
  • The clock idea does better than ΛCDM: Δχ² from −6.6 to −11.6, depending on the supernova sample. CPL does a little better still, but it has one more parameter.
  • The version in which the clock follows its own growth does better still: Δχ² from −7.0 to −11.9.
  • The growth predicted by this version agrees with the growth measurements.

What is fitted or assumed.

  • The law that links growth and clock is assumed. The model is phenomenological, that is, chosen to describe the data. It is not derived from the principles of MM.
  • The exponent n ≈ 3.6–3.7 is fitted to the data, not derived.
  • The amplitude δ0 is fixed by today's normalisation. It is not predicted.
  • That distances follow the read rate is one more assumption. Changing only the clock would not change the measured distances.
  • The growth data and the expansion data are partly tied to each other.
  • With the same cosmological ingredients and the same dynamics of the perturbations, these data cannot tell the clock reading from a substance with the same expansion history.
  • The study is exploratory and preliminary.

Why it is proposed. A single rule answers several requirements together: acceleration without Λ, a late onset, the shape with a changing w, and a growth compatible with the data. It is coherence, not proof (section 10).

Where to look. The hypothesis is in section 7. The test of MM 2.32 is in section 4. The reconstruction from the data alone is in section 6. The growth of the 2.32 family is in section 8.

1. What this deposit is, and what it is not

What it is. It is one link of the MM programme: the one on dark energy.

What it is not.

  • It is not a derivation of dark energy from first principles.
  • It does not show that the clock reading is physically different from a dark energy with the same expansion history. With the ingredients and the perturbation rule adopted here, the two give the same growth (section 7.4).

How the work was done, in short. Each test was registered before its calculation. The registrations were written knowing the results of the earlier tests on the same data; each one says what was already known. The additions decided after seeing the results are labelled as such. The failed predictions are reported as failed. Each block was checked by a second AI assistant. The details are at the end, in How the work was done.

The closest precedent is timescape cosmology.

  • Timescape attributes the apparent acceleration to a difference between clocks. Observers inside bound structures, like galaxies, have a clock different from the mean clock over the whole volume.
  • Timescape uses walls, voids and Buchert's averaging.
  • The model of this work uses neither voids nor averages. It is a relation with a single function: it links the expansion rate read by the observer to a tracer of the growth of structures.

3. Data and procedure

The data. The combinations C1–C3 and the samples are in the label table. Third-party data are not redistributed.

The codes.

  • A fast code does the fits. It computes the background with closed formulas and uses a tabulated recombination.
  • A full check redoes the calculations at the minima with the programs CAMB 2.0.4 and HyRec-2. The dark sector is passed as a w(a) table, through PPF.
  • Where the full check fails: for K1, T8d and two variants of R1. It is declared in each note.
  • Where it passes with difficulty: T8e and T8g pass it only with the tolerance on the density widened to 10⁻⁴.

Approximations to keep in mind.

  • The CHW priors are derived within ΛCDM. Using them for other histories of the universe is an approximation.
  • The three supernova samples use the same BAO and CMB data. So they are not three independent confirmations.
  • The growth data are never summed with each other.

4. MM 2.32 against DESI DR2 (T1)

What was tested. The history of the universe published in 2.32 has w ≥ −1 at every epoch, by construction. Three readings were registered: L1, L2 and L3 (see the label table).

How.

  • For L1 and L2 an analytic test statistic was used, checked with simulations.
  • For L3 a calibration with simulations was used.
  • In total: 22 runs of 400 simulations, four extended to 2000, and 3728 tail and control simulations refitted separately.
  • The intervals are computed with the Clopper-Pearson method.

7. The hypothesis: the read expansion rate follows the growth of structures (T8)

7.1 The translation (one among several possible)

  • Bare universe. Matter and radiation (with neutrinos), and no dark energy. Its expansion rate is H_bare(z) = 100 √G(z) km/s/Mpc, where G is the physical density in ω units.
  • Read rate. H_read(z) = H_bare(z) [1 + δ0 S(z)], with S(0) = 1 and S → 0 without structures.
  • δ0. It is fixed by the flatness of the universe: (1 + δ0)² = h²/G(0). It is a normalisation, not a prediction of the amplitude.
  • The distances. D_M = ∫ c dz / H_read. This is one more physical assumption. For this reason the model is phenomenological, not a derivation from the premises on the clock.
  • The sign.
  • With this form the deceleration parameter is q = q_bare − δ0 S′/(1 + δ0 S), where S′ = dS/d ln a ≥ 0. So the growth of structures can produce acceleration.
  • For δ0 > 0, the opposite form, H_bare/(1 + δ0 S), decelerates more than the bare universe. With the same δ0 it does not preserve today's normalisation. It is used only to check the sign.
  • The author's premise «il tempo proprio rallenta dove la struttura cresce» ("proper time slows where structure grows") is read accordingly: the slowed proper time makes the measured rates appear faster.
  • The equivalence. The same H(z) can be written as an effective dark density G[(1 + δ0 S)² − 1]. Standard cosmology would read it as a substance.

7.2 What the data ask without Λ (T8a)

  • From the minima of ΛCDM, of CPL and of the band reconstruction comes H_bare(0) = 37.5–37.8 km/s/Mpc.
  • The read rate must exceed the bare one:
  • by 75–82 % today;
  • by 30–32 % at z = 0.5;
  • by 12–14 % at z = 1;
  • by 3–4 % at z = 2;
  • by about 0 at z = 5.
  • So S(z) is 0.36–0.42 at z = 0.5 and 0.15–0.17 at z = 1.

7.4 Growth with the read history (T8c, K2 at n = 3.5)

  • Growth that follows H_read (CAMB-PPF, with Planck's A_s and n_s). It is compatible with S1, S2 and S3. σ8 = 0.793–0.794, S8 = 0.815–0.819. The read age of the universe is 13.75 Gyr (billions of years).
  • Growth of the bare universe. It is incompatible even with a free amplitude (S2: χ² 36.0 for 7 points). The bare age is 17.3 Gyr.
  • Equivalence. With the same ingredients and this perturbation rule, the growth coincides with that of a history with the same H(z). So the expansion and growth data alone do not tell the clock reading from a substance.

7.5 Tracer built from the measured growth, and a check (T8d, T8e, T8f)

What was done.

  • Instead of the ΛCDM growth curve, a curve fitted to the measured fσ8 (S1 or S2) is used.
  • Growth must not be faster than in the matter era: f ≤ 1. It is an assumption of the tested family.
  • Expansion and growth are fitted together, as if they were independent. The joint covariance of DESI growth and DESI BAO is not treated.

The result (T8e).

  • It does better than ΛCDM plus a free growth curve by 8.7 / 12.7 / 10.9 with DESI growth (S2).
  • It does better by 8.2 / 11.4 / 9.8 with SDSS (S1).
  • It has as many parameters as CPL plus the curve.
  • These results are preliminary.
  • σ8 today sits at the limit set by f ≤ 1.

The check (T8f).

  • The CAMB prediction was rescaled to the fitted σ8(0).
  • For DESI growth with Pantheon+ and with Dovekie, the shape of the growth passes both registered checks, at the tested redshifts.
  • Union3 slightly exceeds the pointwise limit: 1.07 for a limit of 1.
  • Separately, the fitted present-day amplitude differs by less than 0.75 % from the CAMB prediction with the adopted Planck A_s. It is not a full statistical test.
  • With SDSS growth the check does not pass.
  • The details, including T8d and the sensitivity to the growth curve, are in the final note of the block.

7.6 Self-consistent version: the clock follows its own growth (T8g)

The definition.

  • S = (D/D0)^n, where D is the linear growth computed in the same read history.
  • It is a fixed point: the clock depends on the growth, and the growth depends on the clock. It is found by iteration, that is, by redoing the calculation until the result no longer changes.
  • There is no fitted growth curve and no growth data in the fit.
  • Parameters: h, ω_b, Ω_m and n.

The limits. The clock law, the distance rule and the linear growth, equal at all scales, are assumptions. And there is not yet any prediction that only the clock can make.

8. Growth of structures for the MM 2.32 family (G0 and part B)

G0: histories selected from T1–T7, with Planck's amplitude.

  • They are all compatible with the fσ8 of SDSS, DESI DR1 and 6dFGS according to the registered rule.
  • There is one exception: a case in tension at 2.1σ (thawing with Union3, with the full SDSS likelihood).
  • The measurements do not distinguish them from ΛCDM (|Δχ²| ≤ 0.96).

B1: the coupled MM Boltzmann code. A Boltzmann code computes how perturbations grow in the universe.

  • At three points of the chain, its growth normalised to today agrees with CAMB on the same background, within the registered thresholds.
  • The background agrees within 2.2×10⁻⁶. The MM/ΛCDM growth ratio agrees within 6.7×10⁻⁴ on the tested grid.
  • This comparison does not isolate the perturbations of the dark sector. And it does not verify the σ8 normalisation used in B3.

B3: 400 samples of the published chain of 2.32.

  • Median σ8 = 0.790 (95 %: 0.771–0.800).
  • It is below ΛCDM with the same parameters in every sample (median −0.86 %).
  • No paired Δχ² exceeds 4.
  • 2 % of the samples are in tension in the SDSS growth, only through the amplitude (at most 2.44σ).

B2: the noise of the readings. The record counts have a noise: small random oscillations. It was checked how much they disturb matter.

  • At the tested points, the root-mean-square response of matter to this noise (rms: the square root of the mean of the squares, which measures how large the fluctuation is) is bounded, for each Fourier mode (each wave into which the density is broken down).
  • The bound is 1.1×10⁻⁵ rms(δ_m) for the density and 3.8×10⁻⁵ f rms(δ_m) for δ′ = dδ/d ln a.
  • These bounds use the adopted source model and normalisations, in the branch with no additional vacuum component. They hold in linear theory, below the horizon, without back-reaction or relativistic terms.
  • In this approximation the noise is negligible (registered threshold 10⁻³).
  • It is not a bound on fσ8.

The scope.

  • The histories are selected, without MM-specific perturbations in G0.
  • A_s is fixed to Planck's central value.
  • The growth data are not all independent of the expansion data.

9. How MM 2.32 should be read now

  • The translation, not the premises. 2.32 translated the record counts into an energy density and a pressure. There it was declared as an imported effective postulate. The constraint w ≥ −1 comes from that translation, not from the author's premises.
  • The published version stays as it was computed. Its evaluation is that of section 4: "in tension".
  • Still to do. A translation of the counts of 2.32 into the clock reading of section 7 has not yet been written. Renaming the same H(z) as a "rate" would not change its w.

10. What the hypothesis answers, and what is open

The hypothesis answers several requirements together. It is coherence, not a set of independent confirmations.

  1. Late acceleration without Λ, with good fits to the DESI DR2 BAO, to the CMB distance priors and to three supernova samples.
  2. An effective w ≈ −0.8 today, which crosses −1 at z ≈ 0–0.6. It is in the range favoured by these parametrisations and by these data.
  3. A late onset, because the tracer follows the growth of structures. How late depends on the chosen form and on the fitted n; and structures grow earlier too. The reconstructions without MM place the best-constrained deviations at z ≈ 0.4–1, on the same data: a qualitative coherence.
  4. A self-consistent version whose growth, with Planck's amplitude, is compatible with the measurements.
  5. A background and an early dark fraction under control. Not all the physics of the early universe is controlled, nor the CMB spectrum.
  6. Two working premises of the author, read as one possible interpretation: proper time tied to the local structure, and space that changes only through time. The code, however, uses a global cosmological curve, not the local structure or the environment.

The author's motivation. The clock is tied to a quantity that exists and that is observed through growth (here not statistically independent of all the expansion data). A substance with the same H(z), instead, would have to be postulated for the purpose. This is the reason why the author studies the hypothesis. It is not a result of the χ² values. The observed growth does not on its own support the particular law that ties it to the clock.

What is open.

  • The exponent n is fitted: 3.5–3.7 for K2, 3.575–3.707 for T8g, up to 3.93 for T8e with SDSS.
  • The law that links growth and clock, and the distance rule, are assumptions.
  • The joint covariance of DESI growth and DESI BAO is not treated.
  • Growth enters the clock law as a single linear factor, equal at all scales.
  • There is not yet any clock-specific prediction, for example a dependence on the environment.
  • The translation of the counts of 2.32 into the clock reading is not yet written.

Glossary

  • b. A free amplitude that multiplies the predicted fσ8. It serves to test the shape of growth separately.
  • BAO. Baryon acoustic oscillations: a typical distance in the distribution of galaxies, used as a cosmic ruler.
  • Bare universe. Matter and radiation (with neutrinos), no dark energy: H_bare = 100 √G.
  • CMB. The cosmic microwave background, the oldest light of the universe.
  • CPL (w0wa). The dark-energy equation of state w(a) = w0 + wa (1 − a).
  • Dark energy. The name given to what makes the expansion of the universe accelerate.
  • Decision zones. The thresholds fixed before the calculations: holds, in tension, disfavoured, excluded.
  • Δχ². The difference in χ² from ΛCDM at the best points. Negative means a better fit.
  • fσ8. The growth rate times the amplitude of matter fluctuations on 8 h⁻¹ Mpc.
  • Growth of structures. The gathering of matter into galaxies and clusters, through gravity.
  • ΛCDM. The standard model of cosmology, with a cosmological constant.
  • MCD (in the author's language). A local structure of the relational network. Here it is represented only through the global growth of structures.
  • Phenomenological. Chosen to describe the data, not derived from a theory.
  • PPF. A treatment of the dark sector that allows w to cross −1 in the CAMB program.
  • Read rate. The expansion rate as read by the observer's clock: H_read = H_bare (1 + δ0 S).
  • Redshift (z). How much the light has been stretched during its journey.
  • Registration. A test written and dated before the calculations.
  • S8. S8 = σ8 (Ω_m/0.3)^0.5.
  • σ8. How lumpy matter is today, on spheres of 8 h⁻¹ Mpc.
  • Tracer S(z). A function of the growth of structures: 1 today and 0 without structures.
  • w. The pressure divided by the energy density. For the cosmological constant it is −1.
