Abstract

An archaeologist enters an Etruscan tomb that has never been opened. People say that plates and jugs have come back to light; to the funerary stone a plate was never a plate, but a weight discharged onto it, and the distribution of contact of its foot. Recording aspects of a shape is one thing, recognising it as a plate is another. The ceramic holds its plate form by itself and knows nothing of it. MM makes a postulate of this: matter and spacetime are the part of the matrix that enters a reading. The text says, level by level, what each statement rests on: theorem or measurement; coherent reading; postulate or analogy. A fact every physicist recognises. A generic pure state of N qubits needs 2^(N+1) − 2 real parameters to be written down: at 266 qubits they outnumber the atoms of the observable universe. Yet at most N bits of a classical message can be read out of those qubits (Holevo 1973), there is no universal copier (Wootters and Zurek 1982; Dieks 1982), and different global states can have the same parts (Schrödinger 1935). The argument runs in four steps, and the first three only organise known results: accessible facts depend on determinate physical relations, those relations do not make every property available in a single record, and different records can none the less agree on stable structures (Zurek 2009). The fourth step, that matter and geometry too are constituted within this organisation, is the postulate. A contrary check …

Before we start: the words you need

What MM is. Mathematical Materiality, MM for short, is the author's framework of ideas. Its proposal is this. Matter is not described by mathematics. Matter is made of mathematical relations. A relation links two distinct terms. In this work the proposal takes a precise form, which is its postulate: matter and spacetime are the part of the matrix that enters a reading.

The eight words of the framework. These are the words that belong to MM, and we will use them constantly.

  • Nexus: the starting link between two distinct terms.
  • Non-local: not located in space and time. For MM it is present in every thing, place and instant.
  • GCD: the greatest common divisor, that is, the largest positive integer that divides two numbers. MM takes it as the figure of the common part of two terms.
  • Matrix: the structure of the copies of a nexus, and of the constraints among them. It includes the reader. Careful: it is not a numerical matrix, it is not a density matrix, and it is not Hilbert space.
  • Lit part: the part of the matrix that a reading makes determinate.
  • Reading: an internal relation that makes some properties determinate, relative to the systems involved. Inside it there are three operations, to be kept distinct: interacting with an object, registering its properties, recognising it within a network of knowledge.
  • Entering a reading: becoming part of a physical fact relative to that relation. It does not mean being copied. And it does not mean becoming conscious.
  • Mental on/off: the distinction released in the mind, separated from what it distinguishes.

Other useful words.

  • A premise is a starting sentence of the author. It is a fixed point, like a postulate.
  • A postulate is a statement that is assumed, and not proved. It is judged by what it holds and by what it risks.
  • Registered means written, dated and fixed before the comparison, with a hash. The SHA-256 hash is a code computed from the file: if the file changes even by a comma, the code changes.

A few words of physics.

  • Qubit: the unit of information of a quantum computer. A normal bit can only be 0 or 1. A qubit can also be in a combination of the two.
  • Pure state: the most complete possible description of a quantum system.
  • Amplitude: a number that says how much a possibility «weighs» inside a quantum state. Amplitudes are complex numbers, and they can add up or cancel out.
  • Density matrix: the way of describing a quantum system that is not in a pure state. This happens when the state is not known, but also when the system is part of an entangled whole: then, taken alone, it has no pure state at all.
  • Entanglement: two particles can have a definite common state, while each one, taken alone, has no definite value.
  • Decoherence: the environment constantly knocks against an object, with gas and radiation. In this way some properties become stable and others are lost.
  • Thermoluminescence: a method for dating ceramics. The crystal accumulates a dose of radiation; on heating, the dose is read as light.

In short

In three lines. A reading does not extract from a system everything that would be needed to describe it. And yet different registers can agree on stable structures. MM takes this fact all the way, and makes it a postulate: matter and spacetime are the part of the matrix that enters a reading. Thought can refer to totalities and formulate alternatives. And around every one of its acts remains the dark of what is not said.

The starting fact. In quantum physics describing everything costs an enormous amount. But from a system one can pull out far less than would be needed to describe it. These are things every physicist recognises, and they are in section 1.

The proposal. MM makes this limit a postulate, not a consequence. Matter and spacetime are not «already there»: they are the part of the matrix that enters a reading. What remains outside does not vanish: it keeps reality, and it can enter other relations.

The image. An archaeologist enters an Etruscan tomb never opened. For the funerary stone, in two thousand five hundred years, a plate was never a plate. It was only «il loro peso in grammi scaricato sulla pietra funeraria» ("their weight in grams discharged onto the funerary stone"). And yet the ceramic kept its plate form, on its own, knowing nothing about it. The plate lights up as a plate only when a reader arrives who knows about plates.

What changes in the common view. Six sentences we take as obvious are overturned. The world made of things becomes a world made of relations. Space and time become contents, not containers. The past becomes depth of the present. The mind becomes a node in the network, not an outside eye. The table is in section 6.

What it does not do. This work derives no new number. The postulate is not proved, and no theorem cited implies it. The law that says what lights up is missing. The complete list is in section 9.

1. A fact every physicist recognises

Let us start from a count. Take N qubits in a generic pure state. In a fixed basis you describe it with 2^N complex amplitudes. These are 2^(N+1) − 2 independent real parameters.

Writing that table costs an enormous amount. At 266 qubits the amplitudes are more than the atoms of the observable universe, which are about 10⁸⁰. At 399 qubits they exceed 10¹²⁰: that is Lloyd's estimate (2002) for the bits of the universe, counting gravitational degrees of freedom too.

But careful: it is a cost of description. The qubits realise the state. They do not write it anywhere.

How much can be pulled out. From those N qubits the accessible information about a classical message is at most N bits. It is Holevo's bound (1973). With entanglement already shared, and the corresponding protocol, one reaches at most 2N.

And it cannot even be copied. There is no universal perfect copier (Wootters and Zurek 1982; Dieks 1982). To reconstruct a state, many repeated preparations of the same state are needed.

The whole does not reduce to the parts. For a general state, even a mixed one, the density matrix requires 4^N − 1 real parameters. The data of the individual qubits provide 3N of them. At ten qubits that is three parts in a hundred thousand.

From here a consequence. Different global states can have the same parts. To tell them apart, joint data are needed, which include classical and quantum correlations. Schrödinger observed it in 1935: one can know the whole at best without knowing the parts at best.

Entanglement. It is the measured form of the quantum case. Two particles can have a definite common state. And meanwhile each one, taken alone, has no definite value.

The violations of Bell's inequalities exclude one thing. They exclude that the probabilities factorise into local terms, if the measurement choices are independent of the source (Brunner and others 2014). Here three things must be kept distinct: non-separability, causal locality and the absence of faster-than-light signals. The experiments do not choose among the interpretations.

The proposal of MM. MM proposes an ontological reading of these facts. «Ontological» means: what things are, not only how they behave. The non-local phase is present in every thing, place and instant. The local is the same reality, read from a point.

This text shows what happens to the common view of the world, when the proposal is taken to its ultimate consequences. The theme seems far from what we call physics. In fact it concerns the question that physics presupposes: how much of the real can enter what is measured.

2. The common view, in six sentences

These are the six sentences we take as obvious.

  1. The world is made of things.
  2. Things are in space and last in time.
  3. Every thing has its properties, and has them even if no one is looking.
  4. What there is, is all there: reality is full of itself.
  5. The past is a storehouse: what happened stays there and can be found again.
  6. The mind is inside a head and looks at the world from outside.

MM overturns them all, starting from a single premise.

3. An Etruscan tomb never opened

An archaeologist enters an Etruscan tomb never opened. People will say that plates and jugs have come back to the light after two and a half millennia.

The author observes something else. For the funerary stone a plate was never a plate. It was «il loro peso in grammi scaricato sulla pietra funeraria» ("their weight in grams discharged onto the funerary stone"). One clarification: grams, strictly speaking, measure mass; weight is the force that follows from it.

The premise. Existing is reciprocal. Two entities exist for each other only through what they have in common (MM 2.68.2, section 1).

Think of a stone in a universe where there is nothing else. It is not a light stone. It has no weight at all, because weight is a comparison.

What plate and stone have in common. Little. Gravity offers a universal coupling: it is the guaranteed minimum. In the AI's reading it is their «one», the only unavoidable common part. Then there is contact, which also involves electromagnetic interactions.

The tomb registers more than the load. The distribution of pressure on the stone depends on the shape of the plate's foot.

Even in the dark things happen. The gas knocks against the plate, and thermal radiation interacts with it. In this way decoherence arises between distinct positions (Joos and Zeh 1985). The times depend on the separation considered and on the environmental conditions.

The ground writes even inside the ceramic. Thermoluminescence estimates the dose of radiation accumulated since the last heating, that is, the last one strong enough to reset the signal. And that dose also depends on what surrounds the vase (Aitken 1985).

A famous case shows how much can be read without entering. The muons of cosmic rays revealed in the pyramid of Cheops a large void previously unknown, with no direct access (Morishima and others 2017).

Registering is not recognising. They are two different things. The stone registers the load and the distribution of contact. The archaeologist connects these and other data to the category and the history of the plate.

The plate, meanwhile, keeps its own form on its own: every atom is bound to its neighbours. Recognising that form requires someone who knows about plates. Keeping it does not. The ceramic keeps its plate form and knows nothing about it.

For this reason whoever enters finds that plate and not another. Objects remain autonomous in their local relations (MM 2.68.2, section 4).

What «partially non-existent» means. In MM's proposal a thing exists in the relations it has, and not in those it does not have. No relation gives it everything. The author's formula is this: «il mondo che ci circonda è parzialmente inesistente» ("the world around us is partially non-existent"). It is read like this: non-existent for whoever does not have that relation. The stone does not feel anything missing.

4. Near nothing, far everything

A stone of seventy kilos and an archaeologist of seventy kilos have the same mass. The MICROSCOPE experiment compared titanium and platinum alloys in free fall: it detected no difference, with a precision of a few parts in 10¹⁵ (Touboul and others 2022).

Now put the plate on the chest of someone asleep. In substance it leaves what it leaves on the stone: load and contact. So what distinguishes them? The author's answer is four words: «vicino niente lontano tutto» ("near nothing far everything").

What the far is. The AI's reading calls far the experience acquired elsewhere, and kept in the organisation of the reader.

For two nearby bodies the cosmos is in practice the same. And distance is not necessary: someone who cannot see recognises a shape with their hands, by contact. Nor is the complexity of the structure taken alone enough. What counts is the structure formed by past readings.

These cases suggest one thing. Physical proximity and the amount of matter, on their own, do not determine recognition. They orient the interpretation, without selecting it uniquely. Here are four cases.

  • The linen. The longest surviving Etruscan text has about 1,200 words. It reached us on linen, reused to wrap a mummy. Krall published its identification as Etruscan in 1892. Today the text is read aloud, and understood only in part (Uranić 2006). The case distinguishes the persistence of the signs from their identification and understanding.
  • The network before and after. A neural network initialised at random does not yet possess the learned performance. Trained on more than a million images, it learns to distinguish a thousand classes, among them the jug (Krizhevsky and others 2012; Russakovsky and others 2015). Architecture and mass of the machine remain in practice the same. What changes are the internal parameters, and in those the far is kept (MM 2.76).
  • Those who come to see. Young people blind from birth, after surgery, at first match badly what they see with what they touch. They improve over time (Held and others 2011). The interpretation is debated, because of the role of the initial visual abilities (Schwenkler 2012).
  • Those who lose the link. In associative agnosia particular cases are documented: one copies the drawing of an object without recognising it (Lissauer 1890). The category is debated.

The nearest reader. For 2,500 years the nearest reader of the plate was the stone. Full contact, no recognition. Proximity, on its own, is not enough for recognition.

The mind does not enter the tomb alone. With it enter all the plates it has seen, the words it has learned, those who taught them. These are the «condizioni di contorno» ("boundary conditions") that reactivate a part of the world.

The object that lights up is another one. The banquet does not come back. What lights up is the find, which says things the Etruscan could not read: an estimate of the time since the last thermal reset, normally associated with firing; the clay; any lipid residues absorbed in the ceramic.

The plate today exists partly less and partly more than when it was in use. The author said it like this, of every reading of the past: «la parziale premessa della nuova storia» ("the partial premise of the new history").

5. The postulate

MM assumes this postulate: matter and spacetime are the part of the matrix that enters a reading.

Here the postulate is not deduced from the results cited. The author states its status like this: «naturalmente non ci può essere nessun teorema in tal senso altrimenti MM sarebbe inutile» ("naturally there cannot be any theorem to that effect, otherwise MM would be useless"). A postulate is judged by what it holds and by what it risks. The comparison with Einstein's postulates is one of method, and does not give it the same support.

What it says. These are three statements, to be kept distinct.

  1. Constitution. Matter and spacetime are the structures that the relations of the matrix constitute in an internal reading. The reader belongs to the same matrix.
  2. Non-saturation. Every reading determines some aspects, and does not exhaust the matrix. Not even a network of readings exhausts it.
  3. Reality of the unread. What a reading does not determine keeps relational reality: it can enter other relations. The plate keeps relations the archaeologist has not yet met.

The part that does not enter. For the author that part is crucial. It is «la più importante in quanto portatrice della parte più complessa della matrice» ("the most important, as the carrier of the most complex part of the matrix"; 9 November 2026). And it is «la base non locale della realtà che forza il cosiddetto presente ad una parte parziale della matrice di cui fa parte l'osservatore» ("the non-local base of reality that constrains the so-called present to a partial part of the matrix to which the observer belongs"; 11 October 2026).

This sentence is a premise, and it says more than non-saturation. Here no measure of that complexity is defined. And the law that would show it is missing.

The programme. It is about deriving matter and geometry from the same counting (MM 2.68.2, sections 1 and 4). The fractions read in common give objects and distances. The multiples read in sequence give time. The multiples contained and invariant give mass.

There is, however, a limit to be stated. Spacetime requires order and metric, and the number of copies alone is not enough. The structure of the counting and the physical geometry derived from it must be kept distinct.

The argument is convergent. There are four steps.

  1. The accessible facts depend on determinate physical relations. Decoherence shows how couplings make some properties stable.
  2. These relations do not make every property available in a single register. Holevo limits the accessible information. And different global states can have the same parts.
  3. Different registers can agree on stable structures. Quantum Darwinism studies how the environment spreads many records of the same property (Zurek 2009). Partial readings build a shared world: it is the tomb.
  4. MM postulates that matter and geometry too are constituted within this organisation.

The first three steps organise physical results within a coherent reading. The fourth introduces the ontological postulate. Those results make it intelligible, without imposing it.

A check the other way. Let us run the test the other way round, with classical objects. N coins can require 2^N − 1 parameters, for an arbitrary joint distribution. In an epistemic model the coins occupy a determinate configuration, and the distribution represents the ignorance about which it is. The number of parameters, on its own, does not decide the ontology.

In the quantum case two other things count: interference, and the correlations that violate Bell. Those constrain the possible explanations. But on their own they do not select the ontology of MM.

The reading limit as a postulate has already borne fruit. Rovelli (1996) poses two postulates. First: there is a maximum of relevant information that can be extracted from a system. Second: it is always possible to acquire new information.

Höhn (2017) develops a reconstruction starting from rules on the acquisition of information. Höhn and Wever (2017) complete it, selecting the quantum theory of qubits. In other forms Zeilinger (1999) and Spekkens (2007) also work in this direction.

In these works the limit is not a consequence. It is the starting point, and from it, with other assumptions, the structure follows. MM makes the same move, and extends it to matter and geometry.

The task follows from this. The postulate, on its own, does not make three things expected quantitatively: Holevo's bound, the Born rule and the value of the Bell violations. It must recover them with a law of reading. 2.48.2 explores part of one.

Whoever rejects the postulate pays a price, and the postulate has its own. What follows are comparative assessments, not results.

The nearby programmes. Some research programmes already treat geometry as derivable from other structures. They are AdS/CFT (Maldacena 1998), geometry from entanglement (Van Raamsdonk 2010), matrix models (Banks and others 1997), causal sets (Surya 2019).

Those programmes make MM's programme conceivable, and impose on it a level of precision. They do not, however, confirm the postulate. There the mathematical structure is defined, and there is no selection by reading.

A constraint, and MM's answer. Pusey, Barrett and Rudolph (2012) consider systems prepared independently, and a joint measurement. In the elementary example there are two systems; in the general case a sufficiently large number.

Under two assumptions — that of the ontological model and that of the independence of preparations — reproducing the quantum predictions requires one precise thing: non-overlapping ontic distributions for distinct pure states.

This touches whoever says that amplitudes are only ignorance about ontic states, whose distributions compose independently. They have to reckon with that result. And MM does not get out of it with the word «relation» alone. For relational mechanics the compatibility is debated (Oldofredi and Calosi 2021). Some weakenings of independence invalidate the original argument; other results recover further constraints, under additional conditions (Mansfield, arXiv:1412.0669).

The author's answer is an image. Two plates made in two distant kilns have in common «la bocca che li mangia entrambi» ("the mouth that eats them both"), which «fa esistere la fornace e i piatti non in senso materiale ma in senso totale» ("makes the kiln and the plates exist, not in a material sense but in a total sense"). In the AI's reading: what two systems prepared separately have in common is the reading that will unite them.

Where this answer can pass. The frame of the theorem assumes two things. That the distribution over physical states does not depend on the future measurement. And that the systems have intrinsic properties.

Leifer (2014) lists among the ways out exactly retrocausality and relationalism. MM has both among its premises. In the first way the distributions do not overlap when the measurement that distinguishes them is made, and they can overlap when others are made. It has the form of the mouth: it is the common reading that decides.

Pointing out a way is not walking it. The loop reading proposes that the earlier physical states depend also on the future choice of measurement (Wharton and Argaman 2020). If this dependence remains after summing over all outcomes, then an assumption of the standard frame is abandoned. Closure between preparation and outcome alone does not prove it.

There is another delicate point. Suppose the closure rule reproduces Born also for these preparations and measurements. Then it assigns zero weight to the outcomes to which quantum theory assigns zero probability. But that coincidence is built into the rule: it is not an independent check.

The task remains. For independent preparations and separate measurements, the model will have to reproduce the local and joint quantum statistics, with no operational dependence on remote choices. The absence of a joint reading is not enough to guarantee it. The ontic distributions and their composition must be specified. The complete model remains to be written.

What would make the postulate fall.

  • The construction claims to derive matter and geometry, but introduces them already complete in the ingredients.
  • Readers comparing preserved records of the same event obtain contradictions. And those contradictions cannot be traced back to disturbance, error or differences of context.
  • A law of reading fixed before the comparison produces statistics incompatible with the experiments, in its own domain.
  • The rules allow a controllable choice to modify readable messages outside the light cone.
  • A controllable variation of the future measurement modifies the distribution of the earlier records. This must be considered without selecting the data on the basis of future outcomes.
  • In the admitted model there exists an internal reading that distinguishes all the alternatives of the matrix.

Careful about what falls. A failed law hits that realisation. To hit the framework, one must first delimit the class of admitted laws. And there is a condition: the unread must be able to enter some reading. If it could never affect any, it would remain a metaphysical commitment.

And we are the stone. For the stone the plate is above all load. For us dark matter is above all gravity: a predominantly gravitational reading.

It is an analogy, and that must be said. In the standard cosmological model the ratio between cold dark matter and baryonic matter is about 5.36 (Planck Collaboration 2020). It is an external datum, and its connection with parts of the matrix remains to be defined.

What «objective» means also changes. Objective becomes what more readers have in common (MM 2.68.2, section 4). And science is the widening of that common ground.

How an overturning is measured. By three things.

A mystery stops being one. The distinction between lit part and unlit part receives here a first qualitative reading.

Something becomes measurable. This text brings no new numbers. The measurable side of MM is in the other works.

A question dies. The question «che cosa c'è, indipendentemente da ogni lettore?» ("what is there, independently of any reader?") becomes ill-posed, if the postulate holds.

One last note, an important one. On ordinary objects this reading assumes the predictions of physics, and does not propose to modify them. The plate is there. What changes is what that sentence means. And the recovery of those predictions from a general law of reading remains to be demonstrated.

7. The attempt, chance, the written

The author writes: «l'universo è solo il tentativo di una matrice di costruire uno specchio logico senza parti non locali» ("the universe is only the attempt of a matrix to build a logical mirror without non-local parts"; 2 November 2026). If non-saturation holds, this attempt has no point of arrival reachable from inside.

The direction of time is, for the author, «l'ampliamento del numero di relazioni legate a rete dentro la matrice dell'osservatore» ("the increase in the number of relations tied into a network inside the observer's matrix"; 24 October 2026). Here it must be specified which quantity grows: number of cells, entropy of the data and information about the object are different things.

What decides what lights up? According to the author «il caso si ferma fuori dalla tomba» ("chance stops outside the tomb"). It is the «non motivo», the non-reason, that brought the archaeologist there.

The form is ancient: for Aristotle chance is the accidental cause of an encounter. And there is also chance in a statistical sense. In the clay every decay is random; over large numbers the distributions allow stable estimates. That is how the vase is dated. In a statistical sense: which one is from chance, how much is from the nexus. In the model considered in MM 2.68.2, section 6, the records already written are kept as constraints.

Every encounter without a reason, once written, becomes a constraint. And it enters the far of the next reader. In this way chance becomes structure.

8. The flash

A negative sentence can be written on paper. And a circuit realises logical operations. The «not» always has a support.

What the «not» says, however, is another thing. In the tomb there is a plate. The fact that that plate is not a bowl is not one more object beside the plate. The author: «il linguaggio spazio temporale non può esprimere tutta la matrice che trova la sua completezza solo nel on off mentale» ("the space-time language cannot express the whole matrix, which finds its completeness only in the mental on/off").

The word completeness must be understood properly. Take a consistent formal system, effectively axiomatised and able to express sufficient arithmetic. That system contains propositions undecidable within it (Gödel 1931, in the form strengthened by Rosser 1936).

And the mental on/off is not even enough for itself. The author writes that it «ha bisogno di un relativo di partenza, ossia lo spazio tempo» ("needs a relative starting point, that is, space-time"; 26 October 2026). Its completeness is another one: it can refer to anything at all. One line says «tutti i numeri» ("all the numbers"), and no one has ever counted them. Language refers to totalities without enumerating them.

Anything is not everything. Referring to a totality is not identifying each of its elements.

In a finite matrix, every configuration of lit and unlit copies admits a finite description, sometimes an enormous one. Every act of thought has finite duration and memory, and identifies some of them.

Now imagine instead infinitely many countable copies. Then the configurations would be uncountable (Cantor 1891), while finite sentences remain countable. Only a part could receive a name.

Even in the finite case almost everything remains without a short name. Fix a binary description language: fewer than a fraction 2^−c of the strings of length n have a description shorter than n − c bits (Kolmogorov 1965). Two numbers to grasp the scale. On an alphabet of 27 symbols the strings of a hundred characters are about 10¹⁴³. The ways of lighting and unlighting a thousand copies exceed 10³⁰¹.

Doubt. Doubt compares what is registered with represented alternatives. The author writes that «il dubbio è non locale» ("doubt is non-local") and that «la certezza esiste solo nella cornice del dubbio» ("certainty exists only within the frame of doubt").

Here the expression is a philosophical extension of the MM vocabulary, referring to the content of the doubt. An alternative can name a place, but it is not an object that is there. It does not concern the physical localisation of mental activity. The stone registers, and does not compare with alternatives.

The final image is the author's. What exists in the complete mental on/off «è il qualsiasi cosa, nei limiti relativi di un singolo bagliore logico circondato dal cielo scuro della settima proposizione» ("is \"anything at all\", within the relative limits of a single logical flash surrounded by the dark sky of the seventh proposition").

The seventh proposition of the Tractatus calls for silence about what cannot be spoken of. The text takes up that image freely, and applies it to the readers of MM: every reader has their own limit. The stone registers load and contact, but it does not recognise the plate as a plate. For it the plate as a plate is inconceivable. And the plate is there.

Inconceivable does not mean impossible. Every sentence lights up a distinction. Around it, the dark sky.

9. What is not claimed

  • No new number is derived. This reading assumes the predictions of physics about ordinary objects, and does not propose to modify them. Their recovery from a general law of reading remains to be demonstrated.
  • The postulate is not proved. And no theorem cited implies it.
  • The relation between MM's matrix and the space of quantum states is not defined here.
  • A complete loop model for independent preparations is missing.
  • The dark sector is not explained. The law of lighting up is missing.
  • The mind does not create the world. The text is not idealism: the Moon is read continuously by the Sun and by the tides.
  • The non-local phase of MM is assumed to carry no signal transmission. No experiment proposed here separates this reading from the others.

Glossary

  • Amplitude: a number that says how much a possibility «weighs» inside a quantum state. Amplitudes are complex numbers, and they can add up or cancel out.
  • Decoherence: the process by which the environment, knocking against an object with gas and radiation, makes some properties stable and causes others to be lost.
  • Density matrix: the way of describing a quantum system that is not in a pure state. This happens when the state is not known, but also when the system is part of an entangled whole.
  • Entanglement: two particles have a definite common state, while each one, taken alone, has no definite value.
  • Entering a reading: becoming part of a physical fact relative to that relation. It does not mean being copied, nor becoming conscious.
  • GCD (greatest common divisor): the largest positive integer that divides two numbers. MM takes it as the figure of the common part of two terms.
  • Lit part: the part of the matrix that a reading makes determinate.
  • Matrix: the structure of the copies of a nexus and of the constraints among them. It includes the reader. It is not a numerical matrix, nor a density matrix, nor Hilbert space.
  • Mental on/off: the distinction released in the mind, separated from what it distinguishes.
  • Nexus: the starting link between two distinct terms.
  • Non-local: not located in space and time. For MM it is present in every thing, place and instant.
  • Postulate: a statement that is assumed and not proved. It is judged by what it holds and by what it risks.
  • Premise: a starting sentence of the author, a fixed point like a postulate.
  • Pure state: the most complete possible description of a quantum system.
  • Qubit: the unit of information of a quantum computer. It can also be in a combination of 0 and 1.
  • Reading: an internal relation that makes some properties determinate, relative to the systems involved. It comprises three distinct operations: interacting, registering, recognising.
  • Registered: written, dated and fixed with a hash before the comparison.
  • SHA-256 hash: a code computed from a file. If the file changes even by a comma, the code changes.
  • Thermoluminescence: a method for dating ceramics, which reads as light the dose of radiation accumulated in the crystal.
