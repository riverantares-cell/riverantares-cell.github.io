Abstract

Bell experiments without loopholes give CHSH values above the local bound 2, and high-precision measurements are compatible with Tsirelson's quantum bound 2√2 (Poh et al. 2015: 2.82759 ± 0.00051). This record asks what the author's framework, Mathematical Materiality (MM), must contain to respect both facts, and where it then stands among interpretations of quantum mechanics. No mathematical priority is claimed: the theorems used are published (Tsirelson; Barnum et al. 2010; Sorkin's quantum measures; Craig, Dowker et al. 2007; Aharonov's two-time formalism; Cramer; Argaman 2010; Wharton and Argaman 2020), and the record separates published results, derivations in the working notes and numerical conjectures. Predictions were registered with SHA-256 fingerprints before each test; failed predictions are reported. (1) Local readings and the Tsirelson bound. If each wing is read with the Born geometry (Euclidean state space with all its effects), no no-signalling joint event exceeds 2√2. Replacing the Euclidean ball with ℓ^p balls raises the maximum: 2^(2−1/p) for p ≥ 2 (the value of Ver Steeg and Wehner 2009), above 2√2 also for 1 < p < 2 (exact value conjectural). With marginals of length m the maximum is the quantum 2√(2 − m²). Two registered predictions failed: a hand formula, wrong for p < 2, and a three-dimensional search that stayed below the value already reached by the two-dimensional construction.
