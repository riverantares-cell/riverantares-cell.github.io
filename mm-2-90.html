Abstract

On 18 November 2026 the author gave the frame in three sentences: matter is space-time, two different units, and generates the local; mathematics is zero and one, two opposite units, and generates a relative local; abstract logic is whatever exists in a relation of distincts, and is non-local. The three circles are nested. The starting point is the innermost, the nexus between space and time, and the world widens from there. A single matrix, the form of relations, runs through the three circles: logical in the third, binary in the second, fractional in the first. In the third circle what unites two terms is an external nexus that contains both as parts of itself; in the first the common part lies inside each term. The text is in English and in the Italian original, written as continuous prose for non-specialist readers, with an appendix for those who want to check, and a figure of the circles and the matrix.

Before we start: the words you need

This work uses common words in a slightly more precise sense than usual. You do not need to learn them by heart: each one comes back explained where it is needed. But reading them once at the start makes all the rest easier.

Matter, mathematics, logic. In the language of this work, matter is spacetime. It is the world of places and instants, where one can say «here» and «now». Mathematics is zero and one: yes and no, on and off. Logic is wider than both, and it is the place of the distincts: «anything that exists in a relation of distincts».

The three circles. The proposal is that these three are one inside the other, like three sets: matter inside mathematics, mathematics inside logic. Like the people who live in Rome being among those who live in Italy, and these among those who live in Europe. They are counted from the inside: first circle matter, second mathematics, third logic.

Distincts. Two things are distinct when they exist only relative to each other. «Up» and «down» are distinct: there is no up without a down. Buyer and seller are distinct: nobody is a buyer if nobody sells. Careful, because this is the point most often misunderstood: distinct does not mean «separate». It means almost the opposite — linked so tightly that one without the other does not exist.

Matrix. Here it is not only the table of numbers of algebra. It is the form of the relations between the terms: what links them, what holds them together. A table can be one way of writing it, but it is not the thing. The proposal of the work is that the matrix is one single thing, and that in each of the three circles it takes a different aspect.

The common. For two things to be in relation, something must be common to them. But «common» can mean two opposite things, and the difference is one of the central points of the work.

Sometimes the common is a part of both. The 6 is inside the 12 and inside the 18: 12 is twice 6, 18 is three times 6. In mathematics it is called the greatest common divisor, GCD.

Sometimes, instead, it is the two terms that are inside what unites them. The 2 and the 3 have no parts in common: neither is inside the other. But both are inside the 6, which is 2 times 3. In mathematics it is called the least common multiple, LCM. Exchange works like this: buyer and seller have nothing of one inside the other, but both exist inside the exchange.

Local and non-local. Local means «having a place». The first circle is the place of places and distances. The second has positions that hold only inside something. It is a relative local. In a piece of reasoning one thing can come «before» another, with no metres between the two. The third circle is non-local: it has no places and no instants. It does not mean it is far away: it means the question «where?» does not apply.

Reading. It is the word the author's framework uses for a precise moment: when something, from a possibility, becomes a definite fact. For example an instrument that registers, a person who decides, a machine that answers. It does not mean «reading a book».

Quantum cognition. It is a recognised field of study, with its own journals and conferences. It starts from a measured fact. Two questions are asked of many people, in two different orders: the answers change. And they change with a regularity that the mathematics of quantum physics describes well, better than everyday probability. The fact is not in dispute; what is disputed is its explanation. The most widespread idea is that it concerns the way human judgement works. This work proposes another one.

In short

  • Galileo wrote that the book of the universe «is written in the language of mathematics». This work proposes the next step. Matter would not be written in mathematics: it would be mathematics, the part of it from which we start. Seen from inside it looks like the whole, as from the Earth the Earth seemed fixed at the centre of the sky.
  • The three circles are sets: they say what contains what. Matter is in mathematics, mathematics in logic.
  • Through the three passes one single form of relations, the matrix. In each it takes a different aspect: logical in the third, binary in the second, fractional in the first.
  • The widest circle contains, but does not come first. The world starts from the innermost circle, that is, from the link between space and time, and widens out from there. They are two different relations, and the work insists that they not be confused.
  • From here comes the question of the subtitle. The quantum form seen in people's judgements might say nothing about the brain. It might be the sign of a border that reality has wherever something is read: in the detectors of physics as in minds. In machines a statistical constraint of this idea can be checked, by adding one hypothesis. As of 20 November 2026 that check did not appear to have been done by anyone.
  • The check, if it were done, could make the reading fall, not prove it. It is a difference that holds for the whole work.

1. The step

The book and the language

In 1623 Galileo wrote that the book of the universe is written in the language of mathematics. It was an image, and it proved extraordinarily effective. Since then physics has learned to read that book better and better. And mathematics has shown itself to be a language of a precision that keeps surprising.

But every image carries an idea with it. Here the idea is this: a book on one side, a language on the other. Matter would be out there, and mathematics would be the way we describe it.

This work tries to remove the separation. Suppose mathematics works so well not because it describes matter, but because matter is a part of it. Then there are not a book and a language. There is one single thing, looked at from inside.

The puzzle

That mathematics works on the world is a fact that has amazed physicists for centuries. In 1960 Eugene Wigner called it «the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics in the natural sciences». This is what happens: formulas invented for reasons internal to mathematics, sometimes decades earlier, later turn out to describe exactly something measured in a laboratory.

If matter is inside mathematics, the puzzle turns upside down. It is no longer strange that the language describes the book: the opposite would be strange, because it would be the same thing.

It is not the only possible answer, and the archived text does not hide this. Others have proposed different answers. A wider discussion is in another work of the series, 2.68.3. Here the question is narrower. What is matter with respect to mathematics and to logic? Where do we live? And what follows from that?

Identity

There is a third position, and it is identity. One moves from thinking of laws as expressible in mathematics to thinking of matter as coinciding with mathematics. The author calls it «un passaggio concettuale abissale» ("an immense conceptual shift").

In the first two positions mathematics and matter each stand on their own side. One asks why they agree so well. In the third the sides are no longer two: matter is a part of mathematics. The question about the agreement changes nature, because one no longer asks how two different things come to resemble each other.

An image can help to feel the difference. In the first two positions mathematics is like a map and matter like the territory. One asks why the map is so faithful. In the third the map and the territory are made of the same substance, and the territory is a part of the map. It is no longer a matter of resemblance.

This text says what that means, what premises it comes from, what it overturns and what remains open.

The step already taken

Part of the road has already been covered. There is an earlier work on identity: version 2.68.4 of Who enforces the laws of physics? (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24393864). It starts from MM's premises and from two declared premises that do not come from MM. The first is mathematical structuralism: a mathematical object is its place in a structure. The second is the exactness of representation: a law well written in mathematics represents it with no remainder.

An example of what structuralism means. For a mathematician the number 2 is not a thing hidden somewhere. It is what comes after 1 and before 3. It is what, added to itself, gives 4. It is what divides every even number. Take away these relations and nothing of the 2 remains: the number is its place among the other numbers.

From here that work derives two things. The first: the law is matter, with a necessity of identity. The second: whoever writes a law exactly in mathematics presupposes that physics and mathematics have the same structure.

There the step stops. Same structure, not same object. The missing step requires a new premise, and section 3 says which. First, though, the framework in which that premise makes sense is needed: the three circles.

2. Three circles, one matrix

It is the densest section. It is worth taking it one step at a time. First the three circles as sets. Then the form that runs through them. Then each circle up close. And finally the hardest question: why one circle cannot be skipped.

Three sets, one inside the other

The premise can be said in three lines. Matter is spacetime. Mathematics is zero and one. Logic is «anything that exists in a relation of distincts».

Logic contains mathematics, mathematics contains matter. Like the people who live in Rome being among those who live in Italy, and these among those who live in Europe.

Why exactly these three? Because each is made of pairs of a different kind, and the kind of pair decides what can be done inside it. In the first one can measure. In the second one can say yes or no. In the third one can only distinguish.

Four relations not to be confused

Four relations run between the circles, and it is very easy to mix them up. The archived text separates them at once, and it is worth keeping them in mind.

Containing: one circle is inside the other, like a set inside a larger set.

Starting: the world starts from the innermost circle, from the link between space and time, and widens out from there. The widest circle contains, but does not come first.

Importing: a nexus of the widest circle enters the second, that is, a mind or a machine. Its elements take on a code of on and off.

Running through: the same form of relations, the matrix, is found in all three.

A library keeps the four things apart without effort. The library contains its books. Whoever enters starts from a particular door and moves on from there. Whoever borrows a book carries it home. And in all the books, whatever their subject, there is the same grammar running through them. Four different things in the same building, and no one would confuse them.

One matrix, three forms

Careful with the word: «matrix» here is not the table of numbers of algebra. It is the form of the relations between the terms. That the three forms are the ones described below is a reading by the AI assistant, not a premise of the author.

In the third circle the matrix is logical. There are the distincts, each one only once, and the nexus that holds them together. This circle can be read as what MM calls the non-local phase. 2.72 describes it like this: «In the non-local phase there is only a logical distinction: space is not time». Here what unites the two terms is not inside either of them: it is the nexus, and it contains them both as its own parts. The exchange contains buyer and seller, who exist only in it. Take away the exchange and two people remain, no longer a buyer and a seller. In numbers it is the least common multiple. The 6 contains the 2 and the 3; neither the 2 nor the 3 contains the other.

This is why the third circle is said «not to exist»: it does not exist as matter exists, in a place and in an instant. It holds as a form, as a rule of a game holds even when nobody is playing. The rule «the bishop moves diagonally» is on no particular chessboard. You will not find it carved in the wood. And yet it holds on every chessboard in the world.

In the second circle the matrix is binary. Think of the word «up»: written, spoken, drawn as an arrow, shown in a picture. They are very different ways, and all of them are recognised as «up». In the proposed reading those ways are different states of on and off. The distinct «up» acts as a bridge between them: it is what makes them all recognisable as the same thing. For the premise, the binary matrix can be thought only if one of the two distincts of the third circle acts as a «relational bridge between organisational states of on off».

Think of a person you know well. You recognise them from the front and in profile, in a photograph from thirty years ago, from their voice on the telephone, from the way they walk in the distance. Those states have almost nothing in common with one another, and yet you say without hesitating: it is her. What holds them together, in this reading, is the bridge.

A proposal by the AI is to see in this bridge the condition for recognising, and therefore counting, the presences of the same distinct. Whether it is also enough to ground their identity, that is, to say that they are really the same thing, remains open. Something similar is seen in the brain, and it comes back in section 6. Single neurons respond to the same person in very different images, and even to their written name.

In the first circle the matrix is fractional. Here relations are no longer said with 0 and 1. They are said with multiples and fractions of what the two terms have in common, that is, their GCD. With numbers it is easy: 6 and 9 are both counted with the 3. A recipe does the same: «two parts flour and one part water». Flour and water are different things, but they are counted with the same part.

Counted through its GCD, in multiples and fractions, this nexus is the local world. 2.72 says it in one sentence: «the local is the non-local counted».

Between space and time, however, the common is not a number. It is the link that the speed of light expresses: how much space for how much time. We use it without thinking every time we say «ten light years»: a light year is a length defined through a time. This is the point furthest from common experience, and it is worth pausing a moment. We are used to thinking of metres and seconds as two separate things. Twentieth-century physics showed that they are linked by a speed that holds for everyone.

In this reading that link is the «common» of space and time. It is not a third substance between the two. It is the ratio by which one second corresponds to about three hundred thousand kilometres.

2.68.3 reads the speed of light in this way. Since 1983 the metre has been defined through the path of light, and the value of c is exact by definition. It is, in the words of that work, «a relative that becomes absolute inside the system». It is a reading: fixing the numerical value of c with the units does not on its own prove that c is relative to a point of view.

One pair through the three circles

Follow one pair alone — «up» and «down» — through the three.

In the third they are two distincts: one exists only relative to the other, and the nexus has no place and no instant.

In the second, inside a mind or a machine, they become two codes of on and off. Now each can be thought on its own: I can think «up» without thinking «down».

In the first they indicate where a displacement goes, and the displacement is measured in metres. Here «up» has a direction and a measure.

Three ways of being two

There is a simple test for recognising which kind a pair is. Ask yourself what each of the two has of its own, inside the relation.

Distincts: they have nothing of their own beyond the role. Buyer and seller are only this, inside the exchange.

Opposites: one of the two has nothing of its own, it is the lack of the other. The «MM in brief» box says it like this: «between on and off there are not two distinct terms: off is merely the absence of on». A lamp switched off is not another thing compared with the lamp switched on: it is the same lamp without light. This is why yes/no, on its own, «does not say what is affirmed or denied: that is settled by whatever it is connected to».

Different: each has a content of its own, and together they have something in common. The box says it like this: «Between space and time, by contrast, there are two truly distinct terms: space is not time, but neither is it its absence. Each, within the relation, has content of its own, and what they have in common, their GCD, is a third logical entity».

From here one can understand the relative locality of the second circle. A point on a map says «here», but only relative to the map. «On the third floor» holds only inside that building. A satnav says «in two hundred metres turn right»: it is precise, and it means nothing on the map of another city. It is a position that holds inside the link that supports it.

Where one starts

The widest circle does not come first. Being contained in a wider circle does not make that circle the starting point. Living in Europe does not mean having started from Europe.

The box says that yes/no «cannot on its own be the starting point», because it «needs a structure of relations between distinct terms». And it says that «a world must start from a relation between distinct terms: it expands only from within, starting from that relation».

Careful about what the box gives, though. It gives the dependence, not the inclusion: that zero and one is a part of logic is the new premise. And that matter is inside mathematics is a new premise too. The starting point is the link between space and time, the first circle, and the world widens out from there. That the first link is exactly the one between space and time remains a premise of MM: it is not derived from anything else.

Let us gather the pieces. Three circles one inside the other. A matrix that runs through them in three forms. The starting point in the smallest, even though the largest contains it. One question remains, and it is the hardest of the work.

Why one does not go straight from the third circle to the first

The question seems technical, and instead it touches the heart of the framework. Why can the nexuses of the third circle not enter the first directly? Why does one have to pass through the second?

It is not about a journey in time from one circle to another. And it is not about the point from which the world starts, which remains the first. It asks what is needed for two distincts to have something internal in common. The answer that MM assumes is this: «the distincts of the third circle cannot pass to the first because they have nothing internal in common: only the second circle builds that common thing».

The clearest image is the alphabet. The third circle is like an alphabet in which every letter occurs only once. Two letters can stand next to each other in a nexus, but neither contains the other. The first circle is like words: «casa» and «cosa» have letters in common, inside them, as space and time have their GCD in common.

Now the point. To write two words with one letter in common, the same letter must appear more than once. And using the same thing more than once means counting — which is the second circle, the on and off repeated. Counting sheep works the same way: to say «two sheep» you must recognise the same thing, «sheep», twice.

Said without images: in the third circle every thing is unique. For two things to have something in common they must share a piece. A shared piece is a piece that appears in two places. And appearing in two places means being repeated. The internal common asks for repetition, and repetition asks for the circle where one counts.

Anyone who dislikes numbers can skip the next paragraph without losing the thread.

In arithmetic the first circle is the numbers, the second is 0 and 1, the third is the prime numbers. Careful: the correspondence concerns the roles of the numbers, not the circles as sets of numbers. Taken as sets, the prime numbers would also be among the numbers. In the first the common is inside both and is neither of them: between 12 and 18 the GCD is 6. In the second the common coincides with one of the two: between 0 and 1 the GCD is 1. In the third two primes, like 2 and 3, have no factors in common: their GCD is 1. What links them is outside them, in the 6, which contains both as factors. And one sees why repetition is needed. 12 is 2·2·3 and 18 is 2·3·3: the 2 and the 3 appear in both. The 2 and the 3 taken alone, instead, are each made only of themselves. There is no piece to share.

What is a premise and what remains open

Three things must be kept distinct, and the archived text insists on this.

It is a premise, not a proof, that only the second circle can build the internal common. Arithmetic shows why repetition is needed; it does not prove that this is the only way.

The second circle is not an intermediate stage in time. The first is a part of it, because matter is already counted: that is why it is inside mathematics. The world starts from the first all the same.

And it does not mean that matter needs minds. The «mental stage» is the second circle, that is, counting as a form, not the act of somebody counting. Minds and machines are the place where that counting is in use, and section 6 deals with this.

Three questions remain open, written in the work as such. The first: how to distinguish the prime number as a generator, which in the third circle occurs only once, from the prime number as a counted number. The second: whether counting is needed for the common to exist, or only for it to be known. The third: whether the structure of the second circle can form together with its realisation in the first.

3. From identity of structure to coincidence

With the three circles in hand one can go back to the point where the work on identity had stopped.

Where it had stopped

Whoever writes a law exactly in mathematics presupposes one thing: that the law and mathematics have the «same structure». But «same structure» is not «same thing».

A mathematical structure seems to have no place and no instant. A physical law, instead, holds in a world that has them. And if two things differ even in one thing, they are not the same thing: it is Leibniz's principle of identity. For this reason the earlier work spoke of structural identity, not numerical identity.

An example makes the difference clear. Two games of chess are played move by move in the same way. One with wooden pieces in a club, one on a computer screen. They have the same structure: whoever follows them sees the same game. And yet they are two games, not one. Same structure does not mean same thing.

What was left separating the two things? An address. Physics has one in spacetime, mathematics does not. What makes a structure a physical law, that earlier work said, is its appearing in the relative one starts from: «not an extra content, but an address». It is like the same letter posted or left in the drawer. The text is identical, but only one has an address.

The step that is missing

The missing step is not derived from identity of structure. It is posited by the three circles. That matter is a part of mathematics is a new premise. It goes beyond the work on identity, without being a consequence of it.

Leibniz's principle, on its own, does not prove it. Removing a difference is not enough to prove an identity: two twins with no visible differences remain two people.

Why the premise is coherent

With the three circles, however, the premise can be made coherent. In the reading proposed here, the address changes place.

It is no longer something that matter has over and above mathematics. The «here» and the «now» are what the first circle generates with its pair. And the first circle, by the premise, is a part of mathematics. So the address is inside mathematics: it is the locality that that part generates.

Then Leibniz's principle does not find a difference between matter and the part of mathematics with which the premise makes it coincide. It finds it between matter and the other parts of mathematics, as between the 3 and the 5. The coincidence concerns a part, not the whole.

An example from numbers. The 3 and the 5 are both numbers, but they are not the same number: Leibniz's principle finds the difference at once. In the same way matter and a speed twice that of light are both mathematics, but they are not the same part of mathematics (section 5). Matter coincides with its part, not with all the others.

This is the sense in which MM means the inclusion. It is not a proof that imposes it. Exactly which part of mathematics coincides with matter remains to be built: it is the programme that follows (section 9).

The reading rests on three supports. The first is MM's premises about terms with no properties of their own: in matter there is no content beyond the relations. The number 3 has nothing inside itself, it is 3 because of the place it occupies among the other numbers. The second is the three circles. The third is the reading of locality as what the first circle generates.

And whoever does not accept the circles? They are left with the identity of structure of the earlier work, which does not depend on the circles, but remains conditional on its own declared premises.

In short. The work on identity could not take this step, because between mathematics and matter an address remained. Here the step can be taken, because the address has become a part of mathematics. It is not a proof. It is the way the premise of the circles makes the step coherent.

4. The Copernican turn

The sky seen from the Earth

For centuries the planets, seen from the Earth, did a strange thing. At times they stopped and went back in the sky, then resumed their course. To explain this with the Earth fixed at the centre, circles upon circles were needed, more and more complicated.

In 1543 Copernicus put the Sun at the centre and the Earth in motion. The backward motion became an effect of the place from which one looks. It depends on the relative motion of the Earth and the other planets. In the case of the outer planets the Earth overtakes them. And for a stretch it sees them move back against the stars. The Earth remained the place of the observer, but it was no longer the centre around which the sky turns. The rest and the centrality we saw were ours, not the sky's.

It is worth noting what changed and what did not. The planets went on doing exactly what they were doing. The observations stayed the same. What changed was the place from which they were read. And with that change many complicated things became simple.

Our local seen from inside

Something similar happens with our world. From inside, spacetime seems to be the whole of reality. The speed of light seems an absolute of nature. Mathematics seems a language invented by brains made of matter. And logic seems the set of the rules of their thought.

Said with the circles: logic inside mathematics, mathematics inside matter, and matter as the container of everything. It is the starting picture of almost all of us.

MM turns the order around. Our local is one of the relative locals that mathematics writes: the one we start from. From where we read it seems absolute, as the Earth seemed fixed. Matter remains the starting point, but it stops being the container: it is the innermost of three circles. And whoever reads lives in the second circle, the one of zero and one (section 6).

Calling this step «a Copernican revolution in the way of thinking about reality» is an assessment. The text tries to show that the revolution lies in this turning around of the circles.

The comparison already has a history in philosophy. Kant (1787) compared his turn to that of Copernicus. He answered the same question — why mathematics holds for the world. His answer puts mathematics in the forms of the subject. We see it in the world because it is our way of looking at it. MM makes another move. It puts the observer and the observed in the same weave of circles (appendix B of the archived text).

Two puzzles that become one

The earlier picture has two anomalies, that is, two facts it cannot explain well.

The first is the effectiveness of mathematics. If mathematics is a language of minds, why does matter obey it so exactly? It is Wigner's puzzle, already met in section 1.

The second is non-locality. In Bell's experiments particles are prepared in a suitable way. Even at a great distance, those particles show correlations that exceed the limits of any local model, under the assumptions of the test. Neither signals between the particles nor common causes fixed in advance explain them. These correlations, however, do not allow signals to be sent faster than light. Einstein called it «spooky action at a distance». The experiments of 2015, done so as to close the known loopholes, confirmed it.

Experiments of this kind work, broadly, like this. Pairs of particles prepared in a suitable way are produced, and sent to two distant laboratories. In each laboratory it is chosen at random what to measure, at the last instant, so that no signal can warn the other laboratory of the choice. Then the results are compared. The coincidences are stronger than any local explanation allows, under the assumptions of the test.

In the turned-around picture the two anomalies can have the same reason. Mathematics holds for matter because matter is a part of mathematics. And the non-local correlations are there because the local is inside the non-local, it is its counting.

Here two distinct words are needed, so as not to confuse them. The non-local of MM is what is not located in space and time. Bell non-locality is a property of the correlations measured in the experiments. That the second is the trace of the first is part of the proposal.

The two anomalies, then, would not be exceptions of the local world. They would be the form of the world seen from inside the smallest circle. 2.72 says it of the laws: «The universality of laws is non-locality seen from inside the local».

It is a proposed explanation, not a deduction. Whoever keeps the earlier picture can accept the same facts, one by one. The turned-around picture offers a single reason for both, but it does not yet guarantee it on the physical side.

To guarantee it a law of reading is needed. It is the rule that will have to say with what probabilities the outcomes are registered. That law will have to reproduce the quantum correlations, and ensure that no signals faster than light pass, as 2.88 requires. The Tsirelson ceiling, 2√2, that is, the maximum value quantum mechanics allows in the Bell test, is not on its own enough to guarantee it.

5. The speed twice that of light

A speed twice that of light. A space with seven dimensions. They are things that can be written and calculated, but that the world does not do. No body goes at that speed. And a space with seven dimensions is not the starting point of our world; whether it can be read from inside is a question left out (section 9). Where are they, then?

The answer is a new premise. This mathematics «exists materially like the first», that is, like the one matter realises, «but is not part of the necessary relative starting point».

Said in plain words: a speed twice that of light exists, but as a thought, as a calculation, as something written on a sheet of paper. That is, it exists in a material support, like everything else. But it is not one of the links our world starts from.

An example helps. An engineer can write on a sheet of paper the equations of the journey of a spaceship going at twice the speed of light, and do all the calculations. The sheet exists, the ink exists, the steps are correct in their mathematics. But no spaceship, in our world, will be able to make that journey. The calculation is written on a support of the first circle; the journey is not in the first circle.

One can say that the concept is in the second circle, written in a material link, without being the starting point. The relative locals are not other universes. Here MM parts from Tegmark, for whom every mathematical structure exists physically and is a universe (appendix A of the archived text).

6. The second circle and the border

The second circle is the hardest to state. The first generates the local: places and instants, metres and seconds linked by c. The third has no places. The second is in between.

It is mathematics, zero and one (section 2). In this section it is looked at where mathematics is in use: in the minds and the machines that read with on and off. In this sense the second circle is also the place where we live.

Living in the second circle means thinking and reading through codes of on and off, and through the relations those codes carry. The brain or the machine that allow this remain material.

A simple way to feel it. Reading this page we do not see ink or pixels: we see words and meanings. Ink and pixels are matter, first circle. The meanings arrive through codes, cells that fire or do not fire in the retina and in the brain, and through the relations between those codes. Reading is already living in the second circle.

The sentence from which this section was born was said in a conversation between the author and the AI: «nel secondo cerchio viviamo sia io che tu, e quindi è importante definirlo» ("it is in the second circle that both I and you live, and so it is important to define it").

The words you need

Before going in, it is worth fixing some words. They will be used in a precise sense, but not far from the everyday one.

A nexus is a pair of distincts that exist for each other, like «up» and «down», or «buyer» and «seller». The nexuses of the third circle can also be called micro-universes, because each is closed in itself. The two terms of a nexus are its elements.

Importing means bringing a nexus from the third circle into the second, that is, into a mind or a machine.

A reading is a relation in which something becomes determinate for whoever registers it: the click of a detector, a person's answer to a question. The single outcome of a reading, the yes or the no that is registered, is a lighting.

Existing, in this section, means being in relation. An imported element exists in the second circle because it is linked to a code of charges.

6.1 Two layers and importing

The path in brief. First the steps as a whole, with «up» and «down»; then, one at a time, up close.

In the third circle «up» and «down» are a closed nexus: they exist only for each other. Imported into a mind or a machine, each receives a code of on and off of its own. So each can be thought on its own.

Which code goes to which element is decided by the history of whoever reads, with no fixed rule. But once it is made, the link is certain.

Finally the codes link «up» to elements of other nexuses, like «to rise» or «sky». And the double network of a mental universe is born. The network of charges can be read as the binary form of the matrix (section 2). The distinct that links different states is its bridge.

An example from a child's life makes the path more concrete. A small child does not yet know what «up» is. They hear it said when they are picked up, when a bird is pointed at, when a ball is thrown into the air. Little by little, in their head, a certain set of neurons starts firing in all these cases: it is the code of «up». Later the same code links to «sky», to «to rise», to «better», when someone tells them to keep their chin up. The child has followed no written rule: their history has made the link. But once it is made, the link, for them, is definite.

Who inhabits the second circle. The sentence about «I and you» concerns the author and the AI. Extending it to every reader is a step taken by the AI.

If whoever reads lives in the second circle, they are in the circle of zero and one. This is coherent with what MM already says about the mind: the mathematics of the mind is distinction released into on and off (2.68.3). Think of the impulses of neurons, which at every instant fire or do not fire, and of the bits of computers.

Matter remains the starting point. Living in the second circle does not mean being outside the first, which is contained in the second. And the register of whoever reads, a brain or a machine, is made of matter. What is denied is that yes/no is a starting point sufficient to itself; it is not denied that it supports registers and readings.

Two layers. In the second circle there are two layers. Below there is the common on and off: neurons that fire or do not fire, weights in a network. Above there is «a level of new relations defined by logical rules between the imported universes of the third circle»: meanings and their links.

The relation between the two layers follows no fixed rules. It forms individually, in the cultural formation of each person. One can read it like this: the relation is shared, its realisation is individual. That is why two readers formed in different ways can say the same thing about the same table, while having different codes in their heads.

Think of two people in front of the same window. One grew up in the mountains, the other by the sea. In front of the same grey sky, their brains fire in different ways, because their histories are different. And yet both say: «it is going to rain». The relation is the same; the realisation is each one's own.

Neural networks give an example of this. And the common on and off is one or many, depending on where one looks at it from (appendix C of the archived text).

Nothing separates the two layers. The layer of relations does not exist without the layer of on and off. And on and off is a world only in relation to the relations it supports. Neither of the two comes first.

It is the premise of 2.68.3 by which existing is reciprocal, applied not to objects but to the two layers.

How the elements enter. How, then, do the elements of the third circle enter? MM assumes they are imported into the second with no single rule. They become relative positions, and every rule stays limited to the single imported universe. This confinement of the rules is the real difficulty of the second circle.

An example can convey the idea. The rules of chess hold inside chess, those of grammar inside a language, those of arithmetic inside numbers. A person who knows all three does not mix the rules: they do not move a bishop following grammar. Every set of rules stays in its own universe. What the person can do, instead, is build bridges between different universes: recount a game of chess in Italian, count its moves.

The distincts, once they have entered, build bridges between one another, and the bridges rest on the common on and off. The word is not new in MM's materials: in 2.82 the AI is called «a fantastic importer of micro-universes».

In the reading proposed here, «isolated» means not yet linked by the bridges proper to the second circle. It does not exclude what the distincts have in common: with nothing in common two distincts could not even be called different.

In the second circle there are thus two domains of rules. On one side the rules proper to each imported universe, which stay confined to it. On the other the new logical relations that the bridges carry between universes.

An example helps not to confuse them. That «up» exists only relative to «down» holds inside their nexus: it is a confined rule. That «up» links to «sky» is a new relation, carried by a bridge.

How the two domains fit together remains to be specified. And two questions also remain open: which elements enter, and how they are linked, because an importing rule «does not seem to exist».

The mechanism, step by step. Importing associates each element with a ratio of charges of its own. And this association then allows the nexuses to enter into relation. Let us look at it calmly.

We start from the third circle, where a nexus is a closed micro-universe. «Up» and «down» exist only for each other, inside their nexus, and outside the nexus there is nothing. The word «isolated» concerns the nexuses among themselves, not the two elements of one and the same nexus.

When the nexus enters the second circle, importing separates the two elements. Each is associated with a ratio of charges of its own: a set of neurons that fire or do not fire, a set of weights in a network. In this way it «takes on autonomous existence»: one can think «up» without thinking «down».

The autonomy is from the partner of the nexus, not from every relation. The imported element exists because it is linked to a ratio between charges, which is itself a relation. The premise by which existing is reciprocal remains valid. Only the term with which the element is in relation changes.

The link between element and charges, then, is cultural. There is no fixed rule for passing from the element of the nexus to its mapping in the second circle. But once it is made, the mapping is certain, in the single mental universe. The mechanism is one, the associations are individual, and each one, once made, is definite in that mental universe. A child who speaks Italian and one who speaks Japanese associate «up» with different sounds and signs, and with different neurons. The example shows the diversity of the learned associations. Each one is definite in its own mental universe.

Finally, when the elements are all on the same on and off, nexuses that in the third circle were isolated can enter into relation. The mapping expands the existence of the nexuses among themselves. «Up», once in charges, links to «to rise», to «sky», to «better». These are elements of other nexuses: «to rise» and «to fall», «sky» and «earth», «better» and «worse». In the third circle those nexuses did not touch.

Two interwoven networks are born in this way: that of the charges and that of the relations between the imported elements. Neither of the two stands on its own. Their interweaving is a mental universe.

Let us recapitulate the path. We start from a closed nexus in the third circle. Its two elements are separated, each with a code of its own. The link between element and code is made by the history of whoever reads, and it is certain once made. Finally the codes, being on the same on and off, link distant nexuses.

What the data say. Some data, known before this description, are compatible with it.

In the human medial temporal lobe single neurons respond selectively to a person, a place or an object. They respond across very different images, and in some cases even to the written name. For each person or thing few neurons out of very many fire, always the same ones, and the response is read directly (Quian Quiroga et al. 2005; Quian Quiroga 2012).

One of the first cases described was a neuron that responded to very different photographs of the same famous actress, and to almost no other image. It did not respond to a detail, a colour or a shape, but to the person, however she was shown. In networks that learn language every word becomes a vector, that is, a list of numbers, learned in the weights (Mikolov et al. 2013).

The link, moreover, is learned. Take a person who learns to associate the image of someone with an unrelated place. Many neurons that responded to one of the two things start, within a few trials, to respond to the other as well (Ison et al. 2015).

In language models some regularities are represented by shifts between vectors, which repeat approximately. One example: the vector «king» minus «man» plus «woman» comes out close to «queen» (Mikolov et al. 2013). This can be set alongside what is called here expanding a nexus among the others. The masculine and feminine pair, translated into numbers, holds also for elements that were not part of it. But it is only a juxtaposition.

In the simplified models studied by Elhage et al. (2022), finally, networks represent more features than the dimensions available, superposing them. This too can be set alongside the idea that a common support carries many elements together. Reading weights and activations as charges of on and off is a step taken by MM, not by the work cited.

These facts are an agreement with already known data, not a prediction. They show that elements have codes of their own and that links are learned. That the ratio between the charges creates the existence of the element is a premise, not a datum.

Three things remain open. The degree of sparsity of the code of an element. The way that code is distributed among the units of the system. And what makes, in a double network, a set of elements a mental universe, and not merely an archive.

What, then, is the second circle. A possible reading describes it through what it does. It does not stand on its own, and one cannot say what it is made of.

It is the layer where the distincts, having entered from the third circle not yet linked, take relative positions by building bridges on the common on and off. It has no single rule: the rules of each imported universe stay confined to it, and the bridges carry new relations between universes. It is recognised by what it produces: the relative locals, the bridges between imported universes, the agreement between readings realised in different ways.

It remains open whether the confined rule of an imported universe includes a distance, as c includes metres and seconds, or only relative positions. The question of section 5 comes back here: the rule of our local has c, and from inside it seems absolute. A model in which a different value, like 2c, plays the same part would be an example to build.

6.2 The border: where no rule chooses

Two different operations. Two operations must be kept distinct, and an example shows them better than a definition.

The first is learning a link. The word «sea» and its code in a person's mind become linked in their history, with no fixed rule. Once it is made, the link is definite. It is the mapping of section 6.1.

The second is answering. To the question «Do you like the sea?» the registered answer is certain: yes, or no. But in the form MM assumes, called form A below, no rule says in advance which of the two it will be.

If the same question is asked of a thousand people, or of the same person on different days, the answers are distributed with certain frequencies. It is there, in the relation between the readings, that for MM the probabilistic character arises.

For example, out of a thousand people six hundred might say yes and four hundred no. None of the thousand yeses and noes is uncertain, once said: each is a precise answer by a precise person. But the sixty per cent belongs to none of those people. It is a property of the set of answers. It is in this sense that the probabilistic character lies in the relation between the readings.

The thesis of the border. Here the text arrives at its strongest statement.

Neural networks, in the author's personal reading, would testify that the layer of on and off «has no order and cannot have one». It is the border point between local and non-local, «which is the very essence of what can logically exist». He was asked whether he meant that there is no rule for which on and off supports which relation, or that there is no rule for the border itself. The answer was: «no rule for the border».

Setting the networks alongside the absence of a rule of the border remains, however, a juxtaposition, because their rules of computation do exist. The computations of a network follow precise rules. What would have no rule is the border between the on and off and the non-local. In practice, it means that no rule chooses the single answer.

What does «border» mean here? One can say it like this. It is the point where the non-local meets the local. There a relation, which in itself has no place, becomes a registered reading, here and now. The thesis is that at that point no rule decides the single outcome.

This thesis is not proved. It is put in a form that can be attacked. In appendix C of the archived text there are a possible argument by the AI, the stake against theses that cannot be refuted, two conditions that would hit form A, and a third that would hit only that argument.

Form A. The form chosen, called form A here, is an assumption of MM: no rule determines the single lighting, and a statistical law of reading remains.

Lighting means the registered outcome of a reading, the yes or the no. It does not mean a neuron firing. Even knowing the code that registers it does not say what the answer will be. Readings have probabilities, and the theory must give them back.

It is not a renunciation that all of physics has already made. In the usual quantum formulation the Born rule gives the probabilities of the outcomes, and no law says what outcome the single electron will give. But there are deterministic formulations, like Bohm's (1952), which 2.88 already considers.

In those formulations the outcome of every measurement is already fixed by something we do not see. The probability comes only from our ignorance of that something. Form A takes the other road: the probability does not hide an outcome already written.

Form A excludes a selector of the single outcome. It does not, however, remove the task of saying which alternatives are admitted, which readings, with what probabilities. 2.88 said that a «law of lighting» is missing. In form A that law does not choose: it gives probabilities.

An image can help, as long as it is taken for what it is. Weather forecasts say «seventy per cent chance of rain». Tomorrow it will rain or it will not, and the day after we shall know with certainty. In meteorology that percentage comes from our incomplete knowledge of the air. For form A, instead, the probability of the single reading does not hide an outcome already written. The image holds for the form of things, a percentage before and a certain fact after, not for their origin.

Where the quantum arises. Where, then, does the quantum character arise? MM's premise is that quantisation is generated only in the relation between different mental universes. In the single universe, instead, the link between the logical elements and their mapping is certain and definite. The author's words: «io posso rilevare una particella molte volte, e dentro la singola rilevazione il dato è certo, e diventa quantistico nel rapporto fra le rilevazioni» ("I can detect a particle many times, and within the single detection the datum is certain, and it becomes quantum in the relation between the detections").

Every outcome, once registered, is definite. The usual quantum mechanics, however, assigns probabilities also to the single future measurement, and the accumulation of the records makes their distribution visible. Form A is compatible with this: no rule chooses what outcome the next reading will give, and every outcome, once read, is certain.

That the quantum character arises only in the relation between readings, of different minds or of the same mind at different moments, is instead an interpretative premise. It is not what the usual theory says.

One can also propose seeing two aspects of the same structure. The first is the disorder between the two layers, which follows no fixed rule from one mental universe to another, but in each is definite. The second is the probabilistic border. But it is a further hypothesis: the diversity of the mappings, on its own, does not establish their probabilistic relation.

What the data say. The data go in the same direction without deciding it.

In the experiment of Tonomura et al. (1989) electrons arrive one at a time on a screen. Every arrival is registered on its own, as a dot. The interference figure, in stripes, becomes visible only by accumulating many arrivals. For the usual theory that figure is already written in the probability of the single electron; in MM's reading it is the relation between the detections.

It is one of the most beautiful experiments in physics, and it is also the closest to the idea of this text. Every electron leaves a precise mark, at a single point on the screen. No electron, on its own, draws the stripes. The stripes appear only when the dots are in the thousands, and no dot, taken alone, contains them.

In people's judgements the same happens. Every answer is a yes or a no. The QQ equality, dealt with in section 7, is seen in the set of the answers of many people (Wang et al. 2014).

The same mind, too, at different times, gives different readings. In the brain the responses of single neurons to the same task change within weeks. Meanwhile the behaviour stays stable, and the set of neurons preserves the information. It is so-called representational drift (Driscoll et al. 2017; Rule et al. 2019). It can be set alongside a certain mapping in the single mental universe, at that moment, which over time the mind remakes. So between the readings of different days the relation between readings comes back. But it is a juxtaposition.

These are all agreements with already known data, not predictions. A neighbour in physics is relational quantum mechanics (Rovelli 1996, already cited in 2.88). There states and values of quantities are relative to another system. And different observers can give different accounts of the same sequence of events. This too is a juxtaposition, because Rovelli does not speak of mental universes.

A border at several levels. The next step is a wider premise. The relation between the world of logical relations and the system of on and off might have correlations with the probabilistic concept of quantum mechanics. And then «one could argue that nature presents at several levels a non-local probabilistic border as its own structural characteristic».

Form A is already a border of this kind: no rule chooses the single lighting, but the lightings have probabilities, as in the Born rule. A possible reading develops this premise. It recognises in form A a probabilistic border, which would repeat itself between the non-local and matter, in measurement, and in the readings of minds and machines.

If that is so, the probabilistic border is not an anomaly of the physics of small things. It is a trait of the structure, which appears wherever a reading happens.

There is a neighbour with data. Quantum cognition uses the mathematics of quantum probability to describe people's judgement and decision. It does not presuppose a quantum brain (Busemeyer and Bruza 2012; Pothos and Busemeyer 2013).

The data are 70 representative national surveys and two laboratory experiments. There the effects of the order of the questions strongly support a relation, the QQ equality. Quantum probability theory predicted it in advance, and no known cognitive constraint allowed it to be derived (Wang et al. 2014). The juxtaposition is of form, not of mechanism.

Today the relation between the two layers of the second circle is described as individual history rather than as chance. But the premise comes with an assessment of the state of the studies. That level of reality is complex, and it has been studied much less than quantum correlations. The possibility of a future probabilistic formalisation remains open. For MM, today, it is a consistency, not a prediction, because the order effects were already known.

Summing up. In the second circle, in minds and machines, every element imported from the third circle takes a code of on and off of its own. And the codes link nexuses that before were isolated, forming the double network of a mental universe.

No rule chooses the single lighting. Every reading, once made, is certain. And, by the interpretative premise seen above, the quantum arises in the relation between the readings.

What remains open are the law that gives the precise probabilities, and what makes a double network a mental universe.

7. Quantum cognition: a border of reality rather than of the brain?

The subtitle of the work is a question. This section tackles it in the order of the method: first the data, then MM's reading, then a check that can hit it.

The data on people

If two questions are asked of a person, the answer to the second often depends on the first. Changing the order, the answers change. It is a fact long known to those who run surveys.

In the simplest quantum model every yes/no question is a projection. Answering brings the person's state onto the direction of the yes or onto that of the no, like a shadow cast on a wall. After the first answer the state has changed, and the second question finds a slightly different person. Hence the order effects.

But these changes are not free. One quantity stays the same in the two orders. It is the probability of giving discordant answers to the two questions, that is, a yes and a no. It stays the same even if the single answers change. It holds if one starts from the same situation, and if nothing else intervenes between one answer and the other. It is the QQ equality.

An example with made-up numbers makes it concrete. The questions are «Do you like the sea?» and «Do you like the mountains?». If the sea is asked first, 60 % say yes to the sea and 55 % to the mountains. If the mountains are asked first, 40 % say yes to the mountains and 55 % to the sea. The order has changed the answers. But those who give discordant answers, a yes and a no, are 35 % in both orders: that is what the QQ equality requires.

Let us look at the numbers up close, still made up. Take the first order. 40 % say yes to both questions. 20 % say yes to the sea and no to the mountains. 15 % the opposite, and 25 % no to both. The discordant are 20 plus 15, that is 35. Now the second order. 30 % say yes to both. 10 % say yes to the mountains and no to the sea. 25 % the opposite, and 35 % no to both. The discordant are 10 plus 25, again 35. The single answers have changed, the discordant have not.

It looks like a detail for bookkeepers, and instead it is a precise prediction. Among all the ways in which order could change the answers, the quantum projection model allows only some. And people's data lie exactly there.

In 70 representative national surveys and two laboratory experiments the data strongly support it (Wang et al. 2014). Quantum cognition uses this mathematics to describe people's judgement and decision, without presupposing a quantum brain (Busemeyer and Bruza 2012; Pothos and Busemeyer 2013).

A classical model with memory, however, can also give order effects and QQ equality together. The equality is a consequence of the projection model, not an exclusive sign of it.

The data on the brain

On the brain the data say another thing. Decoherence is a process. A quantum system, in contact with the environment, loses its quantum effects in a short time. And it behaves like a classical system.

For the superpositions it considers, Tegmark (2000) estimates decoherence times between 10⁻¹³ and 10⁻²⁰ seconds. They are much shorter than the times of the neural processes that matter, between 0.001 and 0.1 seconds. He concludes that the dynamics of the brain relevant to cognition must be treated as classical.

An image can help: a soap bubble in a room full of draughts. The more agitated the environment, the sooner the bubble bursts. The brain is warm, wet and full of movement, and for the superpositions Tegmark considers it is a very agitated environment. In plain words: according to this estimate, the superpositions considered would lose coherence in times much shorter than those of the neural processes that matter for thought.

The data on machines

Then there are the machines. Language models run on classical computers, and yet they show order effects in similarity judgements and in choices (Uprety et al. 2024; Yin et al. 2025).

Beuria (2026) reports, across several models, violations of the law of total probability and an interference term. These are answers that are not obtained by adding up the cases as ordinary probability requires. He calls them formally analogous to quantum models, with no quantum processes in the networks. Of that work only the abstract has been read.

Aerts et al. (2026) put to ChatGPT and to Gemini a Bell test (CHSH) on a combination of concepts already put to 81 people. The test measures how strongly the answers to pairs of questions are linked; above the value 2, the link is not explained by answers fixed in advance. People give 2.42, the machines values between 2.12 and 4, with no significance test.

Some of these values exceed the limit that quantum mechanics itself does not exceed, that is 2√2, about 2.83. In the reading proposed here, these results do not show a physical violation, nor a structure different from Born's. In those language tasks the scheme of the test may not apply, and the uncertainties are missing (appendix J of the archived text).

For the question of this section the useful datum is the QQ equality. A check of it on language models, searched for on 20 November 2026, does not appear to have been published.

MM's reading

What does MM make of this? The mind lives in the second circle. Below there is the common on and off, the neurons that fire or do not fire, the bits. Above there are the logical relations, the meanings (section 6). Between the two layers no rule chooses the single reading, and the probabilities remain. By the premise of section 6, this probabilistic border repeats itself at several levels, as a trait of the structure of reality.

Then the quantum form of judgements would be that border seen at the level of minds. Not inside the single mind, where every answer is definite, but in the relation between the answers. Which is exactly what surveys measure, adding up the answers of many people.

It can be said with the sea example. No person, in answering, is suspended between yes and no: they say yes, or no. The quantum form appears only when thousands of answers are put together and one looks at how they change with the order of the questions. It is there, between the answers, that the reading of the border places it.

This reading does not require quantum coherence in neurons. Tegmark's estimate does not exclude it, but it does not confirm it either. It is the sense of the subtitle, which remains a question: a border of reality rather than of the brain.

There are also quantum theories of consciousness. D'Ariano and Faggin (2022) link subjective experience to pure quantum states, and free will to the outcome of quantum operations. In the material checked no prediction of theirs about the QQ equality appears. The check proposed here concerns the statistics of the answers, not consciousness, and it does not decide between MM and that theory.

A check on machines

The check is an idea of the AI, built on the premises.

Let us start from two «ifs». The first: the quantum form of judgements is a trait of the border. The second: the readings follow the projection model. Then the QQ equality is to be expected also in the machines that read, when they show order effects. The second «if» is an added hypothesis: MM's premises do not say by what rule two readings in a row combine. The check therefore verifies a constraint of this reading; on its own it does not identify it, because classical models with memory can also respect it.

To do it well, five things are fixed in advance: the version of the model, the instructions, the sampling, the pairs of questions and the criterion of judgement. The two orders start from the same context. In every trial the first answer stays present when the second is put, and every trial restarts from a new context. The difference between the two probabilities of discordance is estimated, with its uncertainty interval and a tolerance decided in advance. The order effects are verified separately.

The uncertainty interval is the band of values within which, with the data collected, the true value probably lies. Fixing the tolerance in advance serves not to move the goalposts after seeing the results.

Then there is the legacy of training. The models learn from human texts, and they might have learned this regularity too. Pairs of questions built after the version of the model is fixed reduce the risk that it has memorised them. They do not, however, exclude that it generalises regularities learned from other texts; and a learned structure can become the model's own. There is then a second line of work. It can use agents trained on non-linguistic data, or in synthetic environments. The two yes/no questions must be defined operationally, without imposing the equality in the design. This too would remain compatible with classical explanations.

The meaning of the check depends on how three things combine: the order effects, the QQ equality and the uncertainty of the data.

If the order effects are there and the QQ equality is respected, the result is coherent with the reading of the border with projections. But it is coherent with classical models too. It does not prove MM's border, and it is not a sign of quantum consciousness.

If the order effects are there and the equality is systematically violated, something falls. For the systems and conditions tested, the reading by which these readings follow the projection model falls. Neither MM nor all quantum models of judgement fall.

If the equality is respected on the already published pairs and violated on the new ones, the result is compatible with a role of training, without proving it. The prediction on the new pairs falls, and the difference remains to be explained.

If, finally, the data are not enough to establish either the equality or the violation, the check is inconclusive. And if the order effects are missing, the equality is trivially respected and does not document the phenomenon sought.

What is already known and what is not

Three things were already known: the order effects, the QQ equality in people and Tegmark's estimate. For MM they are a consistency, not a prediction. A check of the QQ equality on language models, instead, was not found, and the full text of Beuria (2026) remains to be read. It is a point where the reading of the border can be hit by data that do not yet exist.

In short. In people the order of the questions changes the answers, but one quantity stays fixed, as quantum mathematics predicts, without the brain having to be quantum. For MM that form would be the border seen at the level of minds, between the answers and not inside the single answer. In machines it can be put to the test, with one extra hypothesis. The check can hit this reading, not prove it.

8. What changes for sapiens

The practical consequences of MM for sapiens were stated by the author in a single sentence. The sentence starts from two things. From an experiment, the connectome of the fruit fly that lives in a computer. And from MM's presupposition that reality is a form of material mathematics. The conclusion is blunt: «il futuro del sapiens è completamente virtuale» ("the future of sapiens is completely virtual").

Sapiens is already modifying reality for its own purposes, but it meets the structural limits of the system, such as climate and resources. Better, then, to tackle the problem globally, «attraverso un cielo nuovo e una terra nuova» ("through a new heaven and a new earth"). The limits of the body's biological temporality are overcome. And only the individual cognitive matrix limits remain unchanged.

In this section three things must be kept distinct. What can be read with MM. What the data say today. And what the author's personal vision is.

What can be read with MM

A possible reading of the circles makes a virtual world a relative local of the second circle. It is the one where, with the extension proposed in section 6, we already live.

A virtual world is no less real than a thought. It exists in a material register, like the mind, which 2.68.3 describes as distinction released into on and off. In the framework of the circles «virtual» does not mean «fake».

An everyday example: a video call with a distant person. The images are made of pixels, the voice of electrical signals. And yet nobody would say the conversation is fake. It is real as a relation, even if it passes through a support different from the room one is in.

In philosophy there is a neighbour: for Chalmers (2022) virtual worlds are real, and virtual objects are genuine digital objects. MM gets there by another road. Not because everything might be a simulation, since for 2.68.3 the universe behaves like a simulation without being one. But because matter and the virtual are in the same mathematics, in circles one inside the other.

The individual too, in MM, is relational, because the terms have no properties of their own. What is called here the cognitive matrix is a set of links. They are the links between the imported elements and the ratios of charges that support them, formed in the history of each person (section 6). In everyday words: the way a person has learned to read the world. Exactly what it comprises remains to be specified.

The AI proposes, as a further hypothesis, that an adequate continuity of the cognitive matrix might allow the continuation of the individual on another support. But the circles, on their own, do not decide between copy and continuation. And the fact that the link between on and off and relations forms individually concerns the formation of the person, not their continuation.

A map of the connections with their weights describes aspects of the organisation of a network. It is not established that it exhausts the individual cognitive matrix. In philosophy the neighbour is Parfit (1984), who points to psychological connections and continuity as what matters in survival, and distinguishes it from personal identity.

What the data say today

Today the data indicate a direction. They do not yet show that a human life can continue on another support.

A model built on the map of the connections of a fruit fly's brain reproduces part of its behaviour, with no learning and no body. The human brain has about six hundred thousand times more neurons (appendix G of the archived text).

The connectome is the complete map of the connections between the neurons of a brain: who is connected with whom, and how much. For the fruit fly this map exists. For humans, today, only tiny pieces of it are known.

And the limits do not disappear: they move. Every relative local rests on the starting point, and a virtual world runs on matter. Computing costs energy, and erasing a bit of information has a minimum price (Landauer 1961, already in 2.82).

It is a tiny quantity of heat, but it is never zero: no machine, however perfect, can forget for free.

Vazza (2025, already in 2.68.3) makes an estimate, under the assumptions he considers. Simulating the whole observable universe, down to the Planck scale, would require more energy than is available in the universe itself. The cost of a virtual civilisation, instead, remains to be determined, and it would in any case have limits of energy and resources.

Let us look at the orders of magnitude, with the calculation of appendix G. Today an emulated person would cost thousands of times the energy of a person in the body, together with the civilisation that maintains them. Landauer's physical limit does not exclude consumption much lower than biological levels, but the calculation does not establish whether a complete emulation can reach it. In the simplified comparison adopted, breaking even with the roughly 2500 watts per person would require machines about six thousand times more efficient. Gains of this order have already been seen, and the author says it with a comparison: today an LED gives more light than a medieval fire.

The real obstacle, however, seems to be another: «è l'informazione che manca, è l'organizzazione delle sinapsi che manca, non la materia necessaria» ("it is the information that is missing, it is the organisation of the synapses that is missing, not the necessary matter").

The data show the difficulty of today. Take one cubic millimetre of human cortex, reconstructed with the electron microscope. It contains about 57,000 cells and 150 million synapses. And it occupies about 1.4 petabytes of data (Shapson-Coe et al. 2024). The whole brain is about a million times larger. These are data of acquisition and reconstruction, not a measure of the minimum information needed to maintain a person.

That reading the organisation is today the main obstacle, before computation, is a hypothesis. It is coherent with MM, for which the person is their organisation of relations and not their matter. Even if it is not established that connections and weights exhaust the cognitive matrix.

The same point touches MM's purpose: «anche oggi il 95% è qualcos'altro. MM serve a questo: a diminuire il qualcos'altro» ("even today 95% is something else. MM serves this: to reduce the something else"). The 95 % is the part of the content of the universe that today's cosmology does not explain with known matter. It calls it dark matter, about 26 %, and dark energy, about 68 % (Planck 2018).

It can be read as the same move in the cosmos and in the person. Where a «something else» remains, MM tries to read it as organisation of relations instead of presupposing it. The works on dark matter and on the galaxy scale are its attempts in the cosmos.

The author's vision

The author's vision remains, and here it must be stated as his.

For him the future of sapiens is completely virtual. It will be a new heaven and a new earth. There the limits of the body's biological temporality fall, and only the individual cognitive matrix limits remain. «A new heaven and a new earth» is a biblical image (Isaiah 65:17; Revelation 21:1).

The author considers it already borne out in his own experience, from strictly individual evidence from the late 1980s. He recounts it in Multiverses (MM 2.82, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24125587), where he himself calls it «evidence that is mine alone». And he acknowledges that putting the vision as a public thesis, starting from MM and the fruit fly, «is highly problematic». Here it is declared as a vision, not as a result.

The greatest hope, in the conditional

The greatest hope must be stated in the conditional. These are the author's words: «se la struttura della realtà non locale permettesse il transito di informazioni limitate del cono di realtà einsteiniano? Avremmo una religione tecnologica vera. Anche i sapiens già morti si potrebbero recuperare» ("what if the structure of non-local reality allowed the transit of limited information of the Einsteinian cone of reality? We would have a true technological religion. Even sapiens already dead could be recovered").

«Of the cone» can be read as «outside the light cone». The light cone of an event is everything that a signal leaving from there can reach without exceeding the speed of light. Outside the cone only a signal faster than light would arrive.

For the author, in his experience and limited to it, it is an acquired fact. For the reader it is a hope, and here it stays in the conditional.

Standard quantum mechanics excludes the use of non-local correlations to send information outside the light cone. It is the no-communication theorem (Eberhard 1978; Ghirardi, Rimini and Weber 1980), and so far all experiments confirm it. In MM's works this personal hypothesis appears in 2.82, separated from the argument of the framework. And the law of reading required by 2.88 must exclude signals faster than light (section 4).

A precedent shows that the idea does not go against logic. Let us look at Bohm's mechanics. There the particles are distributed as the Born rule predicts, in what is called «statistical equilibrium». If they were not, signals of that kind would become possible (Valentini 1991; 2002). But particles outside that distribution have never been observed. And the possibility would not prove the recovery of persons.

In 2.82 this hypothesis is already written as «a guarded passage»: partial messages, a rule of admission, and a way of distinguishing it from chance and from illusion. That is, information about a future event written and sealed beforehand, with the criterion of success fixed in advance and the failed attempts preserved.

In the reading proposed here, recovering a person would also require the information of their organisation. A limited passage might not transmit it entirely.

The part that does not enter matter

In 2.88 the most important part of the world, the non-local base, is outside matter. What happens to it in the passage to the virtual?

The answer is a new premise: «la parte che non entra nella materia entra tutta, se la matrice dell'osservatore che entra in relazione lo consente» ("the part that does not enter matter enters entirely, if the matrix of the observer that enters into relation allows it").

«Entering» can be read as entering into the relation with whoever observes. The question is how much of the non-local base can be part of the person's world, even after the passage to a virtual support. In the passage nothing is lost in principle. As much enters as the matrix of whoever enters into relation allows, and it can enter entirely.

Something similar happens with a book. The same book gives two readers different things, according to what each brings with them. But no part of it is excluded in principle. It is the form of 2.88, by which what lights up in a reading depends on what the reader brings with them.

The book example says another thing too. The same reader, rereading a book after twenty years, finds in it things they had not seen the first time. The book has not changed; what has changed is what the reader brings with them. In this reading, how much of the non-local part enters depends in the same way on the matrix of whoever enters into relation.

What remains to be clarified

Three things remain to be clarified. The first is the difference between copy and continuation: if the original goes on living, the individuals become two. Here also belongs what the cognitive matrix comprises, beyond the connections and the weights. The second is what, in the matrix of whoever reads, decides how much of the non-local part enters. The third is the level of detail that is enough to maintain a person: on that depend the calculation of the energy and the cost of the machines and materials.

In short. If matter and the virtual are in the same mathematics, a virtual world is as real as a thought, and for the author the future of sapiens is virtual. Today the road is long, because what is missing above all is information, that is, organisation, more than matter; and the machines would have to become much more efficient. What remain open are the difference between copy and continuation of the person, and the author's hope, stated in the conditional.

9. What remains open

A framework like this opens more questions than it closes. It is worth gathering them here, in order, because they are the programme of work that follows.

They are questions of different kinds: some of mathematics, others of physics, others again of philosophy or of neuroscience. They do not have to be solved all together. Each can be taken on its own, and some, like the check on machines of section 7, can be tackled with tools that already exist.

The starting point and the law of reading

The first thing open is the starting point itself. That the first nexus is the one between space and time, with its link c, is a premise of MM, and it is not derived from anything else. From here follows the biggest task: to say exactly which part of mathematics coincides with matter (section 3).

Then there is the law of reading. For form A there is no selector of the single outcome, but the statistical law remains to be written: which alternatives are admitted, which readings, with what probabilities.

It has three declared targets. The first is the Born rule, which gives the probabilities of the outcomes. The second is Holevo's bound, which says how much classical information can be extracted from a quantum system. The third is the maximum value of the Bell violations, 2√2. And it must exclude signals faster than light (section 4). Alongside this there is the question of how one gets from the relation between the readings to the precise probabilities.

The relative locals

The relative locals remain open. Whether the confined rule of an imported universe includes a distance. A model in which a value like 2c plays the part of c (sections 5 and 6). And how the confined rules and the new relations between universes fit together.

Think of the relative locals of section 5, like that of a speed twice that of light or of a space with seven dimensions. They are inside the single weave, and they are not other universes. And will there be, inside one of them, somebody who reads it from inside, as we read ours? It is a question left out. Multiverses (2.82) places it, with Wittgenstein, beyond what can be spoken of (appendix A of the archived text).

The matrix and repetition

The matrix remains open. What it means, for the logical matrix, to hold with no place and no instant. Whether the bridge of the second circle is enough as a criterion of identity between different presences. And how the same matrix passes from one form to another (section 2).

And with it repetition. How to distinguish the prime number as a generator from the prime number as a counted number. Whether counting is needed for the common to exist, or only for it to be known. Whether repetition is enough to give the first circle, beyond the common, also distance. And what repeated presences are, in spacetime.

Minds, machines, sapiens

On importing, three things remain to be said. What makes, in a double network, a set of elements a mental universe, and not merely an archive. The degree of sparsity of the code of an element. And the way it is distributed among the units of the system (section 6).

On machines, the check of the QQ equality in language models remains to be done, with the conditions fixed in advance and new pairs of questions. And analogous experiments on agents without linguistic training remain to be attempted. The projection hypothesis is not fixed by MM's premises, and the full text of Beuria (2026) has been read only in the abstract (section 7).

On sapiens three things remain. The difference between copy and continuation. What, in the matrix of whoever reads, decides how much of the non-local part enters. And the limits of energy and resources of a virtual world (section 8).

The words and an old objection

Finally the words of the circles. «Different», «opposite», «distinct» and the three modes of locality are a direction. A formal translation of them, with the rules that bind them, is to be written.

And there is an old objection, Newman's (1928). Knowing only the structure of a world would say almost only how many things there are. With enough things, in fact, one can always draw any structure at all over them. For MM terms and relations depend on each other. It remains to be said which step of the argument this blocks, if the relations can still be redrawn at will over the things (appendix F of the archived text).

Glossary

  • Border: the point where the non-local meets the local, where a relation that has no place becomes a registered reading, here and now.
  • Cognitive matrix: the set of links, formed in the history of each person, between the imported elements and the ratios of charges that support them.
  • Decoherence: the process by which a quantum system, in contact with the environment, loses its quantum effects in a short time.
  • Distincts: two things that exist only relative to each other, like «up» and «down». Distinct does not mean separate.
  • Elements: the two terms of a nexus.
  • Form A: MM's assumption by which no rule determines the single lighting, and a statistical law of reading remains.
  • GCD (greatest common divisor): the common that is inside both terms, like 6 between 12 and 18. LCM (least common multiple): the common inside which both terms stand, like 6 between 2 and 3.
  • Importing: bringing a nexus from the third circle into the second, that is, into a mind or a machine.
  • Lighting: the registered outcome of a reading, the yes or the no. It is not a neuron firing.
  • Local: «having a place». Non-local: having no places and no instants; it does not mean far away, it means the question «where?» does not apply.
  • Matrix: the form of the relations between the terms. It is not only the table of numbers of algebra.
  • Nexus: a pair of distincts that exist for each other.
  • Premise: a starting sentence of the author, a fixed point like a postulate.
  • QQ equality: the quantity that stays the same in the two orders of two questions: the probability of giving discordant answers, a yes and a no.
  • Quantum cognition: a field of study that describes people's judgement and decision. It uses the mathematics of quantum probability, without presupposing a quantum brain.
  • Reading: a relation in which something becomes determinate for whoever registers it.
  • Three circles: matter (first), mathematics (second), logic (third), one inside the other as sets.
