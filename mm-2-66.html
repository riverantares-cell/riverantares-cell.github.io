Abstract

Standard quantum mechanics has no mass at which a superposition stops: large objects look classical because of decoherence, the environment that keeps recording where they are. Collapse models (Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber, CSL, Diósi–Penrose) add such a limit by hand. The framework says that a threshold exists: below a certain mass level the principal GCD of the point of observation and the GCD of the diffraction that generates the object become equal, and the object spreads over several positions (see the box above). The box gives no number. This deposit tries to give the threshold a number, or to say clearly why it cannot. A second part tests two later remarks of the author: the limit cannot be fixed as a mass, because a calculation of the «fractional distance» is missing; and large distant bodies stay fixed because their size makes up for the distance. It was announced in deposit 2.64 ( DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23610071 ).
