Abstract

The structure of the Standard Model can be written with known constructions. Gauge fields are transport matrices on lattice links (Wilson). The electroweak model has a Higgs doublet (Glashow, Weinberg, Salam). The charge pattern of one family is that of Pati–Salam and of Furey's number-operator construction. A single light lattice fermion with exact chiral symmetry comes from the Ginsparg–Wilson relation and Neuberger's overlap operator. This record asks whether one reading rule from the author's framework, Mathematical Materiality (MM), gives these pieces a common meaning. It goes from the data to MM: for each piece, the measured fact, the reading, whether they are compatible, the published construction that already does the same, and what would make the reading incompatible. Selected gauge-sector structures and the charge pattern of one family admit a common interpretation under stated additional hypotheses. The checks concern separate constructions, and their joint chiral gauge realisation remains to be established. No mathematical priority is claimed, and the checks provide no experimental discrimination from the Standard Model. Files labelled "registered predictions" were fingerprinted with SHA-256 before their corresponding calculations; subsequent rechecks are identified separately, and predictions not met are reported. The fingerprints were recorded locally and are not publicly time-stamped.

1. What this work is, and what it is not

The question

The Standard Model is the theory that describes the particles and the forces between them, except gravity. It works very well. But it has many different pieces: different forces, different charges, colours, masses. The question of this work is: can a single reading rule give a common meaning to the structure of the Standard Model?

By structure we mean five things:

  • which forces there are;
  • which charges the particles have;
  • how many "colours" there are;
  • why the charged weak current acts on only one "hand" of the particles;
  • where the masses come from.

The work goes from the data to MM, never the other way. For each piece of the Standard Model it states, in this order:

  • the measured fact;
  • the MM reading;
  • whether the two are compatible, that is, whether they agree;
  • the construction already published by others that does the same thing;
  • what would make the reading incompatible.

The result

Some selected structures of the gauge sector of the Standard Model admit a common MM interpretation. The same holds for the charge pattern of one family of particles. But this holds under some additional hypotheses, listed in section 3. The pieces that fit are four:

  • three colours, with eight gluon modes that interact with each other (gluons carry the strong force);
  • the four bosons of the electroweak force, with a massless photon and ρ = 1 at tree level (we explain this in section 4.5);
  • fractional charges, such as 1/3 and 2/3, with a neutral neutrino and a neutral neutron;
  • one single species of light Dirac particle on the lattice. From it a single "hand" (a Weyl sector) is selected with a projection. In the U(1) backgrounds tested, its calibrated index agrees with the topological charge.

We will explain these words one at a time. For now it is enough to know that they are four separate checks. The checks concern separate constructions. Putting them together in a single chiral gauge theory remains to be established.

The parameters are not read. The parameters are the numbers that the theory does not fix by itself. Here they are taken from the data or left open:

  • the weak mixing angle θ_W;
  • the scale v;
  • the masses;
  • the quark and neutrino mixings;
  • the strong CP angle θ̄.

What it is not

  • It is not a derivation of the Standard Model. Every construction used here is already known:
  • Wilson's lattice gauge theory;
  • the Glashow–Weinberg–Salam model;
  • the Pati–Salam charge pattern and Furey's number-operator construction;
  • the Ginsparg–Wilson relation and Neuberger's overlap operator;
  • Kaplan's domain wall.

Section 2 lists them before anything else. No mathematical priority is claimed: the work does not say that it discovered this mathematics first.

  • What is specific to MM is only the reading rule. It is the rule that gives these constructions a common meaning. Together with the rule there are the registered checks: where the rule fits the data and where it does not.
  • These checks do not make it possible to tell MM apart from the Standard Model with an experiment.

How the statements are kept apart

The text keeps four kinds of statements apart.

  • Published results by others (section 2).
  • Readings. They are the author's premises translated into physical terms. What the translation adds to the author's words is marked as an addition of the translator (section 3).
  • Numerical checks. Some predictions are "registered". This means that they were written in a file and fixed with a fingerprint before the calculation. In this way they cannot be changed after seeing the result. The checks made afterwards are labelled as such. The registered predictions that did not come true are also reported (section 6).
  • Review. Each working round, and this package, was checked by an independent artificial intelligence system. Its corrections are applied here (section 7).

3. The reading rule, in physical terms

The premises are the author's starting points, like the axioms in geometry. On 15 October 2026 there were ten of them. Others come from earlier: from the MM book and from 27 September 2026. Here they are stated in physical terms. The author's exact words, in Italian, are only in the original registrations. There is also an informal account of the picture behind them, with its words explained. But nothing in the checks depends on that account.

The rule in one sentence

Particles and forces are not different objects. They are different readings of the same structure.

To understand, imagine a lattice: a grid of points in space, like the crossings on a sheet of squared paper. Between two neighbouring points there is a link, like the side of a square. Each reading assigns three things:

  • to each link of the lattice, a transport of the readings involved. The transport says how a particle changes when it passes from one point to the next. For light it is a phase, that is, an angle of rotation, like the position of a clock's pointer. For the weak force it is two units. For colour it is three readings;
  • to each fermion, that is, to each particle of matter, two things: the number of readings it shares, which is its charge; and the hand it is read with, which is its chirality;
  • to the Higgs field, the projection that links the two hands. This projection is the mass.

Chirality is like the difference between the right hand and the left hand. They are equal but mirror images: they cannot be superimposed. Particles too have a "left-handed" version and a "right-handed" version.

Its parts

The rule has six parts, from R1 to R6. In the third column there is what the translation into physical terms adds to the author's words.

A few words for the table. A matrix is a table of numbers that transforms another list of numbers. Unitary means that it preserves lengths, like a rotation. SU(3), SU(2), U(1) are the names of the symmetry groups of the three forces: strong, weak and electromagnetic. det U = 1 is a condition on the determinant of the matrix: it is what separates SU(3) from U(3). A triplet is a group of three states that transform together, like the three colours of a quark. A singlet is a state on its own. The doublet is a pair, like electron and neutrino for the weak force.

4. The map, piece by piece

Now let us look at the pieces of the Standard Model one at a time. For each piece there are the same entries: the data, the reading, the check, whether it is compatible, who had already done it, what is not read. The labels in brackets (such as P2a or G1) are the names of the registered checks. They help to find them again in the table of section 6.

4.1 The charges of one family (registered checks P2a–P2c)

  • Data. The charges of the particles are 0, ±1/3, ±2/3, ±1. The neutron and the hydrogen atom are neutral. For example the u quark has charge 2/3, the d quark −1/3, the electron −1.
  • Reading. R1 + R2, that is, rule R-Q: the charge counts the shared readings, divided by 3.
  • Check. The forces of the theory must be anomaly-free. Here an anomaly is a defect that appears when going from classical to quantum physics: if it hits the symmetries of the forces, the theory is no longer consistent. Let us take the anomaly-free family with a right-handed neutrino ν_R and Dirac masses (MM 2.38). There the charges depend on a free number: the hypercharge Y_Q of the quark doublet. The formulas are: q_u = Y_Q + 1/2, q_d = Y_Q − 1/2, q_ν = 1/2 − 3Y_Q, q_e = −1/2 − 3Y_Q. The four anomalies vanish for every value of Y_Q. Rule R-Q instead admits exactly two values: Y_Q ∈ {−1/6, +1/6}. To find them, all fractions with denominator up to 60, between −3 and 3, were tried. In addition there is an exact solution.
  • Y_Q = +1/6 is the Standard Model: neutrino 0, electron −1, u 2/3, d −1/3, neutron 0, hydrogen 0.
  • Y_Q = −1/6 is the same content, with two changes. One takes Q → −Q. And one exchanges the two members of every doublet: u ↔ d, ν ↔ e, with their right-handed partners, the Higgs with its conjugate and the Yukawa couplings. The colour does not change. This was checked afterwards. The registered form of the relabelling (Q → −Q with 3 ↔ 3̄) was wrong.
  • Without ν_R the rule adds nothing. The anomalies already fix Y_Q = 1/6, and the rule admits it.
  • Compatible: yes. With ν_R the rule fixes the charge where the anomalies alone leave it free.
  • Place already taken. Pati–Salam and SO(10) also fix the charges with ν_R. And Furey (2016) obtains the charge pattern from a number operator, on the same space of 8 states. R-Q is not a derivation of those models. It is an added rule that contains the distinction between quarks and leptons.
  • Not read. The value of the fine-structure constant, that is, the strength of the electromagnetic force. The Yukawa couplings.

4.2 The algebra of the three readings (registered check T-A)

The 8 states of R1 can be described with three switches, each on or off. Mathematically one uses three operators ε_i (i = 1, 2, 3) that turn a switch on or off. The space of all the states is called a three-mode Fock space. It is the exterior algebra of C³. It is the same construction as in phase 2.29 of the MM 2.27 deposit and in Furey (2016). Here are the five checks.

  • A1. The operators obey the basic rules of fermions (canonical anticommutators). Exact: error 0.
  • A2. The possible values of the number operator N are {0, 1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3}. So N/3 gives 0, 1/3 (three times), 2/3 (three times), 1. These are exactly the charges. A technical note. In the archived convention, Σ ε_i†ε_i counts the empty readings (3 − Σb), and N_shared = Σ ε_i ε_i†. The reviewer noted that in the log the labels of the sectors N = 1 and N = 2 are swapped with respect to the operator. The spectrum and the Casimir values do not change.
  • A3. There are nine combinations ε_i†ε_j. They commute with N: that is, they do not change the charge. Their traceless part generates su(3), the algebra of colour. The Casimir is a number that characterises a group of states. It is 4/3 on the sectors with one and two readings (two conjugate triplets) and 0 on the other two.
  • A4. The spin generators of phase 2.29 are S_i = −(i/2) C_j C_k. They do not commute with N (‖[S_i, N]‖ = 1). Stated conditionally: these spin generators cannot act on the internal index with this charge. A representation is needed in which spin and charges commute. Usually separate factors are used.
  • A5. The fifteen products C_iC_j, K_iK_j, C_iK_j conserve the parity of N, that is, whether N is even or odd. They generate an algebra of dimension 15. It is irreducible on the even and odd sectors (1 + 3 and 3 + 1). Correction (by the reviewer, rechecked). These raw products generate so(3,3) ≅ sl(4,R), a non-compact form. With the Hermitian generators (C_i, iK_i) the algebra is instead the compact su(4), of dimension 15, with 4 → 3 + 1. This is the Pati–Salam pattern: the lepton as a fourth colour. It is not the full Pati–Salam model. The weak factors, the identification of the physical generators and the dynamics are missing.

4.3 Three colours, three families (registered check T-B; data only, low weight)

  • Data. One measures how many light neutrinos couple to the Z particle: N_ν = 2.9963 ± 0.0074. It is 0.5σ from 3. The symbol σ is the standard deviation: 0.5σ means very close. There is also R_ℓ = Γ(hadrons)/Γ(leptons) at the Z. It is the ratio between how many times the Z decays into hadrons and how many into leptons. It is roughly proportional to the number of colours. With 2 or 4 colours it would change by about 33 %.
  • Reading. R3: the same triple read from two sides.
  • Compatible: yes, with low weight, as registered. The number three appears everywhere in MM: three directions, eight states = 2³, rank 3 of the book's decomposition (which holds when the prime supports are disjoint). So a rule that gives 3 is not surprising. The figure 1/3 in the registration is an example with an assumed distribution. It is not a significance. Then N_ν counts only the light active neutrinos coupled to the Z. It does not count all possible families.

4.4 The gluons (registered checks G1–G3)

  • Data. There are eight massless gluons that interact with each other. There is no ninth long-range force coupled to baryon number (baryon number counts protons and neutrons).
  • Reading. The photon method of MM 2.27 is used, that is, a phase on the link. But with the transport of the three readings (R1). On each link there is a 3×3 matrix, U = exp(iA). The plaquette action S = Σ_p Re Tr(1 − U_p) is used. A plaquette is the small square of the lattice: one goes around its four sides. Static fields, that is, fields at rest, are used on a periodic 6³ lattice: a cube of 6 points per side that closes on itself.
  • Check.
  • G1. Three wavevectors are tried: (1,0,0), (1,1,0), (1,2,3). A wavevector says in which direction a wave goes and how closely packed it is. In all three the static quadratic form has 16 complex transverse amplitudes for su(3). That is 8 × 2: eight gluons, each with two modes. (The program prints half the rank of the real sine/cosine form: 32.)
  • G3. With u(3) instead of su(3) one finds 18. The ninth gluon appears only without det = 1.
  • G2. One looks for a cubic term in S − S₂, for a generic su(3) configuration whose pieces do not commute. The cubic term is the sign that the gluons interact with each other. One finds c₃ = −39.0 from a fit c₃ + c₄ε, with ε down to 1e-4 (that is, 0.0001). This recheck was done afterwards. For fields that commute the cubic term vanishes. The compact plaquette action instead keeps higher even powers. The registered criterion was not met. It asked for a ratio constant within 5 % between ε = 1e-2 and 1e-3. But at ε = 1e-2 the quartic term weighed as much as the cubic one.
  • Compatible: yes. But be careful: these are transverse amplitudes of the static quadratic form. They are not verified counts of propagating polarisations. And c₃ is a coefficient of that configuration. det = 1 is the added hypothesis of R1. The central U(1) of U(3) is not automatically baryon number.
  • Place already taken. Wilson (1974): with matrices on the links, Yang–Mills theory follows by construction. The MM part is only the meaning of the link (three readings) and the reason offered for det = 1. Confinement (quarks are never seen alone) and the running of α_s are not redone here.

4.5 The electroweak bosons: two units on the link (registered checks E1–E4)

  • Data. There are four bosons: W⁺, W⁻, Z and γ (the photon). The W have charge ±e. The triple and quartic couplings between bosons were measured at the LEP 2 accelerator. They are those of a non-abelian theory, that is, one where the order of the operations matters. M_W = 80.369 GeV, M_Z = 91.188 GeV. The photon has no mass. Then ρ = 1 at lowest order. The number ρ compares the masses of the W and the Z with the weak angle: ρ = 1 means that they are linked in the simplest way.
  • Reading. R4 + R5. On the link there is a U(2) transport. And there is a Higgs doublet frozen at its vacuum (0, v/√2), in unitary gauge. Unitary gauge is a convenient way to choose the internal coordinates.
  • Check.
  • E1. In each of the three tested wavevectors there are 8 complex transverse amplitudes: 4 bosons × 2.
  • E2. The registered statement on the cubic term is supported in two ways. First: the calculation with a generic field gives c₃ = −29.4. Second: for fields that commute there is no cubic term. For W3 and B only, (S − S₂)/ε³ goes to zero like ε: −0.77, −0.23, −0.06. The absence is also proved by hand. With fields that commute the plaquette is exp(iεH_p), with H_p Hermitian and independent of ε. The real part of its trace contains only even powers of ε. The original program said "not met". But it used a fit criterion that is not in the registration: an intercept of 0.0144 against a tolerance of 1e-3. A possible explanation of the residual is the loss of numerical precision in the subtraction. No comparison at higher precision is supplied here.
  • E3. The registration gives the squares of the physical masses: 0, g²v²/4 (twice) and (g² + g′²)v²/4. The program built the matrix K of the quadratic form c^T K c. Its eigenvalues are 0, 3201.7 (twice) and 4164.7 GeV². The program compared them directly with the predicted physical values. But for real fields normalised in the standard way M²_phys = 2K holds. The original automatic comparison forgot a factor of two, and so it said "not met". The original program also printed ρ = 2. It came from a wrong formula: in the numerator there was the predicted physical M_W², in the denominator the computed eigenvalue K_Z. A common rescaling of the two masses would instead leave ρ unchanged. The subsequent calculation uses consistent normalisations. It recovers the registered values: 0, 6403.44 (twice) and 8329.35 GeV². And ρ = 1.000000000000. It is an identity at tree level, that is, at the first level of calculation, the simplest one. The twelve digits describe the numerical check, not an experimental precision. The original program also checked the direction of the photon, W3/B = tan θ, and the charge of the W: +1 in units of the photon coupling. One chooses g = 0.65. This gives M_W = 80.022 GeV and M_Z = 91.265 GeV.
  • E4. M_W/M_Z = cos θ holds. With the MS-bar value sin²θ = 0.23122 it gives 0.8768, against 0.8814 measured (−0.52 %). It was registered in advance as the known difference between two conventions, MS-bar and on-shell. It was not a test of MM.
  • Compatible: yes, for some selected identities of the bosons. With a U(2) representation of the link the checks reproduce:
  • four bosons;
  • a cubic self-interaction for fields that do not commute;
  • a massless photon;
  • massive W and Z, with ρ = 1 at tree level, and W charge ±1.

This is not yet an assembled electroweak model. Two things are missing. The normalisation of the two kinetic terms, which fixes g and g′ independently. And the action on the representations of the fermions.

  • Place already taken. The Glashow–Weinberg–Salam model on a lattice: with 2×2 matrices on the links and a doublet, all this follows by construction. The MM part is the meaning of the two states.
  • Not read. θ_W (the ratio g′/g is taken from the data), v, and which hand the weak force acts on. The ratio between the two units might one day fix g′/g at some scale. No such rule was written in advance. And none is sought afterwards.

4.6 One light hand (registered checks O1–O5, H1–H2)

This is the hardest part. Let us go step by step.

  • Data.
  • The charged weak current acts on left-handed fermions. The muon decay parameters say so (PDG 2025, review 57, table 57.1). The 90 % limits are: |g^V_LL| > 0.960, |g^V_RR| < 0.017, |g^V_LR| < 0.023, |g^V_RL| < 0.105. In words: the "left-left" coefficient is large, the ones that involve the right hand are very small.
  • These data must be read in a precise framework: four fermions, local interaction, without derivatives, with lepton number conserved. In that framework the measurements of muon decay and of inverse muon decay support the V−A charged interaction of the leptons, within the errors. The helicity −1 of the neutrino ν_μ in pion decay also supports it. These limits concern specific operators and low-energy processes.
  • No light "mirror" fermions with the same weak charges are observed. For the charged fermions of the Standard Model, the Z couples both to the left hand and to the right hand. A ν_R that does not feel the electroweak force does not have this coupling.
  • The obstacle. Putting particles on a lattice has a famous problem: fermion doubling. On a lattice every particle carries along some unwanted twins, like an echo. The Nielsen–Ninomiya theorem says that, under certain assumptions, this always happens. The construction called overlap uses a special form of the symmetry between the two hands: the Ginsparg–Wilson form. Section 4.7 shows what doubling means for the checkerboard of MM 2.40.
  • Reading. It is R6, implemented as a standard overlap operator. It is built with the matrices and parameters of MM 2.40. The Dirac matrices come from the checkerboard. The Wilson parameter is r = 0.15 and the level is m₀ = 0.2 (the mass of the 2.40 domain wall). Time is treated as a fourth direction of space (Euclidean). In formulas: D_W(k) = i Σ γ_μ sin k_μ + r Σ (1 − cos k_μ), A = m₀ − D_W, D = 1 − A(A†A)^(−1/2). Alongside, the standard set (r = 1, m₀ = 1) is also run, for comparison. It is not shown that this Euclidean operator is the limit of the 2.40 tick automaton.
  • Check.
  • O1. The Ginsparg–Wilson relation γ5D + Dγ5 = Dγ5D holds to 9e-16 for both sets. This is the machine precision.
  • O2. In the free field D has a single zero, at k = 0. At the other 15 corners D = 2. A zero corresponds to a light particle. So there is only one light particle, and the 15 twins are heavy. Outside |k| < 0.3, the smallest singular value on a 16⁴ grid is 1.056 (for 2.40) and 0.390 (standard).
  • O3. With γ̂5 = γ5(1 − D), the Weyl operator P₊ D P̂₋ has a cone at k = 0 and a gap elsewhere. The cone has slope 1/m₀ = 5 for 2.40. A gap is a jump in energy: it means that there are no light particles there.
  • O4, locality. One looks at how much the operator "feels" distant points. The norm of the kernel f(r) at Manhattan distance r decreases. The Manhattan distance is counted as in a city of blocks: only along the streets, not diagonally. The fitted effective slopes are 0.4054 over distances 4–14 (2.40) and 0.8049 over distances 2–8 (standard). Both calculations used 48⁴ sites instead of the registered 32⁴. The registered range for 2.40 was 0.1–0.4. So O4 was not met for 2.40, by a small margin. For the standard set the numerical criterion is met, on the modified lattice.

Be careful: the fitted slopes are not the asymptotic decay rates, that is, at very large distance. The 2.40 kernel has an axial complex-momentum singularity at k = (π + iκ, 0, 0, 0), with κ ≈ 0.101. It has a diagonal one at k = (π + iη, iη, iη, iη), with η ≈ 0.050. The reviewer found them; both were rechecked. So the asymptotic rate in the Manhattan distance used in O4 is not established here. The operator is local with exponential tails, not ultralocal. The earlier working phrase "the twin fades over 2.5 cells" is withdrawn.

  • O5, the index with the light field. The lattice is 6⁴, with constant U(1) fluxes ('t Hooft). The index counts the difference between left and right zero modes. The topological charge Q counts how many times the field "twists". The calibrated index is equal to Q for Q = 0, 1, 2, −1, 0, both for 2.40 and for the standard set. The gap of H_W stays open (≥ 0.0999 and ≥ 0.74). For 2.40 the registered prediction was a mismatch at L = 6. The reason: the flux per plaquette (0.17) exceeds the margin 2r − m₀ = 0.1. The anticipated mismatch was not observed in the five backgrounds tested. No general conclusion on reliability follows from this.
  • Index convention. With H_W = γ5(m₀ − D_W) the identity n₊ − n₋ = ½ Tr sign(H_W) holds. The first program counts the exact zero modes as n₋ − n₊. After the first background, a second, faster program uses the spectral asymmetry. The two methods agree on the first background, Q = 0, where the sign is not tested. The sign convention is calibrated separately, at Q = 1, as registered. So the index is not an independent prediction. The fast method does not exclude extra pairs of zero modes with opposite chirality.
  • H1, the "hill". One counts the number of light species and their net chirality, as m₀/r varies. The net chirality is the coefficient of the index after the Weyl projection. For m₀/r = −0.5, 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 one finds: 0/0, 1/+1, 5/−3, 11/+3, 15/−1, 16/0. This holds for r = 1 and for r = 0.15.
  • H2. With Q = 1 the index is 1, −3, 3, −1, 0 for m₀ = 1, 3, 5, 7, 9.
  • Compatible: yes, in a window. Imagine the spectrum of the lattice as a landscape. At k = 0 there is the bottom of a valley. Further away there are ridges and peaks. The level m₀ must lie above the bottom of the valley and below the first ridge: 0 < m₀ < 2r. The 2.40 values (0.2 < 0.3) are inside. Above the top (m₀ > 8r) all the twins return and the net chirality cancels. In the Ginsparg–Wilson form the projector of a field depends on the operator itself: P̂ = (1 − γ5(1 − D))/2. So the difference between the hands sits in the reading operator. This is R6 inside a known object. It is a compatibility, not a derivation.
  • Precisely what is shown. The overlap operator has one light Dirac species, with both chiralities. A Weyl sector, that is, one single hand, is selected with a projection. These checks do not construct the fermion measure of a chiral gauge theory. They do not construct it even for U(1). And here everything is abelian. Constructions of the electroweak model on the lattice exist, non-perturbative, with stated restrictions (Kadoh–Kikukawa 2008). Implementing one of them is outside this work.
  • Place already taken. Neuberger (1998), Hernández–Jansen–Lüscher (1999), Kaplan (1992), Shamir (1993). It is already known, in the overlap literature, that the number of species and the index depend on m₀.
  • A side check on pion decay. The pion can decay into electron and neutrino, or into muon and neutrino. Let us take massless neutrinos and V/A couplings equal for all leptons. At tree level the ratio Γ(π→eν)/Γ(π→μν) is 1.2833·10⁻⁴. It does not depend on the mixture of the hands. The measured value, 1.230·10⁻⁴, is consistent with this helicity suppression. The radiative corrections of the Standard Model account for a reduction of roughly four percent. They are not computed here. A pion–lepton coupling equal for all leptons, without derivatives and without an additional lepton-mass factor, would give 5.487. The ratio does not select a hand. An earlier working note read the ratio as evidence for one hand. That reading is corrected here. The evidence that the charged current is left-handed is the muon decay data, seen above.
  • Would be incompatible if:
  • an established right-handed charged-current contribution were found, incompatible with the specified chiral interaction;
  • a light mirror sector with the stated charges were found;
  • the non-locality of the reading operator could be observed at long range.

4.7 The obstacle on the 2.40 checkerboard: winding (registered checks T1–T6)

The 2.40 checkerboard is an automaton: at each tick each particle takes one step, along x, then y, then z, in turn. For the part with only one hand (the Weyl sector) this gives a map U: T³ → SU(2). The winding number W of a map says how many times the map "wraps around" its target. Think of a rubber band wound around a finger: once, twice, no times. The formula is W = −(1/24π²)∫ ε_ijk Tr[R_i R_j R_k] d³k, with R_i = U†∂_iU.

For SU(2), when ±I are regular values, W is equal to the sum of the node chiralities. The nodes are the points where light particles appear. The chiralities are defined from the local effective Hamiltonian. They are counted at quasi-energy 0 and, separately, at π, with a consistent sign convention. The registered definition used the sign of the Jacobian of the vector part u of U = u₀I − i u·σ. It agrees at quasi-energy 0. But at π it has the opposite sign, because there the local effective field is −u (the reviewer noted this).

  • T2. W = 0 for MM 2.40 (−2e-16 on 48³ and 96³ grids).
  • T3. W = 0 for 200 random automata made of single-direction steps in SU(2). The largest |W| is 1.2e-14, that is, zero. This is also proved: each step is a loop in SU(2), and π₁(SU(2)) = 0. This means that every loop in SU(2) can be shrunk to a point.
  • T3b. The Weyl automaton of D'Ariano–Perinotti is exactly a product of three single-direction steps: A± = e^{−iσ_z q_z} e^{±iσ_y q_y} e^{−iσ_x q_x}. So W = 0.
  • T4 (control). The model of Higashikawa and colleagues gives W = +1.00000, with the sign convention that reproduces their dispersion. W = 1 is met. The unrestricted registered statement "a single gapless point" is not met. Uniqueness holds at quasi-energy zero. But degeneracies also appear at π, on the faces of the zone.
  • T5a. Partial steps: one component of the spin moves, the other waits. With three steps: W = 0 in 64 cases out of 64.
  • T5b and T5c were not met. The registered prediction was some W ≠ 0 with 4–8 partial steps. But all 236 cases had W = 0. On the doubled cell the model of Higashikawa gives +1 − 1 = 0, not 2. Both facts are explained afterwards by an identity: Tr[((gh)⁻¹d(gh))³] = Tr(g⁻¹dg)³ + Tr(h⁻¹dh)³ − 3 d Tr(g⁻¹dg ∧ dh h⁻¹). So W(gh) = W(g) + W(h) for regular single-valued maps on the torus. In words: the winding of a product is the sum of the windings.
  • Consequence. Let us take a finite product of steps. Each step is periodic on the same torus and depends on only one component of k. The "coins", that is, the mixing rules, are constant. Then W = 0 in U(n). The conditions matter. The coins must be constant (or have a known W). Each factor must be periodic, not only the product. Higashikawa's half steps are not single-valued on the cell.
  • What it means. In the checkerboard with separate steps, the Weyl sector does not realise an isolated hand in the bulk. To obtain one single hand one must state which further conditions or sectors enter. Section 4.6 does this with the reading level. The earlier working phrase "the opposite hand can only be moved" is withdrawn. Corners, bands and walls are different mechanisms, each with its own assumptions.
  • T1c. With the Wilson term r = 0.15 the registration expected one light species and 15 heavy species with gap ≥ 0.1. The single light species is confirmed. The other valleys have a gap ≥ 0.160 (grid 40³). But the valleys are 64, not 16 (section 7). The original search for minima was incomplete.
  • T6, dispersion. The dispersion says how the energy of a wave depends on its wavelength. The registration concerned the leading order of the group-velocity deviation. The program instead computed the relative deviation of the energy. The leading orders are supported. The coefficients were corrected by hand afterwards:
  • with one tick (2.40 without the palindrome, and D'Ariano–Perinotti) the relative energy shift is linear in κ, with coefficient n_x n_y n_z and opposite sign for the two helicities;
  • with the palindrome and with the 2.27 photon there are only quadratic shifts. For the photon, with k = κn, the relative deviation of the energy is −(κ²/24) Σ n_i⁴. The radial deviation of the group velocity is −(κ²/8) Σ n_i⁴. The registration had written 1/24 for the velocity (checked afterwards).

The registration also pointed to some observable consequences: a linear term at the Planck scale, to be compared with the limits on Lorentz violation. These consequences are not verified here. The comparison would require fixing in advance the operator, the sector and the physical step.

4.8 The Higgs sector (readings compared with the data; no new code)

The Higgs boson is the particle linked to the field that gives mass to the other particles.

  • Couplings and mass. The measured Higgs couplings are consistent with the Standard Model pattern (PDG 2025). At tree level, the couplings with the fermions go as m_f/v. Those with W and Z go as m_V²/v. The couplings with the photon and the gluons pass through loops, that is, through virtual particles. R5 reads the mass of the fermion and its coupling to the Higgs as the same projection seen twice. Compatible. Two limits apply:
  • "one scale for all masses" holds only in the electroweak sector. The mass of the nucleon comes from the strong force (QCD);
  • "no right hand, no mass" holds for Dirac masses. Majorana terms also exist.
  • The Higgs mass as self-coupling. In the Standard Model m_H² = 2λv², with λ = 0.129 at first order. The trilinear coupling of the Higgs with itself is measured at −1.2 < κ₃ < 7.2 (ATLAS, 95 %). The Standard Model gives 1. Compatible by structure. Today's data do not allow a distinction. A κ₃ different from 1 would not by itself contradict the reading.
  • λ = 0 at the Planck scale. It is an additional condition, not contained in the premises. From MM 2.38: λ(M_P) = −0.0090 ± 0.0052 with the pole top mass (1.7σ from 0). Or −0.0100 ± 0.0031 with the direct top mass (3.2σ). The place is taken: Shaposhnikov–Wetterich (2010).
  • Numerology declared in advance and not used. Two coincidences had been noticed before: m_H/v = 0.5085 against 1/2, and λ = 0.1293 against 1/8. They are not used. As an exact relation at first order, m_H = v/2 is excluded: it would give 123.11 GeV against 125.20 ± 0.11 GeV measured.
  • v and the history of the cosmos. Could v have changed over time?
  • Methanol at z = 0.89, that is, 7.5 billion years ago: Δμ/μ = (−1.0 ± 0.8_stat ± 1.0_sys)·10⁻⁷, with μ = m_p/m_e (Bagdonaite and colleagues 2013).
  • Nucleosynthesis, in the first minutes of the universe: |δv/v| ≲ 1 %. It is a bound that depends on the model (Burns and colleagues 2024).
  • To go from the bound on μ to a bound on v one needs the relation Δln μ = Δln m_p − Δln y_e − Δln v, with m_p tied to QCD. So the bounds on a variation of v in the cosmos depend on which dimensionless ratios are compared. And they depend on assumptions about the Yukawa couplings and QCD. They refer to the low-temperature vacuum value, not to the thermal history of the electroweak transition.

The reading L8-a takes the low-temperature electroweak scale as a fixed structural parameter. It is compatible with these bounds, but it is not the only possible one. The value v/M_P ≈ 2·10⁻¹⁷ (M_P is the non-reduced Planck mass) is not read.

4.9 SU(2) from the records (registered; working result only; no code in this deposit)

A different route was also tried for the group of the weak force. One looks at what happens when a record is transported along a closed history, which returns to the starting point. The set of these loops forms a group, the holonomy group. The record bench of the 2.34 working programme was used. The registered expectations were not met.

  • With 4 registers: semisimple algebras of dimension 6 (so(4)) or 3 (so(3)), depending on a parameter.
  • With 3 registers: abelian (dimension 1) or trivial.
  • With 5 registers: undetermined.

Algebra, group and force must be kept apart: so(4) ≅ su(2) ⊕ su(2); SO(4) ≅ [SU(2) × SU(2)]/Z₂; SO(3) is not SU(2). No stable SU(2) is read from the records today. The programs depend on unpublished working files of the 2.34 programme and are not included. The registrations and the outcome note are included.

7. Corrections

The corrections are important. They change some earlier readings. This is why they are here, in view.

Corrections to the working notes. The notes remain unchanged in the archive. The later notes and this text prevail.

  • The algebra of the fifteen raw products is so(3,3), not su(4). And the labels of the sectors N = 1 and N = 2 were swapped (4.2).
  • It was said that the overlap escapes the Nielsen–Ninomiya theorem by giving up strict locality. This reading is corrected. The operator is local with exponential tails. The decisive step is the Ginsparg–Wilson form of the symmetry between the hands.
  • The phrases "the twin becomes non-locality" and "the twin fades over 2.5 cells" are withdrawn. The fitted slope is not the asymptotic rate (4.6).
  • The phrase "the opposite hand can only be moved" is withdrawn (4.7).
  • The pion ratio does not select a hand (4.6).
  • The classification of E2 and E3 in the working note is replaced by section 4.5.
  • The phrase "exactly the su(4) of Pati–Salam", in the Tappa 2 note, is replaced by section 4.2.

Correction to MM 2.40.

  • The valleys. The README of MM 2.40 (line 85) says that the massive automaton has 16 species. But the full count over the Brillouin cube [0, 2π)³ is different. The Brillouin cube is the set of all the possible wavevectors on the lattice. Without the Wilson term (θ = 0.01), the two-tick palindromic Dirac automaton has 64 Dirac valleys. They lie at distance θ from their quasi-energy centre: 32 around zero and 32 around π/2. The exact relation is cos Ω = cos 2θ · cos 2k_x · cos 2k_y · cos 2k_z. The two-tick evolution conserves the eight parity classes of the sites. So there are 8 valleys in each parity sector, each one a Dirac fermion. The earlier check had examined 16 of them. With the Wilson term r = 0.15 one single light species remains in the bulk (T1c).
  • The walls. A domain wall is a boundary where particles can stay trapped. Wall states were searched for in the new valleys (registered check T-D). Extra states were found at half period. But the counting method used does not isolate the wall states. It used the trace of a projector with a window of 0.08. So it also caught bulk states. For this reason D1 is reported as not met. A diagnosis made afterwards gives a first picture:
  • with the 2.40 parameters (θ₀ = 0.2 > r = 0.15), part of these states sit on the walls, with a mass ≈ 0.013;
  • with the tested value θ₀ = 0.1 < r, the selected states are mostly bulk states.

"One mode per wall" remains valid as a local statement, near k = 0 and at quasi-energy 0. A corrected text for MM 2.40 requires a better criterion to say where each state sits. It will be given separately. The programs for this are not part of this deposit.

Glossary

  • Algebra, group. Two mathematical ways of describing symmetries. The group contains the transformations; the algebra their "very small" versions. Algebra, group and force must be kept apart.
  • Anomaly. The breaking, in the passage to quantum physics, of a symmetry of classical physics. If it hits the symmetries of the forces (gauge symmetries) it makes the theory inconsistent: there it must be absent.
  • Boson. A particle with integer spin. The photon, the gluons, W and Z carry the forces; the Higgs is also a boson, but it does not carry a force as they do.
  • Chirality, hand. Left or right, like the two hands: equal but mirror images.
  • Fermion. A particle of matter: quarks and leptons.
  • Hill. The Wilson term r Σ(1 − cos k_μ). It is zero at k = 0 (the valley) and has ridges 2rn at the corners with n components equal to π.
  • Index. The difference between left and right zero modes of an operator.
  • Lattice. A grid of points in space, like the crossings on a sheet of squared paper.
  • Link. The segment between two neighbouring points of the lattice.
  • Link transport. The matrix that carries the internal components of a field from one point of the lattice to the next: a phase for light, 2×2 for the weak force, 3×3 for colour.
  • Overlap. A construction on the lattice that keeps only one light particle, with the twins pushed to the scale of the cell, and that preserves on the lattice the symmetry between the hands in the Ginsparg–Wilson form.
  • Plaquette. The small square of the lattice; the plaquette action adds up one contribution for each small square.
  • Premise. A starting point of the author, like an axiom.
  • Reading. In MM, a registered observation. Here, the way in which a force acts on a field: on which components and on which hand.
  • Reading level m₀. The mass parameter of the overlap. The light species are those with Wilson term below m₀.
  • Registered. Written in a file and given a fingerprint before the calculation.
  • Standard Model. The theory of the particles and of the strong, weak and electromagnetic forces.
  • Tree level. The first level of calculation, the simplest one, without virtual particles.
  • Twin, doubler. An extra light species, forced by the lattice (Nielsen–Ninomiya), like an echo of the real particle.
  • Winding W. The number of times a map wraps around its target, like a rubber band around a finger. Technically: the three-dimensional winding number of a map from the Brillouin torus to SU(2) or U(n). For SU(2) ≅ S³ it is the degree of the map. When ±I are regular values it is equal to the sum of the node chiralities, defined from the local effective Hamiltonian, at quasi-energy 0 and, separately, at π.
