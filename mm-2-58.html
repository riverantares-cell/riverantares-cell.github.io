Abstract

Standard big-bang nucleosynthesis, used here as a dictionary of laboratory data, constrains the baryon density ω_b with the deuterium measured in quasar absorbers (and helium, in the primary dictionary). If the dark-matter density is ω_c = 3√3·ω_b, the 13 DESI DR2 BAO distances are read with one cosmological parameter fewer than in flat ΛCDM. No CMB data enter the fits. Results. A premise of the framework on time treats the standard account of the first minutes as a reading in the present. Six translations into measured quantities were registered before computing, and run with PRyMordial (NACRE II rates with LUNA): The predicted lithium stays about 3.5 times the observed value in every reading that holds. Registered test , before the data: the first DESI release beyond DR2, read with the same chain without the CMB. Δχ² ≤ 4 «holds», 4-9 «tension», > 9 «the reading 3√3 does not read BAO + BBN». A secondary block compares a₀ = cH₀/6 with the first g† measured with a total error ≤ 0.06·10⁻¹⁰. An addendum registered before the data reports the same computation with evolving dark energy next to the main block, with a qualifier fixed in advance. Decision programs with tests are included.

Version 2.58 of the Mathematical Materiality (MM) series, October 2026. It continues MM 2.54 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23418156). There the ratio between dark matter and ordinary matter, Ω_c/Ω_b = 3√3, was registered. The data used included the cosmic microwave background (CMB). This work then feeds the chain of MM 2.56 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23506401) on wide binaries.

Before starting: the words you need

The first minutes of the universe. In the first minutes the universe was extremely hot. Protons and neutrons joined together and formed the first light nuclei. This process is called big-bang nucleosynthesis, BBN for short.

  • Deuterium (D) is hydrogen with one more neutron in the nucleus.
  • Helium-4 (⁴He) has two protons and two neutrons.
  • Lithium (Li) is an even heavier nucleus, produced in very small amounts.

How much deuterium formed depends on how much ordinary matter there was. For this reason the deuterium measured today says how much ordinary matter the universe has.

ω_b and ω_c. ω_b measures how much ordinary matter there is in the universe. Ordinary matter is made of baryons. ω_c measures how much cold dark matter there is. Ω_c/Ω_b is their ratio.

The ratio 3√3. In this work it is imposed that dark matter is 3√3 times ordinary matter. As a formula: ω_c = 3√3·ω_b = 5.196·ω_b. In standard cosmology, ΛCDM, ω_c is instead free.

The BAO and the sound horizon. In the young universe matter vibrated like a gas crossed by sound waves. These waves left a typical distance in the distribution of galaxies: the baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO). The distance that the waves travelled up to the drag epoch, when ordinary matter separated from light, is called the sound horizon, r_d. It acts as a cosmic ruler. DESI is a large survey of galaxies that measures these distances.

The Hubble constant, H₀. It says how fast the universe is expanding today. It is measured in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹: kilometres per second for each megaparsec of distance. The measurements do not agree with each other. From the background radiation (Planck) comes 67.36. From nearby stars (Riess and colleagues) comes 73.04.

a₀. It is the acceleration scale of galaxies, in the radial acceleration relation. That relation links the acceleration observed in galaxies to the acceleration of the visible matter alone. Here we read a₀ = cH₀/6, where c is the speed of light.

The χ², the σ and the two z. The χ² (read chi-squared) is an error score. The lower it is, the better a model follows the data. Δχ² is the difference between two models. σ is the typical error. Careful: in this text the letter z has two meanings.

  • As redshift, z says how far away a galaxy is. The larger z is, the farther away and the further back in time we look.
  • As a z score, it says by how many σ a value is distant from another. For example z = −0.38 means 0.38 typical errors below.

The dictionary. BBN is used here as a dictionary. It translates the numbers measured in the laboratory into cosmological quantities. The laboratory numbers are the nuclear reactions, the neutron lifetime and others.

The prior. It is a piece of information given to a calculation at the start. For example: ω_b is about 0.02218, with a certain error.

In short

The chain in five steps.

  • Standard nucleosynthesis, with the measured deuterium (and helium, in the primary dictionary), constrains ω_b.
  • ω_c = 3√3·ω_b is imposed. Then the 13 BAO distances of DESI DR2 are read with one cosmological parameter fewer than in the flat ΛCDM model.
  • The fit costs only Δχ² = 0.09. It gives H₀ = 68.52 ± 0.59 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹, the value already known from BAO + BBN.
  • Then a₀ = cH₀/6 = 1.110·10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². It is at −0.4σ from the value of SPARC, a collection of nearby galaxies.
  • The other way round: the BAO alone, with 3√3, predict D/H = (2.40 ± 0.31)·10⁻⁵. The measured value is 2.51. It is a predictive check, but not independent of the ratio test.

A test on the first DESI release after DR2 is registered here, before those data come out.

What it is, and what it is not. It is a consistency check of one reading: 3√3, chosen knowing the data. It is not a derivation. Its outcome concerns that reading, not the MM framework.

1. The chain

  1. Deuterium → ω_b. Standard nucleosynthesis (BBN) acts as a dictionary. It is a function of laboratory data. First of all the nuclear cross sections, that is, how easy it is for two nuclei to react. Among these is the reaction d(p,γ)³He measured by LUNA. Then the neutron lifetime, G, N_eff and T_γ. The primary dictionary uses D and ⁴He. A dictionary with deuterium alone (D-4) serves as a check.
  2. × 3√3 → ω_c. ω_c = 3√3·ω_b = 5.196·ω_b. In ΛCDM ω_c would be free.
  3. → the sound horizon r_d and H₀. With ω_b and ω_c fixed, r_d is fixed (the CLASS program computes it). The DESI DR2 BAO then leave free a single cosmological parameter, H₀. ω_b remains a fitted parameter, constrained by the BBN prior.
  4. → a₀ = cH₀/6 → galaxies (SPARC) and wide binaries (the chain of MM 2.56).

No CMB data enter the fits. Only one Level 2 check (T4, section 5) compares the result with Planck, afterwards.

How to read some rows.

  • D/H is the number of deuterium nuclei for each hydrogen nucleus. 10⁵ D/H = 2.527 means that there are 2.527 deuterium nuclei for every hundred thousand hydrogen nuclei. It is measured in the light of distant gas clouds poor in stars (12 systems).
  • Y_p is the mass fraction of helium-4 produced in the first minutes: about 0.245.
  • The neutron lifetime is measured in two ways. In the bottle method the neutrons are shut in a container and one counts how many remain. In the beam method a beam of neutrons in flight is observed. The two methods give different values: for example 878.3 s with the bottle and 887.7 s with the beam.
  • H₀ for comparison: Planck gives 67.36; Riess and colleagues 73.04; Breuval and colleagues 73.17.

3. The method (Level 1)

The model.

  • Flat ΛCDM, with N_eff = 3.044: there is one neutrino with a mass of 0.06 eV. The temperature of the background radiation is T_γ = 2.7255 K.
  • The sound horizon r_d is computed by the CLASS 3.4.1 program on a grid of values of (ω_b, ω_c). Between the grid points it is interpolated. The distances are computed with an integrator of our own.

Two fits. The score is χ² = χ²_BAO + ((ω_b − ω_b,D)/σ_D)²: 14 terms in all.

  • ΛCDM: ω_b, ω_c and h free (11 degrees of freedom).
  • MM: ω_c = 3√3·ω_b, with ω_b and h free (12 degrees of freedom).

The priors.

  • Flat: h ∈ [0.55, 0.85], ω_c ∈ [0.085, 0.155].
  • ω_b ∈ [0.0196, 0.0249]. It is the edge of the r_d grid, at least 4.7σ from the centre of D-1.
  • The distributions of the possible values come from a Metropolis sampler with 4 chains. The convergence checks are good (R̂ ≤ 1.0007 also on the split chains, ESS ≥ 11,800).

The dictionaries, all fixed in advance.

  • D-1, primary.
  • D-2, the same with Cooke's D/H.
  • D-3, PDG 2026.
  • D-4, deuterium only: the PDG 2026 D/H through the PRyMordial table, with the ln V term of the Gaussian likelihood.
  • D-5, Cooke 2018 before LUNA (historical).

The validations, all passed.

  • The interpolation of r_d agrees with CLASS within 0.00003 Mpc, and the distances within 1.3·10⁻⁶.
  • The BAO alone give Ω_m = 0.2975 ± 0.0087 and h·r_d = 101.53 ± 0.74. DESI, in its equation 17, gives 0.2975 ± 0.0086 and 101.54 ± 0.73.
  • ΛCDM + BBN gives H₀ = 68.55 ± 0.60. DESI, in its equation 19, gives 68.51 ± 0.58.

How to read it.

  • The cost of the ratio. With the ratio 3√3 the score rises only from 10.282 to 10.369: Δχ² = 0.087. The registered zone for this value is compatible within 1σ. There are also the ΔAIC and ΔBIC criteria, which reward models with fewer parameters. They are negative: they slightly favour the model with the fixed ratio.
  • The ratio left free. In ΛCDM it comes out at 5.282 ± 0.228: at +0.38σ from 3√3.
  • H₀. With the fixed ratio it comes out at 68.52 ± 0.59. In ΛCDM it comes out at 68.56 ± 0.60: almost the same.
  • a₀. It comes out at (1.110 ± 0.010)·10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². Compared with SPARC, z = −0.38. With H₀ = 73.04 it would be z = −0.07.
  • Helium. Y_p comes out at 0.2468, at +0.6σ from the measurements. With the D-1 dictionary it is not an independent check, because D-1 already uses helium.
  • Deuterium only (D-4). Δχ² = 0.023, H₀ = 68.72 ± 0.60, Y_p = 0.2469. Here helium becomes an independent check, and it works out (+0.6σ).
  • The sensitivity. Changing dictionary (from D-1 to D-5), Δχ² goes from 0.02 to 0.42 and H₀ from 67.99 to 68.72.

The inverse reading.

  • In ΛCDM the BAO alone cannot fix ω_b. With ω_c = 3√3·ω_b, instead, they can.
  • The result is ω_b = 0.02316 ± 0.00178 and H₀ = 69.4 ± 1.7, without any information from BBN.
  • Through the standard dictionary this predicts D/H = (2.40 ± 0.31)·10⁻⁵.
  • The measured value is 2.508 ± 0.029: z = −0.34.
  • D/H does not enter the BAO fit. But the check uses the same BAO and the same constraint as the Ω_c/Ω_b test. So it is not independent of it.

What the numbers mean.

  • The chain is consistent: the BAO are read as well as in ΛCDM, with one parameter fewer.
  • H₀ is the BAO + BBN value already known: it is compatibility, not a new number.
  • Today the constraint does not narrow H₀ (0.59 against 0.60), because the data already sit close to the ratio.
  • a₀ = cH₀/6 is consistent with SPARC, but not decisive. The systematic error of SPARC, 20 %, also allows H₀ = 73.
  • The wide-binary chain of MM 2.56 uses H₀ = 67.4. The difference of 1.1 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ lies inside the sensitivity range declared there (67.4-73.04). The protocol of 2.56 does not change.

5. Readings of a premise on time (Level 2)

The premise. A premise of the MM framework treats the standard account of the first minutes as a reading made in the present. In this reading there is a single common time for all processes, and no objective beginning.

The translations. The premise was translated in six ways, into quantities that can be measured. The translations were registered before the calculations. The PRyMordial program was run with the NACRE II rates and with LUNA. It used the full network of 63 nuclear reactions.

What each row says.

  • T0, a common clock. The rates of all processes are counted in a single unit of time. The abundances are ratios of counts, and the dictionary does not change. Deuterium and helium are read well. Lithium is not.
  • T1, the same everywhere. Deuterium is measured in 12 distant gas clouds. The clouds lie in different directions and at different distances (z_abs from 2.4 to 3.6). They also have different amounts of heavy elements. There is no significant variation, at the sensitivity of these tests. The check on the dipole (a difference between two sides of the sky) says little: 9 lines of sight out of 12 lie in the same area of the sky, between 9h and 16h.
  • T2a, expansion rate equal to the local rate. The extra radiation, ΔN_eff, is left free. It comes out compatible with S = 1, that is, with no difference.
  • T2b, a hidden mirror sector at the same temperature, with gravitating radiation. It is a copy world of ours, with its own light, which weighs like ours. It would add a lot of radiation. Before the e⁺e⁻ annihilation one would have ΔN_eq = 6.14, after it 7.44. The annihilation is the moment when electrons and positrons turned into light. Deuterium would come out at +10/+11σ and helium at +20/+23σ. This reading does not hold, in the two cases examined. The full thermal history of the mirror sector is not simulated. The outcome agrees with the assumption of MM 2.44: hidden radiation does not gravitate.
  • T3, the neutron lifetime. Three values are tried: bottle (878.3 s), beam (888.0 s) and J-PARC (877.2 s). Deuterium changes little. There is a weak hint in favour of the bottle value (Δχ² = 1.2). Helium prefers τ_n = 870 ± 15 s.
  • T4, a single baryon-to-photon ratio today. ω_b is obtained from deuterium alone: 0.02242 ± 0.00056. It is compared with the CMB value: 0.02230 ± 0.00021. They are compatible: z = +0.20.

Lithium.

  • In every reading that holds, the predicted lithium is about 3.5 times the measured one. Lithium is measured in the old stars of the halo of the Galaxy, poor in metals.
  • The difference is 4.3-4.6σ, where the log computes z, with a theoretical error of 15 %.
  • No translation was built on lithium, and none reads it.
  • It remains a measured fact to look at. The PDG treats the value measured in stars, strictly, as a lower bound.

What changes for the chain: nothing. The translations that hold leave the dictionary of Level 1 as it is. T2b does not hold in the cases examined. T3 leaves the neutron lifetime open, with a weak hint for the value already used.

5b. The same chain with a dark energy that changes over time

6. The registered prediction

The time limit for the targets is 20 October 2026, at 18:56:04 UTC. The prediction is written before the data.

Block D (main): DESI beyond DR2 + BBN, without CMB. This combination is not covered by the prediction of MM 2.54, which uses CMB + BAO.

  • The target. The first paper of the DESI collaboration on arXiv after the time limit. It must have BAO beyond DR2 and public Gaussian likelihood files. A check on arXiv just before registration found none.
  • Data and chain. The same chain, frozen; dictionary D-1.
  • Validity. W1: the BAO-only Ω_m and h·r_d of the paper. W2: its DESI+BBN H₀. The outcome is suspended in three cases: at the edges of the priors, with chains that do not converge, with a negative Δχ².
  • The rule. Δχ² ≤ 4: "the chain holds". From 4 to 9: "tension". Above 9: "the reading ω_c = 3√3·ω_b does not read BAO + BBN".
  • Expiry. The block expires on 31 December 2028.

Block A (secondary): a₀ with independent calibrations.

  • The target. The first paper that measures g† of the radial acceleration relation, with the function of McGaugh and colleagues 2016. The total error must be ≤ 0.06·10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻².
  • The rule. z is computed against the a₀ = cH₀/6 of the chain. |z| ≤ 2: "holds". From 2 to 3: "tension". Above 3: "does not hold".
  • Expiry. The block expires on 31 December 2030.

Addendum 1. It was registered on 21 October 2026 at 00:29:34 UTC and corrected at 00:32:33 UTC. Both times before any DESI data beyond DR2, and with arXiv checked.

  • The rule of block D does not change.
  • Next to its outcome, the same calculation in the w₀w_a model is reported. A qualifier fixed in advance is added.
  • The qualifier applies if three things happen together. Block D gives "tension" or worse. In w₀w_a Δχ²_q ≤ 4. The same data prefer w₀w_a by at least 2σ (Δχ² ≤ −6.18). Then the text will say: "the outcome of block D depends on the background model".
  • The qualifier is not given in one case: if 3√3 is saved only by a dark energy that already matters before the drag epoch. This happens when w₀ + w_a > −0.3. The drag epoch is the one in which ordinary matter stops being dragged by light: from then on the sound waves no longer propagate, and their distance stays imprinted. A test showed that this region can hide a wrong q. With synthetic data built with q = 6.5 one gets Δχ² 91 in ΛCDM, but only 0.63 in w₀w_a.
  • The program that decides the addendum passed 14 tests.

The program and its tests.

  • A program decides the outcome. Its tests include a dry run on DR2, which reproduces the numbers above. They also include the counterexamples of the second verification round. They all pass.
  • A registered test was faulty. It altered an element that was zero, so it never tested the asymmetry. 21 tests out of 22 had passed. It was redone with a non-zero element, and it passes.

7. What is known and what is specific to this analysis

What is already known. Many have already obtained H₀ from BAO + BBN, without the CMB:

  • Addison and colleagues 2018 (66.98 ± 1.18);
  • Cuceu and colleagues 2019 (67.6 ± 1.1);
  • Schöneberg, Lesgourgues and Hooper 2019;
  • Schöneberg and colleagues 2022 (67.6 ± 1.0; 68.3 ± 0.7 with ShapeFit);
  • DESI DR2 (68.51 ± 0.58).

Krolewski, Percival and Woodfinden 2024 (arXiv:2403.19227) obtain H₀ from BBN by another route. They use a baryon fraction measured in the distribution of galaxies. Their error is wider (67.1 +6.3/−5.3). The H₀ of this work is the BAO + BBN value of the first group of analyses.

What is specific to this analysis.

  • The ratio is imposed, q = Ω_c/Ω_b = 3√3, instead of being measured: one parameter fewer.
  • With this come two more pieces. The first is the inverse reading, from the BAO to deuterium. The second is the link with a₀ = cH₀/6 and with the wide-binary chain.
  • A targeted search found no analysis with this constraint together with BAO and BBN. It was not, however, a complete review.

What is not claimed.

  • 3√3 is not derived. It is a reading chosen knowing the data (grade B*).
  • With the CMB, 3√3 lies about 2σ from Planck (MM 2.44 and 2.54). That comparison is outside this chain.
  • Verlinde's emergent gravity gives the same ratio if Ω_b = 4/81 (MM 2.54).

8. How this can turn out wrong

  • Block D gives Δχ² > 9 with the DESI data beyond DR2. Then the reading ω_c = 3√3·ω_b would not read BAO + BBN.
  • A precise measurement of g† far from cH₀/6 (block A).
  • A shift of the D/H average, or of the rates of the reactions d(p,γ)³He and d+d, beyond the errors of D-1. This would move ω_b, and so the whole chain. The D-5 dictionary shows how much it weighs to use the rates from before LUNA.

Glossary

  • a₀. The acceleration scale of galaxies. Here we read a₀ = cH₀/6.
  • BAO. Baryon acoustic oscillations: a typical distance in the distribution of galaxies, used as a cosmic ruler.
  • BBN. Big-bang nucleosynthesis: the formation of the first light nuclei in the first minutes.
  • χ² and Δχ². An error score and the difference between the scores of two models.
  • CMB. The cosmic microwave background, the oldest light of the universe.
  • Deuterium. Hydrogen with one more neutron in the nucleus.
  • D/H. The number of deuterium nuclei for each hydrogen nucleus.
  • Dictionary. Here: BBN used to translate laboratory data into cosmological quantities.
  • H₀. The Hubble constant: how fast the universe is expanding today.
  • ΛCDM. The standard model of cosmology.
  • Mirror sector. A copy world of ours, with its own particles and its own light.
  • N_eff, ΔN_eff. N_eff measures the very light and very fast particles of the young universe, besides photons, counted as families of neutrinos; ΔN_eff is how much more of them there is compared with what is expected.
  • Neutron lifetime (τ_n). The typical time before a free neutron turns into other particles.
  • ω_b, ω_c. How much ordinary matter and how much cold dark matter there are in the universe.
  • Prior. A piece of information given to a calculation at the start.
  • q. The ratio Ω_c/Ω_b between dark matter and ordinary matter.
  • σ. The typical error.
  • Sound horizon (r_d). The distance travelled by the sound waves in the young universe, up to the drag epoch.
  • w₀w_a (CPL). A model of dark energy that changes over time.
  • Y_p. The mass fraction of helium-4 produced in the first minutes.
  • z. Redshift, or the z score (by how many σ a value is distant from another).
