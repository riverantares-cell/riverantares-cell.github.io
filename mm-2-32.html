Dark energy from growing relations

A Mathematical Materiality proposal explained step by step. The explanation uses examples, observations and formulas. The parts can also be read separately.

A cosmological model describes how the universe changes. This proposal starts from an unusual idea. Dark energy could be the effect of a network of relations that keeps growing. Dark energy is the name given to an effect. This effect seems to accelerate the expansion of the universe. We do not see it directly. We study its effects.

A growth rule sets how this component changes over time. The rule is controlled by three dark-sector parameters. A parameter is a number that can be varied in the model. The comparison uses 1,590 Pantheon+ supernova light curves. It also uses 13 DESI DR2 measurements and the summary distances of Planck 2018. Supernovae are very bright stellar explosions. Their light helps to estimate distances. DESI DR2 provides measurements linked to the distribution of matter in the universe. Planck 2018 summarizes some geometric properties of the cosmic microwave background. It is the very old light that fills the sky.

The chain of sampled values reaches a total chi-square of 1420.02. The chi-square summarizes how much the calculations and the measurements differ. It takes the uncertainties into account. With the same data and procedure, a lower value indicates a closer agreement. In the same calculation, a cosmological constant reaches 1421.19. The cosmological constant is a component with constant density. It is used as a reference for dark energy. The parameter \(p\) measures how far the model departs from that reference. It has an upper bound of 0.0724 at 95 per cent credibility. In simple words, according to this analysis the values compatible with the model lie below that bound. The credibility is 95 per cent. It is a bound, not a precise measurement of \(p\).

The model has three extra parameters in the dark sector. The result shows that it can agree with the data. It does not show that it is preferred. There are statistical criteria that also take the number of parameters into account. These criteria favour the cosmological constant. Moreover, the cosmic microwave background enters only through its geometric summaries. It does not enter through all the temperature and polarization data. The archived materials allow independent checks. A kit to redo the calculations with public data is planned in a separate record.

The question that comes before objects

Let us imagine we want to describe a city. We can list the buildings, the streets and the distribution of the inhabitants. We can also ask which relations make that form of organization possible. For example, which connections allow people to move, communicate and build something that lasts? These are two levels of description. The second does not erase the first. But it changes the point from which we start.

MM tries to do something similar for physical reality. The word ontology indicates the question about what we regard as fundamental. The question also concerns what could arise from its organization. In MM, objects and observers are described as configurations of the same network of relations. The reference point used to describe something is also part of the world being described.

This work grew out of four questions. Why do physical laws keep their regularity in different places and epochs? What lies behind the phenomena attributed to dark matter? What mechanism might account for dark energy? What picture of reality could make quantum behaviour intelligible?

Here we address only the third question. Dark matter and dark energy have similar names. But they refer to different problems. The distal component studied in these pages concerns the expansion of the universe. It is not an attempt to account for the rotation curves of galaxies. Rotation curves show the way stars orbit inside galaxies. A result obtained for one component is not transferred automatically to the other.

The image of the city helps to distinguish levels of description. But it is not physical evidence. In the same way, starting from relations is not enough to obtain a theory. Rules are needed that say which states are possible. They must also say how the states change and what we can calculate. Only then can we check whether the idea leads to a history of the universe consistent with observations.

Our aim is concrete. We want to build a path from the relational link to a history of the expansion. This history must be comparable with observations. Every necessary step must be visible. The result will not rest only on the evocative effect of the word "relation".

A relational link, explained without mysteries

To begin, two registers are enough. Let us imagine them as two boxes. Each can show a positive or a negative sign. They are small mathematical systems, not necessarily physical objects. Here "recording" means only having a state distinguishable from another. Let us think of two squares on a blackboard with different signs.

We connect the boxes with a link. We want their states not to be completely independent. But they must not be forced always to agree either. A simple possibility is to assign more internal cases to agreement than to disagreement.

Take, for example, a given first sign. Imagine that the link has three ways to preserve it and one way to reverse it. If the four cases are equally likely, the second register repeats the first three times out of four. The difference between the cases of agreement and those of disagreement is three quarters minus one quarter: one half.

We call \(q\) the transmitted share of the correlation. In the example, \(q=1/2\). Careful: the probability that the signs agree is \(3/4\). The coefficient \(q\) instead is \(1/2\). Here we adopt a symmetric distribution. With this distribution, \(q\) measures how much the initial sign remains recognizable. The comparison is with a completely random choice.

If the cases of agreement and disagreement were equal in number, the second register would give no information about the first. If disagreement were impossible, the sign would pass through without attenuation. The model we will use lies between these extremes. The transmitted share is positive and smaller than one.

We have not yet introduced metres, seconds or masses. We have not introduced quantum entanglement either. It is the particular connection studied in quantum mechanics. This is a classical probabilistic channel. Its task is more precise and narrower. It must give a mathematical form to the "fractionating link". It does so through a finite count of possibilities.

At this level, the observer is not an external consciousness. One register can be compared with another and serve as a reference. Building a complex material observer is another question. The mechanism described here works even without someone watching it.

What changes when a third register is inserted

Now let us insert a box between the first two. The first link transmits half of the correlation. The second transmits half of that. At the end a quarter remains: half of a half is a quarter.

This is the operational meaning of interposed fractionation. The comparison between the endpoints passes through distinct steps. Each of these steps contributes to the result. With two links we write \(Q=q_1q_2\). In a longer chain we multiply all the transmitted shares. The capital letter denotes the product over the entire path.

We can turn this multiplication into an addition by using the logarithm. The logarithm is an operation that turns products into sums. The quantity \(-\log Q\) grows when the retained correlation decreases. The contributions of the single steps add up along the chain. In this way we measure the depth of mediation, still without physical units. The precise proof is in Appendix A.

What have we gained? A distinction between direct comparison and mediated comparison. This distinction does not depend only on the names given to objects. We have not yet obtained a distance in space to be measured with a ruler.

An example clarifies the boundary. Suppose I draw a link as two segments instead of one. But I choose the fractions so that the total product stays the same. In this case the system has not changed. I have changed the drawing. It would be wrong to say that space grows every time I add a node to the drawing.

We therefore distinguish two operations. The first is describing the same relation more finely. The second is generating a new state of the network. Only the second changes the available capacity. This precaution makes the mechanism independent of arbitrary details of the drawing.

The product formula holds for the chain built here. In a network with several paths and cycles, the correlations can receive different contributions. Without a proof, we will not replace that problem with a formula valid for a single path.

Ten slots, eleven slots, twenty slots

Let us move from transmission to growth. We denote by \(n\) the number of slots. Slots are places where a state can be recorded. A simple rule replaces one slot with two at each update. The total therefore grows by one: ten, eleven, twelve and so on.

We want to connect this growth with fractionation. To do so we propose a precise channel between one state of the network and the next. If the network has \(n\) slots, we assign \(2n+1\) internal possibilities to agreement and one to disagreement. For example, with ten slots there are twenty-one cases of agreement and one of disagreement. The difference is divided by the total number of cases. The result is twenty divided by twenty-two, that is, ten elevenths.

\[q_n=\frac{n}{n+1},\qquad Q_{n_i,n_f}=\frac{n_i}{n_f}.\tag{1}\]

The second part of the formula can be understood with a simple calculation. Let us go from ten to twenty slots. We multiply ten elevenths, eleven twelfths, twelve thirteenths and so on, up to nineteen twentieths. The intermediate numbers cancel. Ten twentieths remain, that is, one half.

Capacity has doubled. The overall transmitted share has halved. In general:

\[C=\frac{n_f}{n_i}=Q_{n_i,n_f}^{-1},\qquad u=\log C.\tag{2}\]

Here \(C\) indicates how much the number of slots grows. It does not measure cubic metres. The quantity \(u\) counts that growth on a logarithmic scale. It is not yet the time shown by a clock.

The network can grow without preserving everything

Increasing the available slots does not mean increasing in the same proportion what remains active. Let us imagine an archive. Some new entries add a useful distinction. Others repeat information already reconstructed. The example separates total capacity from activity. It does not presuppose that a cosmic archivist exists.

We use three labels. A and B denote two active types. I denotes a slot that is inactive for the count we are tracking. "Inactive" does not mean nonexistent. That slot remains included in the total capacity.

At each step one slot is chosen at random. All slots have the same probability. If A is chosen, we replace it with A and B. If B is chosen, two B slots usually appear. With a certain probability, called \(p\), A and I appear instead. If I is chosen, two I slots appear.

The letters A and B do not denote known particles. They are minimal states of a possible mechanism. Their role depends on the operations they permit, not on the names chosen.

An explicit rule lets us follow the process. We do not have to decide in advance which cosmological result we would like to obtain. Once the rule is fixed, we can calculate a distribution of possible histories. We can find the mean growth, the random fluctuations and the response to changes in the rule.

Two probabilities not to be confused

The possibility that A and I are obtained from B is called redundancy. We denote it by \(p\). A simple way to realize it is to draw two independent labels. The case in which they are equal is regarded as redundant.

Take four equally likely labels. There are sixteen possible pairs and four equal pairs. The probability is one quarter. In general, one sums the squares of the label probabilities. The number four is a choice in the example, not a cosmological result.

Now we have two different quantities. The fraction \(q\) describes how much of the comparison passes from one state to the next. The probability \(p\) governs which records remain active while capacity grows. Calling both of them "fractions" would hide an essential difference.

The two constructions can coexist in a single probabilistic process. Appendix B gives the joint rule. It also checks that the probabilities sum to one. There is no need to pretend that one rule has been automatically deduced from the other.

The uniform choice also has a meaning. It says that a slot is not chosen more often merely because it carries the label A, B or I. If we changed this choice, the dynamics could change. Here robustness means that a behaviour is retained when specific changes are made to the rules. It does not mean that it is retained under every imaginable change.

This is a difference between a free narrative and a model. In a narrative we can give each word the behaviour that is needed. In a model we must accept the consequences of the stated rules. We must accept them even when they do not lead to the desired result.

The average of possible histories

Let us repeat the game many times starting from the same initial state. The sequences will be different. At one step A will be chosen, at another I. Chance will also affect the outcomes of B. We can nevertheless ask how many active registers there are on average after a given increase in capacity.

For this rule, the calculation of the mean closes exactly. The next step depends linearly on the current means. To write that particular equation we do not have to invent an approximate form of the correlations. The matrix in Appendix B collects in an orderly way the contributions of the two active types.

When the initial number of slots is large, we can describe growth continuously. This means replacing many small updates with a curve. The error is controlled in the specified limit. It does not mean that the network has become a continuous spacetime.

Let us consider the long run, in the model's dominant regime. The mean number of active registers grows as capacity raised to an exponent \(\lambda\). An exponent says how quickly one quantity grows compared with another. In this case it is calculated like this:

\[\lambda=\frac{1-2p+\sqrt{1+4p^2}}{2}.\tag{3}\]

There is no need to memorize the formula. Its message is this. Once the redundancy rule is fixed, the relative growth rate is no longer free. For \(p=1/4\), it is about \(0.809\).

A power with an exponent smaller than one grows more slowly than the starting quantity. Capacity can therefore keep increasing while the share of slots occupied by active registers decreases. At the same time, the total number of active registers can grow. The two things do not contradict each other.

The mean alone does not describe a single sequence. For this reason Appendix F also retains the calculation of the random fluctuations. It specifies the intervals in which their relative effect decreases when the initial number of slots grows.

More active registers, but less activity per slot

Let us imagine a numerical example in the dominant regime. We use the exponent just calculated. If capacity doubles, the active total is multiplied by about \(1.75\), not by two. So it grows by about 75 per cent. But now we divide the total by a doubled capacity. The active share per slot falls to about 88 per cent of its initial value.

This is the heart of the mechanism: the total grows, while its concentration decreases slowly. It is not yet a law of dark energy. It is the statistical behaviour that we will try to translate into physical language.

Why might cosmology care? When a volume increases, what matters is how much the energy it contains changes. What matters is not only how much energy it contains. Two components with the same density today may have different histories. They may lead to different evolutions.

Before speaking of energy, however, we must cross a boundary. A count of records does not automatically have the units of a volume or of an amount of energy. This step must be stated, not left to the reader.

The step from counts to physics

On a map we can measure distances in centimetres. To obtain kilometres we need a scale. Here there is a deeper question. We must not only choose a unit. We must decide which physical property a count represents.

We propose two identifications. The first assigns to every slot the same volume contribution. More slots mean more volume for the region considered. The second assigns the same energy to every active record. It uses the mean number of active records. These are simple, testable rules. The fact that the link fractionates the correlation does not prove them.

[IMPORTED EFFECTIVE POSTULATE] Volume is proportional to capacity. Energy is proportional to the mean number of active records. Volume is interpreted within a cosmological geometry with three spatial dimensions. For the component considered we assume the balance \(dU=-P\,dV\). We also assume that there are no additional exchanges with matter.

Let us read the formula in words. \(U\) is the energy contained in the region. \(V\) is its volume. \(P\) is pressure. The letter \(d\) indicates a very small change. The minus sign specifies how energy changes when volume changes in this balance.

If energy increases during expansion, the balance assigns a negative pressure. If energy is proportional to volume raised to \(\lambda\), a simple result is obtained:

\[w=\frac{P}{\rho}=-\lambda.\tag{4}\]

Here \(\rho\) denotes energy density, that is, energy divided by volume. The number \(w\) compares pressure with energy density. The two quantities have the same physical dimensions. In the example with \(p=1/4\), \(w\) is about \(-0.809\).

The result has a precise meaning. A discrete rule selects a growth rate. With the step to physics just assumed, that rate determines an effective pressure. We have not chosen the value of \(w\) directly. We have, however, chosen the microscopic rule. We have also chosen the way of translating counts into physical quantities. Both are assumptions to be assessed.

Why a negative pressure can change the expansion

In everyday life we think of pressure as something that pushes against a wall. In cosmology we must not imagine it as a wind that carries galaxies away. It is a property of the component that enters the energy balance and the equations of gravity.

In general relativity, pressure also contributes to the rules of expansion. For a uniform component, the decisive term contains energy density plus three times pressure. If pressure is sufficiently negative, that contribution can support the acceleration of the scale factor. The scale factor is the number that indicates how cosmic distances grow. For a dominant component, the threshold is \(w<-1/3\) [2].

Here we assume an effective theory of gravity. Counting records has not, by itself, proved Einstein's equations. The proposal derives a component to be inserted into those equations. It states the assumptions used.

How much space and how much time?

If the number of slots represents a three-dimensional volume, eight times as many slots correspond to a doubled length. Two cubed is eight. This leads to the scale factor, denoted by \(a\). The factor describes how the distances change between references that follow the mean expansion.

Let us compare the epoch in which the light is emitted and the one in which we observe it. Between these two epochs, the assumed step between capacity and volume gives:

\[\frac{a_o}{a_e}=\left(\frac{n_o}{n_e}\right)^{1/3}=Q_{e,o}^{-1/3}.\tag{5}\]

The subscripts indicate which epochs we are comparing: "e" for emission and "o" for observation. The cumulative link is thus connected to the increase in physical lengths. This happens because we have identified capacity with volume.

But a doubling can take different amounts of time. Knowing how much a quantity grows does not yet say how quickly it grows. Counting the updates does not automatically provide a clock.

To obtain the rate we adopt cosmological proper time. It is the time measured by ideal observers who follow the mean expansion. We also adopt the Friedmann equations. The clock is therefore connected consistently to the effective dynamics. It has not been derived from the algebra of the link alone. This distinction lets us reach a physical prediction without crediting the network with a deduction we have not made.

What does "distal" mean?

When we look far away, we receive light that set out in the past. Observed distance and the history of the universe are therefore tied together in the same light. Here distal denotes the links that accumulate between two epochs being compared. An epoch is a moment in the history of the universe, like a date in a diary.

This does not mean that a region has a higher density today simply because it is far from us. Take another observer who follows the same mean expansion. That observer does not become the physical centre of a different universe. The reference point of the comparison changes. The cosmological law must remain the same. It is the shared rule for describing the expansion.

Light lets us check whether this connection is consistent. In the adopted geometry, wavelengths stretch together with the scale factor. A wavelength is, for example, the distance between two crests of a light wave. We call redshift the relative increase of that length. If the length doubles, the redshift is one, not two. The stretching factor is \(1+z\).

Two light crests emitted a short time apart also arrive farther apart in time. For very small intervals, the same relation holds:

\[1+z=\frac{d\tau_o}{d\tau_e}=Q_{e,o}^{-1/3}.\tag{6}\]

In words, the formula says that three things have the same factor. They are the redshift, the stretching of time intervals and the growth between the two epochs. Here \(e\) indicates emission and \(o\) observation. The letter \(d\) indicates a very small change. The symbol \(\tau\) is cosmological proper time. It is the time followed by observers who take part in the mean expansion. The symbol \(Q\) denotes the share of correlation transmitted along the links. For finite intervals, this approximation holds if the intervals are short compared with cosmological changes.

This relation is not a new account of quantum behaviour. Nor does it derive local relativity from the binary channel of the registers.

What do we ask of supernovae?

A cosmological theory must confront measurements. It must not only propose suggestive images. Here we use an already completed comparison with Type Ia supernovae. They are stellar explosions. Their light curves, after some corrections, help to estimate distances. They are not all identical light bulbs. The analysis takes into account their standardization and the uncertainties [3].

The model supplies an expansion history. From that history we calculate the relation between redshift and apparent brightness. Apparent brightness indicates how bright the supernova looks from Earth. The question is restricted. A curve is fixed before the new calculation. Does it remain comparable with other observations, without changing its physical shape to fit the new points?

The first data set served to determine the parameters. It contained Pantheon+ and seven DESI BAO measurements, up to redshift one. BAO denotes a characteristic scale in the distribution of cosmic matter. This scale is used as a reference for measuring distances. This work is described in Section B18 of the MM 2.30 volume [11, 14].

The subsequent comparison used the DES-Dovekie reanalysis. A reanalysis is a new examination of data already collected. Objects already associated with the comparison inventory were excluded. Two uncertain associations were also excluded. Under the documented criteria, 1,448 distinct DES objects remain.

Pantheon+ also had 1,448 unique objects in the first comparison. It is a numerical coincidence between two different sets. It is not the same sample renamed.

The physical parameters stayed at the values used before. On the new observations only a uniform shift of the brightness scale was recalculated. This shift is needed when the absolute calibration is not fixed. Calibration is the reference that connects the measured numbers to a known brightness. Moving the whole curve up or down is not the same as changing its shape.

The procedure was fixed before acquiring and calculating the individual new distances. But it was fixed after the published DES results as a whole were known. This was neither a blinded test nor an external preregistration. A blinded test hides some results from the researchers until the end. An external preregistration records the procedure in advance with an independent party.

Even catalogues without shared objects might share the same calibrations. These conditions limit the strength of the comparison. They are part of the result and must be stated.

A proposal within the scientific conversation

The idea that geometry might have a more fundamental description did not begin with MM. Recognizing neighbouring work helps to understand which question we share. It also helps to understand how our construction differs. This recognition does not assign to MM the results obtained by others.

In causal sets, spacetime is described with discrete elements. It is also described with an order that represents cause-and-effect relations. The number of elements is connected to the volume of spacetime [4]. There is a similarity with the step from counts to geometry. But in our model the spatial volume of a region at a given epoch is not their spacetime volume. Moreover, our binary channel does not already contain that causal order.

In Everpresent Lambda, Ahmed, Dodelson, Greene and Sorkin study a varying, fluctuating cosmological constant. It is inspired by unimodular gravity and causal sets [5]. Here we instead follow the growth and dilution of an active component. We keep a possible constant part of the vacuum separate. The fluctuations of our counts do not automatically inherit the predictions of that programme.

Padmanabhan proposed interpreting expansion through an imbalance. It is the imbalance between the degrees of freedom associated with a surface and those in the interior [6]. The connection lies in associating expansion with counts. The mechanism is different. Here MM does not calculate those two numbers and does not derive their balance law.

Oriti, Sindoni and Wilson-Ewing derive cosmological dynamics from particular collective states in group field theory. They also use a scalar field as a reference for time [7]. It is another route that goes from a microscopic description to cosmology. It uses quantum structures of its own. Our statistical model does not replace it through a simple analogy.

Finally, the mathematics of branching belongs to a family studied for a long time. These are the generalized Pólya urns and the processes with several types, including Janson's work [8]. One draws an element and changes the composition according to rules tied to its type. This formalism was not invented by MM.

The difference of this construction lies in its starting element. We do not choose an even smaller piece of geometry, as many of these programmes do. We choose a relation: the possibility of telling two states apart. Space does not contain these differences. It counts them.

The proposed contribution is the specific chain that connects four elements. They are the fractionating channel, capacity, active growth and the cosmological connection. To establish whether and to what extent this chain is original, a broader comparison with the literature is needed. We do not claim general priority for the idea that space emerges from degrees of freedom.

How does a zone stay stable in a growing network?

Consider a region in which all registers have the same symbol. Its internal links transmit the signal without errors. Every internal reading then confirms agreement. Under the proposed rule, these comparisons add memories. But they do not divide the spatial links of the region.

Outside that region, imperfect links can produce different readings. Compatible interpositions increase the distances measured along the surrounding spatial network. The same update rule can therefore keep an organization and make the environment grow. There is no need for a separate command ordering that this particular object be held fixed.

The exploratory mesh model gives a numerical result on this mechanism. Let us consider the recorded simulation with a coherent region. Here the mean internal distance on the network stays about 1.32. This holds up to a total of 131,072 records. In a control, the links in the region are imperfect. Here the internal distance instead grows to about 6.96 [16]. These are steps in the network, not metres. The simulation is an experiment internal to the model, not an astronomical measurement.

What is given at the start matters. We supply the model with coherent states and perfect internal links. The simulation shows that this preparation can last. It does not explain how ordinary matter first forms organized in that way. The structure of the cells in three dimensions is also supplied. The dynamics does not select it from a network without structure.

The model distinguishes two descriptions that must not be confused. The cells can be subdivided inside a reference region. The coordinate volume of this region stays fixed. Meanwhile, the paths measured in steps of the current network can become longer. To interpret this second change as physical expansion, a physical calibration is needed. A denser network, by itself, does not prove that the universe has expanded.

Even keeping this limit in mind, we have obtained a useful result. Expansion need not mean the same behaviour for every relational organization. We can study stability and growth with a single explicit candidate mechanism. Formation, the calibration of physical lengths and gravitational binding remain steps to be built. They are not hidden ingredients of the demonstration.

Appendix A. The link has a precise probability

[CANDIDATE MM RULE] A channel connects two registers, denoted by \(\sigma\) and \(\sigma'\). Each can have state \(-1\) or \(+1\). For each pair of equal signs we count \(a\) internal possibilities. For each pair of different signs we count \(b\). All possibilities have the same probability. The integers satisfy \(a>b\geq1\).

The matrix \(T\) collects these counts. \(\mathsf{I}\) leaves each sign in its position. \(\mathsf{J}\) swaps the two positions. Dividing by the total \(a+b\) we obtain a probability matrix:

\[T=a\mathsf{I}+b\mathsf{J},\quad P(q)=\frac{T}{a+b},\quad q=\frac{a-b}{a+b}.\tag{A1}\]

In words: the channel favours agreement over disagreement. The number \(q\) measures this difference after dividing it by the total of the possibilities.

We adopt an initial distribution in which the two signs are equally likely. This distribution stays the same after passing through the channel. Moreover, knowing the initial sign \(\sigma\), the mean value of the final sign is \(q\sigma\). The mean value summarizes many cases. It is like averaging many tosses of a coin. One assigns \(+1\) to heads and \(-1\) to tails.

Repeating the step along an open chain, we obtain a mean correlation between the endpoints. It is the product of the values \(q\) of all the links: \(Q=\prod q_e\). The matrices share the same two eigenvectors. They are two special directions that do not change direction when the matrix acts on them. For this reason \(P(q_1)P(q_2)=P(q_1q_2)\).

On a tree, there is only one path between two endpoints. In a network with cycles, instead, there can be several paths. The product formula does not hold in general for the correlation of the joint distribution of that network.

[DEDUCTION WITHIN THE MODEL] If the path is unique, we can define \(d_I=-\log Q\). It is a length without units of measurement. It is positive if every link has \(q<1\). It is not a Lorentzian metric, that is, the measure of space and time used in relativity. It does not determine how many dimensions space has.

Suppose that the relative capacity is a continuous and positive function \(F(Q)\). We require that the product of two correlations multiplies the capacities: \(F(xy)=F(x)F(y)\). We also require that capacity grows when \(Q\) decreases. We then define \(g(s)=\log F(e^{-s})\). The function \(g\) is continuous. It turns sums of values of \(s\) into sums of values of \(g\). These conditions imply \(g(s)=\alpha s\), with \(\alpha>0\) if the function is not constant. Thus \(F(Q)=Q^{-\alpha}\).

This form follows from the assumptions on the function. It does not follow from them that this capacity must be the number of slots, nor that \(\alpha\) must equal one.

The version adopted here chooses \(\alpha=1\), \(a_n=2n+1\) and \(b_n=1\). Multiplying the steps and adding the logarithmic lengths we obtain:

\[\prod_{n=n_i}^{n_f-1}P(q_n)=P\left(\frac{n_i}{n_f}\right),\quad \sum_{n=n_i}^{n_f-1}(-\log q_n)=\log\frac{n_f}{n_i}.\tag{A2}\]

The first formula concerns the product of the links between the two states. This product is equivalent to a single channel with value \(n_i/n_f\). The second formula concerns the logarithmic lengths of the steps. They add up to the logarithm of the growth of capacity.

The case \(\alpha=1\) is a bridge assumption. It was not measured with an experiment and it is not a theorem about MM's ontology. The sequence of channels connects the epochs of the process considered. It is not a rule for every spatial branch.

The internal counts of the channel are neither the number of slots \(n\) nor the number of active registers \(K\). Nor is the multiplicity descriptor \(W\), introduced earlier, identified here with any of these three quantities.

Let us divide a link into several segments, while their product stays the same. In this case the relation described does not change. An update \(n\mapsto n+1\), instead, changes the product. Growth therefore does not depend on the arbitrary number of segments with which we draw the chain.

Appendix B. A single rule for capacity and registers

The state is \((A,B,I,\sigma)\). The quantities \(A\), \(B\) and \(I\) are nonnegative integer counts. Moreover, \(A+B+I=n\geq2\). At each step we choose a slot at random. We replace it according to the rules already described.

We denote by \(\mathcal K_n(x,x')\) the transition rule for the counts. Here \(x=(A,B,I)\) is the initial state and \(x'\) the next one. The probabilities of all possible outcomes sum to one. Every outcome increases the total of slots from \(n\) to \(n+1\).

[CANDIDATE MM RULE] Given the state and the value of \(n\), we assume that the binary history channel and branching are independent. The joint probability becomes:

\[\Pr(x',\sigma'\mid x,\sigma)=\mathcal K_n(x,x')P(q_n)_{\sigma\sigma'}.\tag{B1}\]

The formula is normalized. The probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one. It realizes at the same time the multiplication of channels of A2 and the dynamics of the active registers. It does not say that one of the two rules automatically imposes the other. The law depends on global capacity. A local causal mechanism that generates it remains to be derived.

Let us now consider \(X=(A,B)^T\). It is the column that collects the counts of the two active types. The possible changes are \(v_1=(0,1)^T\), \(v_2=(1,-1)^T\) or no change. Their probabilities are, respectively, \([A+(1-p)B]/n\), \(pB/n\) and \(I/n\). The mean of the change is:

\[\mathbb E[\Delta X\mid X]=\frac{MX}{n},\quad M(A,B)^T=(pB,A+(1-2p)B)^T.\tag{B2}\]

The matrix \(M\) collects how much each type contributes to the mean change of the next step. We call \(m_n=\mathbb E X_n\) the mean of the counts after step \(n\). We obtain exactly:

\[m_{n+1}=\left(\mathsf{I}+\frac{M}{n}\right)m_n.\tag{B3}\]

It is exact because the mean increase depends linearly on the current means and the total \(n\) is determined, not random.

Let us take an eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(M\). It is a number that describes one of the matrix's own growth rates. The accumulated factor between \(n_i\) and \(n_f\) is:

\[\prod_{n=n_i}^{n_f-1}\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{n}\right) =\frac{\Gamma(n_f+\lambda)\Gamma(n_i)} {\Gamma(n_f)\Gamma(n_i+\lambda)}.\tag{B4}\]

The function \(\Gamma\) extends the factorial to non-integer values as well. If \(n_i\) becomes large and we keep the ratio \(n_f/n_i\) fixed, the formula approaches \((n_f/n_i)^\lambda\). The relative error is of the order of \(1/n_i\).

This limit leads to the continuous equation \(dm/du=Mm\), where \(u=\log(n/n_i)\). It is a controlled approximation for the means. To approximate a single random history as well, the fluctuation control of Appendix F is needed.

Appendix C. Why active growth is robust

The eigenvalues of \(M\) in B2 are those of Formula 3. Both signs in front of the square root are taken. For \(0<p\leq1\) and nonzero initial activity, the dominant mode is accessible and positive. The dominant mode is the growth direction that prevails in the long run.

In the continuous limit, we call \(\bar K=m_A+m_B\) the mean active total. We call \(f=m_B/\bar K\) the mean share of type B. We obtain:

\[\frac{d\log\bar K}{du}=1-pf,\qquad \frac{df}{du}=1-(1+2p)f+pf^2.\tag{C1}\]

Here \(f\) is the ratio between two means. It is not the mean of the random ratio B divided by A+B.

The value of \(f\) stays between zero and one. If it is zero, its rate of change is one. If it is one, the rate is \(-p\). So the rule does not push it out of the interval. Moreover, let us take two solutions \(f\) and \(g\) with the same \(p\). The difference between their fields of change is \(-(f-g)[1+p(2-f-g)]\). The distance between the solutions therefore cannot be greater than \(e^{-u}|f(0)-g(0)|\). The different initial conditions come closer at least according to this bound.

The stable fixed point is \(f_*=(1+2p-\sqrt{1+4p^2})/(2p)\). At that point the growth rate is \(1-pf_*=\lambda_+\). The approach to this value depends on the same initial conditions. This transient phase is not removed before the comparison with observations.

Let us also examine a broader family of rules, with reduced productivity. A produces A+B with probability \(s\). Otherwise it produces A+I. B produces A+I with probability \(p\), B+B with probability \((1-p)t\) and B+I in the other cases. All probabilities remain admissible when \(s,t\in[0,1]\).

The matrix of the mean contributions in this family is:

\[M_{s,t}(A,B)^T=(pB,sA+bB)^T,\quad b=t(1-p)-p.\tag{C2}\]

The dominant rate is:

\[\lambda_+=\frac{b+\sqrt{b^2+4sp}}{2},\quad \lambda_+>\frac{1}{3}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ t+p(3s-t-1)>\frac{1}{3}.\tag{C3}\]

The other root is not positive. To verify the condition in C3, one evaluates the characteristic polynomial \(x^2-bx-sp\) at the point \(x=1/3\). The inequality has a strictly positive margin for every \(p\in[0,1]\) exactly when \(s>4/9\) and \(t>1/3\). It is a result about the model, not a measurement of parameters of the universe.

If \(p=0\), the redundant channel disappears. In this case the original rule gives \(\lambda_+=1\). It is a formal limit. With a finite number of labels having normalized probabilities one cannot have \(\sum\pi_j^2=0\). Moreover, that limit does not automatically eliminate the fluctuations.

Appendix D. The physical bridge and the complete clock

[IMPORTED EFFECTIVE POSTULATE] Consider a homogeneous region that follows the mean expansion. It is called comoving because it keeps its identity while its physical volume changes. We set \(V=v_*n\) and \(U_D=E_*\bar K\), with positive constants. \(V\) is the volume and \(U_D\) the energy of the distal component. The units assigned to each count are \(v_*\) and \(E_*\).

In three dimensions, the scale factor satisfies \(a/a_i=(n/n_i)^{1/3}\). We define the energy density \(\rho_D=U_D/V\). We assume the pressure \(P_D=-dU_D/dV\), without additional exchanges with matter. From these assumptions it follows:

\[w_D=-\frac{d\log\bar K}{du}=-1+pf,\qquad \frac{d\rho_D}{du}=-(1+w_D)\rho_D.\tag{D1}\]

The first formula concerns the ratio \(w_D\) between pressure and density. This ratio depends on the growth rate of the active registers. The second describes how the density changes. In the general case of C2, \(w_D=-s(1-f)-t(1-p)f\). Since the values of \(s,t,p,f\) lie between zero and one, \(-1\leq w_D\leq0\).

In the dominant mode, the density follows \(\rho_D\propto a^{-3(1-\lambda_+)}\) and \(w_D=-\lambda_+\). These results depend on the effective connection assumed between counts and physics.

[IMPORTED EFFECTIVE POSTULATE] We adopt cosmological proper time \(\tau\). It is measured by ideal observers who follow the mean expansion. We assume a flat FLRW geometry and general relativity. We also assume separately conserved nonrelativistic matter and a possible constant component \(\rho_v\). FLRW denotes the homogeneous and isotropic model used to describe the expansion. We neglect radiation in the late period considered here.

Keeping the speed of light \(c\) explicit, the closed system is:

\[H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}(\rho_m+\rho_D+\rho_v),\quad \frac{du}{d\tau}=3H,\quad \frac{dm}{d\tau}=3HMm.\tag{D2}\]

\(H\) is the expansion rate, \(G\) the gravitational constant, and \(\rho_m\) the matter density. We choose the branch \(H>0\), a positive total density and the initial conditions. With these choices we can calculate the evolution. Matter follows \(\rho_m=\rho_{m,i}e^{-u}\).

Differentiating the Friedmann constraint and using D1 and D2, we find:

\[\dot H=-\frac{4\pi G}{c^2} [\rho_m+(1+w_D)\rho_D].\tag{D3}\]

The derivative with the dot is with respect to proper time \(\tau\). The acceleration of the scale factor is also:

\[\frac{\ddot a}{a}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3c^2} [\rho_m+(1+3w_D)\rho_D-2\rho_v].\tag{D4}\]

The formulas make it possible to check that the constraint, the energy balance and the acceleration are consistent. No growth law incompatible with the energy balance is added.

If only the distal component dominates and \(\lambda_+<1\), the scale factor follows \(a\propto(\tau-\tau_*)^{2/[3(1-\lambda_+)]}\). If instead \(\lambda_+=1\), it follows \(a\propto e^{H\tau}\). These are solutions valid in those regimes. They do not reconstruct the Big Bang or the actual age of the universe. When matter is present, D4 must be used. Checking only that \(w_D<-1/3\) is not enough.

Appendix E. From the universe's rate to light signals

[IMPORTED EFFECTIVE POSTULATE] We assume that light follows the null geodesics of the FLRW geometry. We also assume that the number of photons is conserved. A null geodesic is the path that light follows in this geometry. For a comoving emitter and receiver, the radial path satisfies:

\[\chi=\int_{\tau_e}^{\tau_o}\frac{c\,d\tau}{a(\tau)}.\]

The quantity \(\chi\) represents the comoving distance. It is the distance measured taking the expansion into account. Two light crests emitted a short time apart travel the same comoving interval. Subtracting the two integrals we obtain:

\[\frac{d\tau_o}{a_o}=\frac{d\tau_e}{a_e},\qquad 1+z=\frac{a_o}{a_e}=Q_{e,o}^{-1/3}.\tag{E1}\]

The formula connects cosmological events. It does not derive from the algebra of the registers the local time dilation predicted by relativity.

We set today's \(a_o=1\). We denote by \(\mathcal E(z)=H(z)/H_0\) the expansion rate divided by its present value. In the flat case, the luminosity distance is:

\[D_L(z)=(1+z)\frac{c}{H_0} \int_0^z\frac{d\zeta}{\mathcal E(\zeta)}.\tag{E2}\]

To compare the theory with supernovae we use the distance modulus, \(\mu=5\log_{10}[D_L/(10\,\mathrm{pc})]\). We use the usual calibrations and an additional term if the absolute luminosity is not fixed. The parsec, denoted by pc, is a unit of astronomical distance. E1 and E2 are the standard cosmological bridge [1]. They are not a new law of light propagation.

Appendix F. Fluctuations, calibrations and alternatives

The discrete model lets us follow the fluctuations before giving them an astronomical meaning. We denote by \(\Sigma_n=\operatorname{Cov}(X_n)\) the covariance matrix of \(X_n\). A covariance describes how much several quantities vary together. It is like a table that summarizes the differences and the connections between measurements.

For the original rule, we define \(D(m)=[m_A+(1-p)m_B]v_1v_1^T+pm_Bv_2v_2^T\). The conditional second moment of the changes is \(D(X)/n\). Applying the balance of the second moments, we obtain exactly:

\[\Sigma_{n+1}=\Sigma_n+ \frac{M\Sigma_n+\Sigma_nM^T+D(m_n)}{n} -\frac{(Mm_n)(Mm_n)^T}{n^2}.\tag{F1}\]

Suppose that the initial state is fixed without randomness. Suppose also that the ratio \(n/n_i\) stays below a fixed bound \(R\). The possible changes are bounded. Formula F1 then gives an estimate \(S_{n+1}\leq(1+C/n)S_n+C'\). Here \(S_n=\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_n\) and the constants depend on \(R\) and on the rule. Repeating the calculation, \(S_n\) is of the order of \(n_i\). It follows that \(\operatorname{Var}(K_n/n)\) is of the order of \(1/n_i\).

Chebyshev's inequality thus makes it possible to justify the limit of the ratios on finite intervals of relative capacity. It does not justify a uniform limit for all future times when \(n_i\) stays fixed.

To translate F1 into a spatial spectrum, into gravitational lensing effects or into anisotropies, three ingredients are needed. They are correlations between regions, causal transport and a rule that reduces the details at large scale. This observational signature has not yet been derived. Separating the counts does not determine the physical sound speed. We cannot simply assume that the parameter \(w\) equals the square of that speed.

The importance of the physical connection becomes clear if we change it. Suppose that \(V\propto n^\nu\) and that the energy of each register grows as \(n^\eta\). In the dominant mode, one obtains \(w=-(\lambda_++\eta)/\nu\). The prediction therefore also depends on how we connect the counts to physical quantities. It does not depend only on the matrix. Adding \(U_v=\rho_vV\) leaves the counts unchanged. But it changes the cosmology. The vacuum residue remains distinct and unexplained.

Finally, a homogeneous history with \(\rho_D>0\) and \(-1<w_D<1\) can be reproduced by a canonical scalar field. A scalar field is a quantity that has a value at every point of space. Setting \(c=1\), the relations are \(\dot\phi^2=(1+w_D)\rho_D\) and \(V_\phi=(1-w_D)\rho_D/2\) [2].

This is an alternative effective description. It does not derive the microscopic field from MM. It shows why agreement on distances does not uniquely identify a relational origin.

Appendix I. A candidate rule for memory and space

This appendix defines an exploratory model. It is distinct from the three-type B17 process, described in Appendices B-F. We denote by \(S\) the spatial registers and by \(M_{\rm mem}\) the memories. We denote by \(N=S+M_{\rm mem}\) their total.

Every spatial register carries a fixed symbol \(x_i\). The symbol is chosen from a set of \(m\) symbols. The candidate rule excludes memories from the graph of spatial adjacencies. A graph is a mathematical drawing of points and links. Here it serves to calculate which registers are neighbours.

Copying a memory is an operation defined by the model. It is not a derivation of the existence of an observer. Nor is it an account of quantum measurement.

On a link \(e\) we use the reading channel \(r_e\). In the exploratory notes this quantity is called q. It is not the epoch channel \(q_n\) of Equation 1.

At each of the two endpoints, the reading copies the stored symbol with probability \(r_e\). Otherwise it picks one of the \(m\) symbols at random. All symbols have equal probability. We set \(A_r=(1-r^2)/m+r^2\) and \(B_r=(1-r^2)/m\). The confirmation probability is:

\[A_r=\frac{1-r^2}{m}+r^2,\quad B_r=\frac{1-r^2}{m},\quad \Pr(\mathrm{confirm}\mid x_i,x_j) =B_r+r^2\mathbf{1}_{x_i=x_j}.\tag{I1}\]

The symbol \(\mathbf{1}_{x_i=x_j}\) equals one if the symbols of the two endpoints are equal. Otherwise it equals zero. The formula says that the probability depends on the quality of the reading. It also depends on the agreement between the initial symbols.

[CANDIDATE MM RULE] A confirmation creates a memory. Two different readings instead request the interposition of a spatial register between the endpoints. Every comparison needed to prepare the interposition also follows the same reading rule.

If the request cannot be completed, a memory of an unrealized distinction is created. If it succeeds, a spatial register is created with a new symbol chosen at random. The interpositions that succeeded in the earlier steps remain. They remain even if a later request fails. Every reading thus has its own recorded outcome. The entire chain is not a single indivisible update.

The numerical implementation uses ordered subdivisions of tetrahedra. It checks that neighbouring cells can be updated compatibly. A tetrahedron is a solid figure with four faces. The model already assumes the three-dimensional cells and their order. It also already assumes the selection weights and the reading channel. It does not derive them from a deeper rule.

Spatial distance is the length of the shortest path in the current spatial graph. It is not the negative logarithm of a historical correlation. Here neither of the two measures is identified with a relativistic metric.

Consider a region initialized with all symbols equal and with \(r_e=1\) on the internal links. In that region every internal reading confirms. The rule protects those links from subdivision. The reported stability of the internal distances is a numerical result. It holds for this preparation of the network. It is not a general theorem excluding every change of the shortest paths caused from outside.

In the limit \(r_e=0\), the confirmation probability is \(1/m\). It is so whatever the initial states and the previous readings. This recovers B17's independent coincidence probability. It does not recover its entire three-type transition rule. It does not recover the energy assignment or the cosmological predictions.

Appendix J. How we count the outcomes of an event

We denote by \(k\) the number of top-level attempts. In each state, we choose a cell event with probability \(s=S/N\). Otherwise we copy a memory and add one memory.

Consider a cell event and the whole state at that moment. We define \(\gamma_S\) and \(\gamma_{\rm mem}\) as the mean number of new spatial registers and new memories produced. We also count all the comparisons needed before the event is completed. These means can depend on the state. They need not be constants.

Let \(R_{\rm done}\) be the number of realized readings and \(F\) the number of confirmations. Let \(D_{\rm stop}\) be the number of unrealized distinctions. Let \(L\) be the number of memory copies. In the numerical model considered, these identities hold:

\[S-S_0=R_{\rm done},\qquad M_{\rm mem}-M_{{\rm mem},0}=F+D_{\rm stop}+L.\tag{J1}\]

The checks of the outcomes verify J1 in the runs examined. A cell event can produce several records. It is not correct to suppose that every top-level attempt increases \(N\) by exactly one.

The mean changes, given the state, are:

\[\mathbb E[\Delta S]=s\gamma_S,\qquad \mathbb E[\Delta N]=1+s(\gamma_S+\gamma_{\rm mem}-1).\tag{J2}\]

The first formula says that spatial increases happen only in cell events. The second also includes the memory copies. The formulas are obtained by separating cell events from copies.

From these means we can build a deterministic approximation. It is a curve that describes the mean behaviour without following every random case:

\[\frac{ds}{dN}=\frac{1}{N} \left[\frac{s\gamma_S}{1+s(\gamma_S+\gamma_{\rm mem}-1)}-s\right].\tag{J3}\]

J3 is not an exact equation for the mean of a random ratio. To treat it as a convergence theorem, an adequate continuous limit, a control of the moments and sufficient regularity are needed.

Suppose that the coefficients stay constant and that the regime is dominated by memories, with \(s\to0\). In this case the approximation gives \(d\log S/d\log N\to\gamma_S\). This conclusion is conditional. Coefficients measured during a transient phase do not by themselves prove an exponent valid for very long times.

In the reference control, at \(N=131072\), \(S_0=64\) and \(S=27132\). The 27,068 new spatial registers split into 17,241 realizations of the initial request and 9,827 realizations of prerequisites. The 103,940 memories split into 11,517 confirmations, 4,421 unrealized distinctions and 88,002 copies [16]. These are counts of the model, not observations.

Coefficients measured in the same run can be used. This makes it possible to reconstruct the growth only approximately. The maximum recorded discrepancy for \(S/N\) is 0.03042. Appendix K presents a distinct calculation of the coefficients. The calculation is based on an idealized chain rule.

Appendix K. Closed equations for independent chains

Let \(J\) be the number of requests needed to complete a node that distinguishes the endpoints, if all the prerequisites succeed. We use a distribution \(\pi^{\rm top}\) for the initial requests. We use a distribution \(\pi^f\) for the forced ones. In the chain notes, \(J\) is called \(K\). It is not an energy count.

The model assumes that the child subtrees are independent. It assumes that \(J\) is independent of the reading outcomes. It also assumes that the confirmation probability is constant. We call this probability \(p\) and set \(\alpha=1-p\).

The generating functions collect the probabilities of the different values of \(J\). The first is:

\[G_f(R)=\sum_{j\geq0}\pi^f_jR^j,\qquad H_f(R)=\sum_{j\geq0}\pi^f_j\sum_{h=0}^{j-1}R^h.\tag{K1}\]

The inner sum is zero when \(j=0\). We define \(G_{\rm top}\) and \(H_{\rm top}\) in the same way.

Let \(R\) be the probability that a forced request succeeds. To succeed, its reading must distinguish the endpoints and all the children must succeed. Hence \(R=\alpha G_f(R)\). We choose the fixed point obtained recursively by increasing the depth of the chain. Here we use the subcritical case. It is the case in which the expected growth of the subtrees stays below the threshold that would make the chain explode. In this case the fixed point is unique.

Let \(T_f\) be the mean number of readings attempted in a forced chain. Let \(M_f\) be the mean number of spatial registers realized. The chain stops at the first child that fails. The earlier successful children, however, remain. It follows:

\[R=\alpha G_f(R),\qquad T_f=\frac{1}{1-\alpha H_f(R)},\qquad M_f=RT_f.\tag{K2}\]

For an initial event, the means of spatial registers and memories are:

\[\gamma_S=\alpha[G_{\rm top}(R)+H_{\rm top}(R)M_f],\quad \gamma_{\rm mem}=1+\alpha H_{\rm top}(R)T_f-\gamma_S.\tag{K3}\]

These are exact means for the independent-chain model. For them to be finite, the mean size of the subtrees must also be finite. A sufficient condition is \(\alpha\mathbb E[J_f]<1\). The distribution used here satisfies this condition.

The formulas do not assert that the events in the spatial mesh are independent. Applying them to that mesh is an approximation to be assessed. No metric, gravitational constant or cosmic clock appears in K1-K3. The formulas connect conditionally the local statistics of the prerequisites to the mean production of spatial registers and memories.

Appendix O. An explicit rule that depends on the state

We denote by \(\eta\) the complete state. It includes the ordered mesh and the symbol retained by each spatial register. It also includes the reading channels and the number of memories. In the internal calculation there is also an ordered list of the requests still to be checked. Each reading uses rule I1 and the actual symbols at the two endpoints. It need not reconstruct the probability of the symbols starting from a shortened record of past readings. If some symbols are not observed, averaging requires their complete conditional distribution.

[CANDIDATE MM RULE] In the uniform version, we choose a tetrahedron \(c\) of generation \(g\) with a weight proportional to \(2^{-g}\). A generation indicates the level of the subdivision. We resolve the oldest incompatible request first. If two requests have the same generation, we choose the one with the smaller identification number. We use the ordered subdivision into two parts described by the model's rule. Each successful interposition receives a new symbol chosen uniformly. Confirmations and failed distinctions produce the records defined in Appendix I. These choices specify the candidate process. The ontology does not impose them uniquely.

Consider a cell event that terminates. Consider all its possible histories of reading and of choosing the new symbols. We denote a history by \(h\). Its probability is \(P_\eta(h\mid c)\). It is the product of the probabilities of the single conditional readings and of the draws of new symbols. We call \(R_S(h)\) and \(R_M(h)\) the numbers of spatial registers and memories produced by that history. Then:

\[\Gamma_X(\eta)=\sum_c\omega_c(\eta)\sum_h P_\eta(h\mid c)R_X(h), \qquad X\in\{S,M\}. \tag{O1}\]

In words, we want to find the mean growth of \(S\) or of \(M\). We sum the results of every possible history. We multiply each result by its probability and by the weight of the chosen cell. The cell weights sum to one. Suppose that the event terminates with probability one. Suppose also that the number of records produced has a finite mean. In this case O1 gives the exact conditional mean of that number. In J2, the two growth coefficients are precisely these quantities. They are quantities that depend on the state. O1 defines an explicit mean. It is not a computational shortcut. It does not reduce the whole process to two variables.

We denote by \(\mathcal P\) the complete rule. It includes the choice of the cell and the copying of a memory. The distribution of all the states evolves like this:

\[\mu_{k+1}(\eta')=\sum_\eta\mu_k(\eta)\mathcal P(\eta,\eta'). \tag{O2}\]

The formula says how the probability of finding the next state \(\eta'\) is obtained. All the previous states are considered. The probability of each state is multiplied by the probability of passing from that state to \(\eta'\). Then the results are summed. The elementary process of readings and requests is defined even before the results of an event are grouped. To use O1-O2 at the level of the grouped event, the termination conditions stated above are needed. The finite runs verify that every executed event terminates. They do not prove that every possible history of infinite subdivisions terminates.

Copying a memory leaves the mesh and the symbols unchanged. If we remove these copies, we obtain exactly the embedded chain tested in Appendix Q. This, however, does not preserve the growth exponents of the total number of records. Nor does it define physical time.

Appendix P. Why counts alone are not enough

Let us think of an exact rule that looks only at the number \(S\) of spatial registers and at the number of memories. We can show with a counterexample why the rule that depends on the state cannot be replaced with a rule of that kind.

We start from the same periodic mesh of \(3\times3\times3\) cells. We use the same channels with \(r=1/2\) and the same counts. A periodic mesh connects opposite edges, as if the grid closed on itself. All the initial reference links are compatible. So their events require no additional checks.

In preparation A, all vertices have the same symbol. In preparation B, the symbol depends on the first index of the grid, taken modulo three. Each initial link joins opposite corners of the cube. In preparation B the symbols are different, also across the periodic edges. Both preparations are allowed among the four symbols of the model. For each preparation, the selected initial events have the same confirmation probability. Rule I1 gives:

\[\Gamma_S(\eta_A)=\frac{9}{16},\qquad \Gamma_S(\eta_B)=\frac{13}{16},\qquad (S,M)_A=(S,M)_B. \tag{P1}\]

The two states have the same counts \(S\) and \(M\). But they produce different mean rates of new spatial registers. So no exact rule that uses only those two counts can describe all the allowed states. It is a mathematical counterexample, not a small numerical imprecision. It does not exclude an approximate rule for a restricted group of states. Nor does it exclude adding other variables that summarize the relevant correlations. We do not claim that a universal reduced rule exists even for the set of random initial states considered.

We can also check the counting of events without a simulation. Take an initial request with two ordered checks, both at the ends. We denote by \(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2\) the distinction probabilities of the three checks. There are four possible histories, because the process stops at the first confirmation. The first history is an immediate confirmation. The second is a confirmation at the first child. The third is a success of the first child followed by a confirmation of the second. The fourth is complete success. The pairs of outputs produced (spatial registers / memories) are, in order, \(0/1\), \(0/2\), \(1/2\) and \(3/0\).

We weight each result with the probability of its history. With these weights, the mean of spatial registers is \(\alpha_0\alpha_1(1+2\alpha_2)\). The mean of the total number of readings is \(1+\alpha_0(1+\alpha_1)\). The supplied check lists all four histories for 125 triples of probabilities. It also includes the triples with values zero and one. In the third history, the earlier successes remain recorded. Cancelling them all together at the end would be a different rule.

The result clarifies the structure of the model. The reading rule and the complete state determine the growth. The compressed descriptions have a precise task and an approximation error that can be measured. This is a complete mathematical formulation for the specified finite events. It does not derive the rule, the dimension, the energy unit or the cosmic clock.

Appendix U. When the noise is negligible and where the threshold lies

The expansion history describes a mean value. The gate function acts locally. So different regions switch on at different moments. Moreover, every coherence cell contains a finite number of records. So the mean value can also fluctuate. A coherence cell is a region treated as a group. To picture it, let us think of the seats of a classroom in which the pupils answer together.

The analysis does not simulate these fluctuations. For each sample it calculates an upper bound on the chi-square that could be due to the count noise. The count noise is the random variation produced by the finite number of records. The sample is used only if the bound stays below a fixed threshold.

The variance at the moment of switch-on is calculated exactly:

\[\operatorname{Var}(\delta\ln N)= \frac{2-(a_{\rm seed}/a_{\rm on})^3}{N_{\rm seed}}. \tag{U1}\]

The formula compares the fluctuation of the logarithm of \(N\) with the number of records present in the seed. The symbols \(a_{\rm seed}\) and \(a_{\rm on}\) denote the scale factor at the seed and at switch-on. With the other values unchanged, the variance decreases as one divided by \(N_{\rm seed}\). The more records there are in each cell, the smaller the fluctuation.

At a reference point, \(N_{\rm seed}=10^{20}\) and the coherence length is 100 Mpc. An Mpc, or megaparsec, is a large unit of astronomical distance. At that point, the bound on the chi-square is \(4\times10^{-11}\). It is eleven orders of magnitude below what the three data blocks can resolve.

That point is a corner of the initial region explored by the chain. It is not an edge of the allowed values. The initial interval extends to \(N_{\rm seed}=10^4\). 53 per cent of the retained samples have a value below \(10^{20}\). The smallest value is about \(1.7\times10^9\). The certificate keeps all these samples in the negligible-noise branch. It is not the initial choice of the interval that keeps them. The two spatial parameters are not bounded by the comparison. The data, then, are not preferring a finer network.

Let us read formula U1 the other way round. We obtain the only quantitative target offered by this work for the non-uniform sector. The omitted contribution reaches a hundredth of chi-square with about \(4\times10^{11}\) records per cell. It reaches a tenth with about \(4\times10^{10}\) and one unit with about \(4\times10^9\). The declared threshold is crossed near \(10^{11}\).

These values hold for the reference coherence length, 100 Mpc. They change as the other parameters vary. For this reason the certificate checks each sample separately. The interesting interval is wide, down to \(10^4\) records. It lies mostly below the branch used here.

The analysis does not cover that noisier branch. The program rejects those samples instead of extending the results beyond the limit. If the noise is not negligible, the full covariance matrix across the data blocks is needed. Its determinant is also needed. A simple bound on the omitted contribution is not enough.

The program applies the same rejection when the count noise could leave traces beyond the declared limits. These are traces in the temperature of the cosmic microwave background or in its gravitational lensing. Three summary distances alone cannot absorb those traces.

In the 256,064 evaluations of the main chain, 12,433 samples were rejected for this reason. Another 791 were rejected because they exceeded the threshold of the omitted contribution. Another 168,273 were outside the initial interval of the parameters. This happens often with the sampling method used. In 333 cases the calculation was outside the interval foreseen by the emulator. Four cases failed the final check on the closure of the gate function. The chain summary keeps these counts.

So a lower bound shown by the chain for the number of records per cell marks the declared boundary of validity. It is not a measurement, whatever its value. The next step is to build that second branch. Then one must ask whether a network coarse enough to produce observable fluctuations is compatible with the same three data blocks. It is a defined task, not a forecast of what we will find.

The local transition produces noise in the counts. In Appendix U, that noise is negligible above about \(10^{11}\) records per coherence cell. This holds at the reference coherence length. The test concerns a network coarse enough to make that noise important. It asks whether the three data blocks of Appendix S are compatible with a network of that kind.

The test considers the whole interval allowed by the model: from \(10^4\) to \(10^{25}\) initial records per cell. The number of initial records is denoted by \(N_{\rm seed}\).

How it is calculated

For every design point, the certificate stores a linear response. It is the response of every data row to each unit column of the count noise. A linear response indicates how much a row changes when an input changes. It is like noting how much each column of a sheet moves if one piece of data is modified.

The responses are stored for three variances: \(10^{-4}\), \(2\times10^{-4}\) and \(10^{-3}\). The closure coefficients are also archived. These coefficients say how much each row shifts on average per unit of variance. The second branch uses these values in every sample. With them it builds the mean shift \(\mu(\theta)\) and the noise covariance.

In this calculation, the bound on the omitted contribution of Appendix U is replaced by the full form:

\[-2\ln\mathcal L= (r-\mu)^T \left(C+A\Sigma A^T\right)^{-1} (r-\mu) +\ln\det\left(C+A\Sigma A^T\right). \tag{V1}\]

The formula compares the residuals corrected for the mean shift with the overall uncertainties. Here \(C\) is the published covariance of the three data blocks. The symbol \(A\) is the stored linear response. The symbol \(\Sigma\) is the covariance of the count noise. Its scale follows U1.

The matrix inside the brackets includes the uncertainties of the data. It also includes those due to the count noise. The term with the determinant also takes into account how large this overall uncertainty is. The rank of the contribution \(A\Sigma A^T\) does not exceed the number of noise columns. To calculate the inverse and the determinant, the Woodbury identity is therefore used. It is an algebraic method that simplifies the calculations when the added contribution has few independent directions.

The emulator supplies \(A\) and \(\mu\) in the same cases in which it now supplies the rows of the certificate. The declared error of the emulator enters V1, as it already enters the present likelihood.

The rule for deciding

The chain uses the same initial interval as the present test and does not change it. The strong reading is used, \(\nu_v=0\): all of dark energy is attributed to the branching component.

The marginal posterior distribution of \(\log N_{\rm seed}\) is compared with a reference. A posterior distribution summarizes which values remain plausible after the data have been considered. The comparison is necessary because a bound set at the start can exclude the lowest values even without information from the data.

The reference is a second chain with the same sampling method, the same interval and the same rejections. But it has a constant likelihood. In this way, a cut or a rejection near the lower edge removes samples from both chains. It is not mistaken for a measurement.

\(F\) is the name given to the lowest tenth of the allowed interval: \(4\leq\log_{10}N_{\rm seed}\leq6.1\). For each chain, the fraction of samples that falls in \(F\) is turned into an effective count. To do so, the larger of two autocorrelation times is used. One is that of \(\log N_{\rm seed}\). The other is that of the indicator that equals one inside \(F\) and zero outside.

The autocorrelation time measures how long the samples stay tied to the previous ones. The effective counts take this tie into account. For the two fractions, one-sided Clopper-Pearson bounds at 97.5 per cent are calculated. The bound \(U\) is the upper bound of the fraction in the data chain. The bound \(L\) is the lower bound of the fraction in the reference chain. They are statistical bounds used to compare proportions. If the data chain does not visit \(F\), the value of \(U\) still stays above zero.

When effective counts are used, these bounds are approximate. They do not guarantee exact coverage for correlated samples. The rule is archived as a program (script). The program will be applied, not this paragraph.

Outcome not assessable

The rule cannot be applied if either of the two chains has fewer than 500 effective samples. Nor can it be applied if the reference chain has fewer than 50 effective visits to \(F\). The chains can be extended once, up to four times the initial length. If even then the amount of data remains insufficient, the check is inconclusive on that side. It is not an outcome in either direction.

First outcome

If \(U/L<0.1\), the result gives a ratio greater than ten to one against a coarse network. It is conventionally strong evidence. In this case the one-sided 95 per cent lower bound of \(N_{\rm seed}\) is reported as the result.

Second outcome

If the threshold is not reached, one does not conclude that the data say nothing about the lower part of the interval. The one-sided 95 per cent lower bounds are reported and compared anyway. They are those of the data chain and of the reference chain. But the test does not present a lower bound as its own result.

The upper side is also read separately, using the highest tenth. If the data indicate a coarse network, the next step would be to look for it in the matter power spectrum. The power spectrum is the way in which density variations change with size. To calculate it, coupled Boltzmann simulations would be needed. These simulations follow the cosmological components together.

Checks of the rule

Before archiving and again before the test, six synthetic cases with correlated chains are checked. The cases and the expected results are fixed and archived.

A constant likelihood produces the second outcome. A likelihood that vanishes below \(\log_{10}N_{\rm seed}=8\) produces the first. A chain with no samples in \(F\) and a chain that is too short both turn out not assessable. A posterior probability of 0.001 in \(F\) produces the first outcome. A probability of 0.02 moves the lower bound from 5.1 to 6.7. But it does not reach the threshold and produces the second outcome. All six cases pass the checks. If a repetition before the test did not pass them, the rule would not be applied.

Third outcome

The linearity test of the closure may exceed the 5 per cent tolerance even in a single row examined. In that case the branch cannot be certified with this method. The same holds if the emulator error on \(A\) exceeds one tenth of the standard deviation of the corresponding row.

In either of these cases, it is reported that the branch cannot be certified with this method. The two tolerances are not loosened to obtain an outcome.

What stays fixed

After the result has been seen, the initial interval is not changed. No data blocks are added or removed. The gate function, the vacuum fraction and the thresholds stay as declared here.

The weak reading, with \(\nu_v>0\), is a secondary analysis. It will be labelled as such. It will be added to the materials in a dated revision, whatever its outcome.

Two consequences already stated by the model

Independently of this test, the branching law imposes \(\bar w_D\geq-1\) at every epoch. This is established by formula S1 and by the averaging with any constant fraction of the vacuum. The model cannot enter the phantom regime. In that regime the ratio \(w\) falls below \(-1\).

A future release on baryon oscillations or on supernovae might establish a crossing of that threshold. It might do so with decisive certainty, according to the criteria declared by the release itself. A result of that kind would exclude this form of the model.

Moreover, in the strong reading Appendix T bounds \(p\), that is, the departure of the relational component from a cosmological constant, to a few hundredths. A larger measured departure would also exclude it, if it kept the pressure-to-density ratio above \(-1\), as S1 requires.

Glossary

Acceleration of the expansion. Increase over time of the speed at which cosmic distances grow. It is not a push of wind dragging the galaxies.

Acoustic scale. Characteristic size left by sound waves in the young universe. The summary data use it to study cosmic geometry.

Autocorrelation. Connection between a sample and the nearby ones in the same chain. If the chain has memory for many steps, nearby samples give less independent information.

BAO, or baryon acoustic oscillations. Trace left by ancient sound waves in the distribution of matter. Its size helps to measure cosmic distances.

Capacity. Total number of slots, including those inactive for the count being tracked. The connection between capacity and volume is an additional physical assumption.

Chi-square. Number that summarizes the differences between calculations and data, taking into account the uncertainties and their correlations. With the same procedure, a lower value indicates smaller overall differences.

Coherence cell. Region treated as a group when the fluctuations of the counts are calculated. The number of records in each cell helps to establish how much the noise matters.

Comoving. Following the mean expansion of the universe. A comoving region keeps its identity while its physical volume changes.

Count noise. Random fluctuation due to the finite number of records. In general, more records per cell mean smaller relative fluctuations.

Covariance. Table that summarizes the uncertainties and how much the errors of different measurements are related to one another. It is like a chart showing which marks tend to go up or down together.

Credibility at 95 per cent. Degree of compatibility assigned to the values after applying the model and the analysis. A 95 per cent bound is a boundary, not an exact measurement of the parameter.

Dark energy. Name given to the component used to describe the acceleration of cosmic expansion. It is not observed directly: its effects are studied.

Dark matter. Name given to the component used to describe some gravitational effects, for example in galaxies. It is a problem different from dark energy.

Dark sector. Part of the model that includes dark energy and any associated parameters.

Distal component. Component of the model tied to the links accumulated between epochs being compared. It does not indicate a greater energy due to the distance from the observer.

Dominant regime. Part of the evolution in which the most persistent growth mode prevails. It need not coincide with the entire observed interval.

Effective bridge. Set of assumptions and physical laws used to turn the counts of the model into observable quantities. It is not a fundamental derivation of all those laws.

Emulator. Approximate calculator that imitates a longer calculation after being checked. Here its uncertainties are included in the analysis.

Expansion rate. Measure of how quickly cosmic distances grow at a given moment. Having a large final growth does not say, by itself, how much time it took.

Fingerprint. Code calculated from a file to check whether it has been modified. It works like a short fingerprint of the content.

Fractionation. Attenuation of the comparison transmitted by a link. \(q\) measures the retained correlation; it is not the probability that a photon survives.

Gate function. Rule that describes when a component begins to contribute. In this test it can open at a different epoch and with a different speed.

Interposition. Insertion of a register between two others, so that the comparison passes through a new step.

Likelihood. It measures how well a model describes the data under a statistical procedure. The chain uses it to compare different choices of the parameters.

Matter power spectrum. Description of how large the variations in the density of matter are at different scales. In the future it could help in looking for the effects of a coarser network.

Memory-space extension. The separate candidate model of Appendices I-R: spatial registers and memories have distinct roles. Its reading channel \(r\) is not the original epoch channel \(q\).

Metric. Rule that makes it possible to measure distances and times. A length defined by the model of links is not automatically a relativistic metric.

Mpc, or megaparsec. Very large unit of astronomical distance. It serves to describe separations between cosmic regions, not everyday distances.

Negative pressure. Pressure that, in the adopted cosmological equations, can contribute to the acceleration of the expansion. It is not a wind that pushes the galaxies.

Parameter. Number that can be varied in a model. Changing a parameter can change the predictions.

Parameter kept fixed. Value retained from the previous analysis. It does not mean that it was derived from the theory or that the new test was blinded.

Posterior values. Values of the parameters regarded as compatible after considering the data and the initial assumptions. They are summarized, for example, by the median and by the bounds of a chain.

Preregistration. Description, archived before the calculation, of what will be done and of how the outcomes will be read. It helps to make visible the choices made in advance.

Pressure-to-density ratio. The ratio \(w=P/\rho\), where \(\rho\) denotes energy density. It expresses the contribution of pressure relative to density in the cosmological bridge.

Profile likelihood. Method that fixes a parameter and looks for the best possible chi-square by varying the others. It shows how the agreement changes for each fixed value.

Redshift. Relative increase of the wavelength of light during its journey. If the wavelength doubles, \(z=1\) and the stretching factor is \(1+z\).

Redundancy. Outcome that, in channel B, does not increase the active total. Its probability \(p\) is different from the share of correlation \(q\).

Register. Element capable of carrying a distinguishable state, like one of the two signs on a square. It requires neither a conscious memory nor a human observer.

Relational link. Connection that relates distinguishable states, like two different signs on the blackboard. It is the starting element proposed by MM.

Sampled chain. Sequence of parameter values used to explore which choices agree with the data. It is like checking many possible combinations, instead of trying only one.

Scale factor. Number \(a\) that describes how distances change between references that follow the expansion. Their distances in comoving coordinates remain constant. In three dimensions, volume grows as the cube of \(a\).

Slot. Place in which the model can record a state. It can be active or inactive for the count considered.

Standard deviation. It measures how much the values are spread around a mean or an estimate. The more standard deviations separate two values, the greater their distance compared with that spread.

Type Ia supernova. Stellar explosion whose light, after corrections and standardization, helps to estimate distances. Supernovae are not all identical light bulbs.

Vacuum residue. Constant part of the energy density kept distinct from the relational component. In this work its value remains unexplained.
