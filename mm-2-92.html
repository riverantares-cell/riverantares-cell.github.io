Before we start: the words you need

What MM is. Mathematical Materiality, MM for short, is the author's framework of ideas. Its proposal is this. Matter is not described by mathematics. Matter is made of mathematical relations. A relation links two distinct terms. For example «twice» links 3 to 6. In MM the things of the world are relations of this kind. They are not objects with a formula written inside them.

The words that belong to MM.

  • A premise is a starting sentence of the author. It is a fixed point, like a postulate.
  • A reading is an interpretation. It is written so that it can be tested. In the text we say that a reading «holds» or «falls». It is the reading that falls, not the whole framework.
  • The nexus is the starting link. In MM the first nexus is the one between space and time.
  • The GCD, in school mathematics, is the greatest common divisor. It is the largest number that divides two numbers with no remainder. In the vocabulary of MM it means instead a unit used for counting. The multiples of this unit come one after another: they appear as time. The fractions stand side by side: they appear as space.
  • The non-local phase is a part of the nexus. It is in no place and in no instant. And it is present in every place and in every instant.
  • The mirror is a sector of matter separate from ours. It is normal in its laws, which are its own. Its constants are read relative to ours.
  • Registered means written, dated and fixed before the calculation. Or before the measurement exists. That way it cannot be changed afterwards.
  • A SHA-256 hash is a code computed from a file. If the file changes even by a comma, the code changes. It serves to show that a text was already written on that date.

Occam's razor. It is a criterion of choice, not a proof. It says this: between two explanations that hold with the data, we prefer the one that asks for less. Mind the condition: that hold with the data. A simpler explanation contradicted by the data does not win.

Entities and parameters. A new entity is something a theory adds to the world. For example a particle never seen, a field, an extra dimension. A free parameter is a number tuned by hand. It serves to make the numbers come out right. The more entities and parameters are needed, the more the explanation costs under the razor.

σ, the distance from the data. Every measurement has an error. It is written with the ± sign. The letter σ is read sigma. It is the typical size of that error. Saying that a number is at 0.4σ means one thing: it is four tenths of its error away from the measured value. It is very close. In this text, as in the archived work, this scale holds. Below 2σ the number holds. Between 2σ and 3σ it is in tension. Above 3σ the reading that gives it falls.

χ², AIC, BIC. The χ² is read chi squared. It measures the overall mismatch between a model and a set of data. The lower it is, the better the model follows the data. Δχ² is the difference between two models on the same data. The AIC and the BIC take the χ² and add a penalty. The penalty applies to every free parameter. That way a model is punished if it follows the data well only because it has many knobs to turn. The AIC adds 2 points per parameter. The BIC adds more when the data are many. The lowest value wins.

The Bayes factor. It says how many times the data make one hypothesis more credible than another. Here is an example. A bet was even. Then the data arrive and make it 15 to 1. The Bayes factor is 15. The value 1 means no preference. From 3 to 20 it is a positive preference. Above 20 it is a strong preference.

The quantities of cosmology.

  • c is the speed of light.
  • H₀ is read H-zero. It is the rate at which the universe is expanding today.
  • a₀ is read a-zero. It is a very small acceleration. Below it the stars of galaxies turn too fast. Faster than the matter we see would allow.
  • ΛCDM is read lambda-CDM. It is standard cosmology: cold dark matter plus a cosmological constant. The cosmological constant is dark energy held fixed. CPL is its variant. There dark energy changes in time, with two extra parameters.
  • Ω_c/Ω_b is a ratio. It says how much dark matter there is, relative to ordinary matter.
  • BAO are the baryon acoustic oscillations. They are a «line» printed in the distribution of galaxies. It serves to measure distances. The DESI instrument measures them.
  • Cosmic background, or CMB, is the light left over from the first 380,000 years of the universe. Planck, ACT and SPT are the instruments that measured it. P-ACT-LB2 and CMB-SPA are two combinations of these data.

The quantities of particle physics.

  • α_s is read alpha-s. It is the strength of the strong force, the one that holds nuclei together.
  • CKM is a table. It says how quarks mix with one another. The «deficit» is a small shortfall. It is in a sum that should give 1.
  • R is a ratio between two forces. It takes the inverses of the strengths of the strong force and the weak force. It carries them to the Planck scale. It is worth about 1.076. It is not an invented number: it is built from the measured values.
  • δ is read delta. It is the small relative difference between the two forces: δ = (R − 1)/(R + 1), that is 0.036655.
  • Planck scale. It is a very high energy. Up there gravity becomes important. No accelerator reaches it.

Prediction and out-of-sample check. A prediction is a number written before the measurement exists. An out-of-sample check is another thing. It is a number chosen by looking at certain data. Then it is tested on other data, which were not used to choose it. The first is worth more than the second. Both are worth more than a form chosen by looking at exactly the data it has to explain.

Look-elsewhere effect. Imagine looking for a formula that holds, and trying a hundred of them. It is likely that one holds by chance. In particle physics this is called the look-elsewhere effect. In the text we say «count of coincidences».

1. The question and the criterion

The author asked for a detailed comparison. He wanted MM set beside the alternative theories, using Occam's razor. Before counting, though, two things must be said. Whom MM is compared with. And on what.

MM is not the explanation of a single phenomenon. From the same matrix it reads many things at once. Quantum behaviour. The time of moving clocks. Gravity. The galaxy scale. The ratio between dark and ordinary matter. Cosmic acceleration. The first minutes of the universe. The structure of the Standard Model. Some numbers of particle physics. The lifetime of the neutron. And galaxy clusters.

For this reason the author fixed the criterion like this. Only frameworks that also hold on several planes at once count as alternatives: cosmological and particle planes. An explanation of a single theme may be excellent. But it is not an alternative to a comprehensive explanation: it is a competitor on that theme.

The criterion has a consequence that must be said at once. Take the interpretations of quantum mechanics, or MOND on galaxies alone. The comparison with MM would almost always end in a draw. On those planes MM recovers what those theories already say. The interesting comparison is another one: who explains more, with less, on the themes physics has not closed today.

There is also a second point. The author raised it while the comparison was taking shape. The list of themes must not contain only those MM treats. That would be like judging a runner on a track chosen by him.

For this reason the themes are eighteen. Eleven are the planes of MM. The other seven are themes MM does not treat, and that some rival does treat. They are the primordial spectrum, proton decay, the matter-antimatter asymmetry, the strong-force angle θ, the hierarchy of masses, the origin of neutrino masses and black-hole entropy. On those seven MM scores zero. And it scores zero in front of everyone.

2. Occam's razor, in three forms

William of Occam lived in the fourteenth century. He wrote that entities should not be multiplied without necessity. In physics the principle means this. Between two explanations that hold with the data, we prefer the one that asks for less. The initial condition matters: the razor applies only between explanations that hold. A simpler theory contradicted by the data does not win.

An everyday example helps. The light in your house goes out together with the neighbours'. First explanation: «the power went out in the neighbourhood». Second explanation: «three bulbs burnt out at the same instant». Both explain the dark. But the first asks for one single cause for three facts. The razor does not say the second is impossible. It says that, until there is a reason, you bet on the first. And the bet is settled only when you look at the meter.

The razor can be used in three ways. In this work the three ways go side by side.

The count. For each framework four things are counted. The new entities: particles, fields, hidden sectors, extra dimensions. The free parameters, that is, the numbers tuned to make the data fit. The choices made after seeing the data. And the themes explained. A form chosen among many costs like a parameter. And it costs more if the attempts were many. A framework that explains more with the same choices is worth more.

The information criteria. Sometimes two models are compared on the same data. Then the AIC and the BIC put the count into numbers. They take the χ² mismatch and add a penalty for each parameter. The AIC penalises each parameter by 2. The BIC penalises it by the logarithm of the number of data. So the BIC weighs a parameter more heavily when the data are many.

The Bayesian form. A model with a free parameter can adapt to many different data. For this reason every single datum rewards it little. A model that fixes that parameter at a precise value risks more. If the datum falls there, it gains a lot. It is like the difference between two bets: «a number between 1 and 30 comes up», or «17 comes up».

The Bayes factor measures this gain. Here the fixed parameter is the ratio 3√3. The calculation uses the Savage-Dickey ratio. You look at how high, at that point, the distribution of the parameter is after the data. And you compare it with how high it was before.

Alongside the three ways there is a caution. It is in the method of MM, and it is rule 4: count the coincidences. If a form was chosen knowing the datum, two questions must be asked. How many similar forms could have been written? And with what probability does one hold by chance? It is the same correction that particle physics calls the «look-elsewhere effect».

3. The frameworks and the themes

Eight rivals enter, all on several planes.

  1. The standard set: the Standard Model of particles, ΛCDM cosmology, general relativity.
  2. Asymptotic safety with Higgs inflation and the νMSM. They are a single research programme, by Shaposhnikov and collaborators. Gravity is made consistent at high energies. The Higgs acts as the engine of inflation. Three right-handed neutrinos give dark matter and the asymmetry.
  3. Noncommutative geometry by Connes and Chamseddine. Spacetime has a small finite internal part. From there comes the group of forces.
  4. Grand unification with supersymmetry. The three forces are united at high energy. Every particle has a partner.
  5. SMASH (Ballesteros, Redondo, Ringwald and Tamarit 2016). It is the Standard Model with an axion, heavy neutrinos and inflation: «five problems at one stroke».
  6. Mirror matter. A full copy of the Standard Model. It interacts with ours almost only through gravity.
  7. The axion. A particle that explains why the strong-force angle θ is almost zero. It can also make the dark matter.
  8. String theory.

Left out are the explanations of a single theme. They are Verlinde's emergent gravity, MOND, superfluid dark matter, asymmetric dark matter, the dark decay of the neutron, the interpretations of quantum mechanics. They are cited where they give the same number as MM, or where they touch the same theme: there the comparison is direct.

Three of these were born to explain one phenomenon, but they also touch a second plane. They are superfluid dark matter, asymmetric dark matter and the dark decay of the neutron. They are read in section 7.9, with the same rules. Each one takes from the data the number of the MM theme it touches: a₀, the ratio exchanged for a mass, the 1 % fraction of the neutron.

4. The rules of the count

Every cell of the complete table carries a code. The table is in appendix A. These are the codes.

  • Np: own number written before the measurement, and later confirmed.
  • Nd: own number that holds today, with the form chosen knowing the datum. Or a relation already given by others.
  • R: own rule or reading that holds with the data, with declared parameters or choices.
  • S: structure derived, such as the group of forces or the charges. Or a problem explained without a measured number.
  • T: theoretical result, without a measurement to compare with.
  • N−: own number in tension or excluded. Or an expectation of the framework not confirmed.
  • =: compatible with the data, but with no own number. The framework coincides with known physics, or adopts it.
  • P: free parameter tuned to the data.
  • —: theme not treated.

«Holds» means that today's data do not contradict it: it is within 2σ. «Stands» is said of a rule with declared parameters or choices, which the data do not contradict.

The entries «=» and «P» do not count as own content. Whoever coincides with known physics adds nothing. Whoever tunes a parameter to the datum does not explain the datum.

A rule with parameters counts as «R» under two conditions. It must describe more data than it has parameters. And the formal criteria must reward it. That is the case of the cosmic clock: it has one parameter more than ΛCDM, and repays it with the AIC in three supernova samples. A parameter counts as «P» when it reproduces only the datum it is tuned on. For example a dark ratio exchanged for a mass. The distinction is the same for all frameworks.

The result depends on the way of counting. To see how much, the count is redone with five different rules. They are written in the program calcoli/tabella_temi.py. They were written when the entries of the table were already known: they are not registered.

  • R1 counts numbers, rules and structures that hold.
  • R2 counts only the numbers.
  • R3 removes the numbers that others had already given before.
  • R4 counts only the numbers written before the measurement and later confirmed.
  • R5 adds to R4 the contents confirmed on data not used to choose them.

The count is made with two combinations of cosmic background data. The same one is used for all frameworks. They are P-ACT-LB2, the one used for 3√3, and Planck 2018 alone. Section 11 gives the outcome.

5. Five structural mysteries, one single ontology

Occam's razor, in its oldest form, counts entities. There are five points where physics works with great precision. But it does not say what things are. These are them. Why matter behaves probabilistically. What entanglement is. Why there is a speed limit. What dark matter is. What dark energy is.

Standard physics keeps the first three as starting principles. They are the Born rule, the quantum mechanics that describes entanglement, the constancy of the speed of light. For the last two it adds entities it does not explain: a particle not yet found and a cosmological constant.

MM proposes one single ontological explanation for all five. «Ontological» means: what things are, not only how they behave.

Matter is made of mathematical relations between distinct terms. The first nexus is the one between space and time. In it there is a non-local phase. It is in no place and in no instant, and it is present in every place and in every instant.

The local world is this same nexus, counted through its greatest common divisor (GCD). The multiples follow one another: they appear as time. The fractions stand side by side: they appear as space (MM 2.27, 2.72, 2.88). That the first nexus is the one between space and time remains the premise of MM. From here come the five readings that follow.

5.1 The non-local phase and entanglement

What MM says. «Non-local» does not mean that something acts at a distance. The non-local phase is nowhere, and it is present everywhere (MM 2.72). Entanglement is its empirical manifestation. Two distant systems remain correlated more than any other explanation allows. No explanation made of separate properties is enough, if influences do not go faster than light. And meanwhile nothing travels from one to the other.

What holds with the data. The violation of Bell's inequalities, measured in many experiments. And the ceiling of correlations, obtained with the Born reading at the two ends: 2√2, as in quantum mechanics, against 2.82759 ± 0.00051 measured (MM 2.48.2). The experiments exclude Bell's assumptions. They do not, however, choose MM among the interpretations. The singlet curve, −cos θ, is obtained in MM only with imported ingredients (MM 2.48.2).

What the rivals say. In the sources read, none of the eight frameworks on several planes puts a non-local phase at the foundation. They all take quantum mechanics, with its entanglement, as their starting point.

Two programmes go in a nearby direction. They start, however, from entanglement as a given. The first is in string theory, with the ER = EPR conjecture (Maldacena and Susskind 2013). It proposes that two entangled black holes are connected by a spacetime bridge. The bridge is created by the correlations between their microstates. So entanglement and geometry would be two descriptions of the same thing. The authors specify that the bridge does not allow signals to be sent.

The second one is outside the criterion: it is Verlinde (2016). There spacetime and gravity emerge from the entanglement of microscopic degrees of freedom, with long-range entanglement at the Hubble scale. Among the interpretations of quantum mechanics, Bohmian mechanics has an explicitly non-local dynamics. This comes from common knowledge, not from a rereading of the source.

The difference is in the direction. These programmes derive space from entanglement. MM derives entanglement and space from the same non-local phase. And with the same reading it reaches the numbers of dark matter and of particle physics.

5.2 Quantum behaviour

What MM says. Take an observed object and whoever reads it, that is, the physical system observing it. When the mass of the object is small compared with that of the reader, something happens. The GCD of the point of observation and that of the diffraction generating the object become equal. And they reflect each other as in a mirror.

Then the object no longer occupies a single point of the observer's spacetime. It spreads into copies. The copies add like waves and can cancel out. This is the reason for probabilistic behaviour. It is not the probability of a hidden die: they are copies with a phase.

What holds with the data. Interference. The Born rule from counting the copies, with four declared assumptions and Feynman's checkerboard as a postulate (MM 2.48.2). The Tsirelson ceiling.

And there is a reason for an observed fact. The experiments find no mass above which superposition stops. For MM a single value might not exist. The threshold is in fact a ratio: between the mass of the object and that of the reader (MM 2.66.1, predictions registered by 2035).

What the rivals say. They all assume quantum mechanics. There the Born rule is a postulate. Outside the criterion, Penrose and Diósi tie the end of superposition to the mass of the object alone and to its distribution.

5.3 The speed limit of light

What MM says. The speed of light is a fixed exchange rate. It links the multiples and the fractions of the same GCD. It says how much space one step of time is worth. Exceeding it would mean covering space without counting time, that is, leaving the local world.

Inside the system it is an absolute assumption, the same for everyone. Outside the system there is no other term to relate it to. So there is nothing to justify.

What holds with the data. What holds for relativity. Einstein's factor comes from counting events. It is the ratio between the geometric mean and the arithmetic mean of the two light legs (MM 2.40.2). And the speed of gravitational waves is c, as measured with GW170817 (MM 2.54).

What the rivals say. Relativity takes the constancy of the speed of light as a starting principle. All eight frameworks inherit it. MM does not derive the value of c: it gives the reason for it.

5.4 Dark matter

What MM says. It is not a new particle with fixed properties. It is the ordinary matter of a «mirror». It is normal in its laws, with constants read relative to ours (MM 2.44).

What holds with the data. The ratio between dark and ordinary matter, 3√3. And the galaxy scale, a₀ = cH₀/6. The two numbers are read with the same factor per direction (sections 6.1 and 6.2).

What the rivals say. A particle: superpartner, sterile neutrino, axion, mirror baryons. The ratio remains a parameter. None of those particles has been found.

The price of MM is declared. There is a postulate: the radiation of the mirror does not gravitate. And a covariant model is missing (MM 2.44).

5.5 Dark energy

What MM says. No substance. The acceleration of the universe is a clock reading. It is the clock of an observer living inside structures that grow (MM 2.50).

What holds with the data. A mismatch with DESI, supernovae and the cosmic background lower than ΛCDM. An effective equation of state that crosses −1, as DESI prefers. And a growth of structures that is compatible (section 6.3).

What the rivals say. The standard set puts in a cosmological constant whose value it does not explain. String theory makes an anthropic choice among very many vacua. The CPL model uses a dark energy that changes, described with two extra parameters.

The price of MM is declared. The law linking growth and clock is assumed. And its exponent is tuned.

7.2 Asymptotic safety, Higgs inflation and the νMSM

What holds. Here is the strongest number among those of the rivals. In December 2009 Shaposhnikov and Wetterich write a prediction. If gravity is asymptotically safe, and if its contributions have the right signs, the Higgs mass is 126 GeV «with only a few GeV of uncertainty».

The 2012 measurement gives 125 GeV. With today's top mass, 172.56 GeV (PDG 2025, with α_s = 0.1180), their formula gives 128.7 GeV. The theoretical uncertainty is ± 2.2 GeV, and the distance is 1.6σ. The number holds within the uncertainty written back then. And with no new particles.

The same programme also does inflation, with the Higgs as the engine (Bezrukov and Shaposhnikov 2008). It gives a primordial spectral index n ≃ 0.966 for 60 e-folds, rounded to 0.97 in the paper. And it gives r ≃ 0.0033. One single coupling is needed, ξ ≃ 49,000 √λ, fixed by the amplitude of the fluctuations. The authors write: «no new particles besides those of the electroweak theory».

What it asks for. For the Higgs, an assumption about gravity at high energies, that is, the signs of the anomalous dimensions. For inflation, a coupling fixed on a datum. The νMSM adds three right-handed neutrinos and 18 new parameters, to explain dark matter and the asymmetry.

What does not hold, or depends on the data. The condition the Higgs mass comes from is λ = 0 at the Planck scale. That condition is 3.2σ away from the value λ(M_P) = −0.0100 ± 0.0031, carried to Planck with today's data (2.54).

The spectral index holds with Planck 2018, which gives 0.965 ± 0.004. It is instead in tension, about −2.9σ, with P-ACT-LB2, which gives 0.975 ± 0.003 (PDG 2025, table 25.1). P-ACT-LB2 is exactly the combination with which 3√3 holds. The 0.966 is the value for 60 e-folds, at the scale of the paper. With fewer e-folds, as at the PDG scale, the index goes down and the tension grows.

Already allowed, awaiting. Suppose sterile neutrinos make all the dark matter. Then the lightest neutrino weighs less than 10⁻⁵ eV, and the sum of the masses is at the minimum. Today this number has the same status as MM's 0.0593 eV: it is allowed by the limits that allow normal ordering. The value r ≃ 0.0033 is below today's sensitivity.

Under the razor. It is the most economical rival per added entity. It has a number written beforehand and confirmed, the Higgs. And, with Planck 2018, it has a second one, the spectrum. All this with no new particles. The spectral index was written after the first WMAP measurements, and before the precise ones from Planck. On galaxies, the dark ratio, the neutron and clusters it says nothing.

7.3 Noncommutative geometry

What holds. A strong economy on structure. Chamseddine and Connes (2008) propose a «purely gravitational» explanation of the group of forces U(1) × SU(2) × SU(3), and of the particle representations. Spacetime is the product of a four-dimensional continuum by a finite internal space. The number of families remains an input.

What did not hold. The model predicted a Higgs «around 170 GeV» (Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli 2007), between 160 and 180 GeV. Then the measurement of 125 GeV arrived. In 2012 the authors showed a way out: a scalar field σ, already present in the action and previously neglected, brings the Higgs back to 125 GeV.

Under the razor. Few entities. A derived structure. And a number that went badly, then was made compatible.

7.4 Grand unification with supersymmetry

What holds. Charge quantisation. And a whole family in a single object, the 16 of SO(10). With supersymmetry the three forces unite at about 2·10¹⁶ GeV.

The early works predicted the weak angle, sin²θ_W, starting from the other forces (PDG 2025, § 92.5). The dates of the original works are not rechecked here. Written as α_s, the leading-order prediction with today's inputs is 0.117. The measured value is 0.1180 ± 0.0009 (equation 92.8).

What it asks for. A unified group. And a partner for every particle. In the minimal supersymmetric model the new free parameters are 105 (PDG 2025). Not all of them enter the unification relation.

What did not hold. The PDG adds a caution: the near perfection at leading order is «to some extent accidental». With two-loop corrections and superparticles at 1 TeV the estimate is about 0.126, which deviates. The agreement is recovered with corrections at the unification scale.

Then there are the searches. None has shown a significant excess of superparticles. And the proton has not been seen to decay: the limit is τ/Br(p → π⁰e⁺) > 2.4·10³⁴ years. The minimal supersymmetric SU(5) model is «under significant tension».

Under the razor. Two themes: structure and weak angle. For a very high price in entities and parameters.

7.5 SMASH

What holds. It is the broadest rival for themes touched. Ballesteros, Redondo, Ringwald and Tamarit (2016) add to the Standard Model three right-handed neutrinos, a new quark and a field σ. The new scale is a single one, about 10¹¹ GeV. In this way they solve «five problems at one stroke»: inflation, dark matter with the axion, neutrino masses, the matter-antimatter asymmetry, the angle θ.

What it asks for. Almost everything with parameters. Dark matter holds if the axion mass is between 50 and 200 μeV. Neutrino masses come with the seesaw. The asymmetry comes with leptogenesis.

What depends on the data. The predicted primordial spectral index is between 0.960 and 0.964, at the scale used by the PDG. It holds with Planck 2018, which gives 0.965 ± 0.004. With P-ACT-LB2, which gives 0.975 ± 0.003, it is between 3.7 and 5.0σ below the datum. P-ACT-LB2 is the combination used for 3√3.

Awaiting. A value r ≳ 0.004, within reach of LiteBIRD and CMB-S4. An axion mass around 100 μeV. And ΔN_eff ≈ 0.03. These numbers await the measurements, like the registered ones of MM.

Under the razor. It is the most economical among the frameworks with new particles, for breadth. On MM's own themes it says nothing new: galaxies, the dark ratio, cosmic acceleration, the neutron, clusters.

7.6 Mirror matter

What holds. It explains why dark matter and ordinary matter have similar densities. Through leptogenesis the mirror world receives more baryons than ours. A ratio of «about 5 or so» can make the dark matter (Berezhiani 2004). The 2026 PSI paper writes it as Ω_B′/Ω_B ≃ 5. The number, though, is not fixed: it is a plausible parameter.

What it asks for. It doubles the whole Standard Model. And the mirror world must be colder than ours: T′/T < 0.64, from the first minutes.

What did not hold. It had an explanation of the two lifetimes of the neutron: the n–n′ oscillation (Berezhiani 2019). It was excluded at Oak Ridge in 2022. At PSI in 2026 the other proposed signals were excluded too, at 95 % confidence, in 99.98 % of the possible directions.

Under the razor. Many entities. And an explanation with no number on the dark ratio. It is also the closest rival to MM in idea: MM too reads dark matter as the ordinary matter of a «mirror» (2.44). The differences are two. MM fixes the ratio, 3√3, and mirror matter does not. And MM does not require the mirror to be a copy of our particles with their masses.

7.7 The axion

What holds. It explains why the strong-force angle θ is below 10⁻¹⁰. That limit comes from the electric dipole moment of the neutron. The axion can also make the cold dark matter: it «may well consist of axions» (PDG 2025).

What it asks for. A particle and its scale. Awaiting: direct detection. Today the searches give limits.

7.8 String theory

What holds. The richest structure. For a class of extremal black holes in five dimensions it derives their entropy by counting states (Strominger and Vafa 1996). It is a theoretical result, without a measurement to compare with.

What it asks for. Extra dimensions. And a landscape of vacua «unimaginably large and diverse» (Susskind 2003). The cosmological constant stays small for anthropic reasons, with about a hundred fluxes (Bousso and Polchinski 2000).

Under the razor. No unique number on the measured themes.

7.9 Outside the criterion, on the same numbers as MM

  • Verlinde (2016) gives a₀ = cH₀/6 with no tuned parameter, written before MM. For the dark ratio his form coincides with 3√3 only if Ω_b = 4/81. He does not treat particle physics.
  • MOND has a₀ free.
  • Superfluid dark matter (Berezhiani and Khoury 2015) has three parameters. And a₀ enters it as a datum.
  • Asymmetric dark matter (Kaplan, Luty and Zurek 2009) explains why the two densities are similar. It exchanges the ratio for a mass, between 5 and 15 GeV.
  • The dark decay of the neutron (Fornal and Grinstein 2018) takes the 1 % fraction from the datum.
  • Timescape by Wiltshire reads cosmic acceleration as an effect of clocks, in an inhomogeneous universe. It is the closest precedent of the reading of 2.50.

On the galaxy scale Verlinde is as economical as MM. But a rival that coincides on one number does not take away the breadth. Verlinde says nothing about the neutron, about particle physics, about clusters.

10. The count of coincidences

Rule 4 of the MM method asks two things. How many similar formulas could have been written? And with what probability does one hold by chance?

The calculation is rough, and it is in the program calcoli/coincidenze_estese.py. Every target is treated as if it fell at random in a window of plausible values. The percentages depend on the windows and on the 1σ threshold, which is narrower than the 2σ used as the agreement zone. They are worth as an order of magnitude.

The ratio 3√3. Suppose it had been the only number fixed beforehand. The probability of falling by chance within 1σ of the P-ACT value would be about 5 %. Counting the forms that can be built with the same √3 it rises to 11 %. With a broad family of simple numbers, to 36 %. The catalogue of 2.54 finds 8 simple constants out of 118 within 1σ of the ACT value, and 26/5 is closer than 3√3. On its own, it is a moderate clue.

The scale a₀. With a 20 % error, four simple fractions of cH₀ fall within 1σ: 1/5, 1/6, 1/2π, 1/2e. The family covers almost the whole window. On its own it is weak: the strength of a₀ does not lie in the single agreement.

The link. The same factor ℓ must do two things. It must give a₀ = (cH₀/2)/ℓ². And it must give the ratio ℓ³. The scale a₀ allows ℓ between 1.52 and 1.86. The ratio instead wants ℓ between 1.728 and 1.737.

Take an ℓ at random among those allowed by a₀: it also gives the ratio in 2.6 % of cases. Now take fifteen simple numbers allowed by a₀: √3, the golden ratio, 5/3, 7/4, √e, π/2, √π and others. The probability that at least one also gives the ratio is about one third. √3 does it, and no other.

There is also the baryon fraction in clusters: 1/(1 + 3√3) = 0.161, against 0.141-0.165 in X-COP. It is not one more agreement: it is the same relation rewritten (2.54).

The neutron difference. With a 23 % error, among nine simple forms of δ two fall within 1σ: δ/3 and δ/4. On its own the agreement is weak. It would be about 17 % if it had been the only number fixed beforehand, and 70 % for the family.

2.84 makes the estimate with a different window, uniform in the logarithm between 0.1 and 10 %. It gives about one time in ten, and one in four. The broad catalogue of 2.54 counts 7 formulas out of 70 compatible with the data. The same δ gives the CKM deficit at 1.5σ, which on its own is a weak agreement.

The strong coupling and the Higgs. The estimates are already in the works. For α_s: 0 hits out of 28 mechanisms registered beforehand, with 0.22 expected by chance. The relation that stands was chosen afterwards, among 14 averages: it would hold by chance about one time in three. For λ(M_P): three forms out of twelve, that is, by chance more than one time in two.

All together. Now let us put together the three own numbers with the form chosen on the datum: ratio, a₀, neutron. The probability that they all hold by chance goes from about 1 in 340 to about 1 in 4. The first value holds if each one had been the only one fixed beforehand. With the narrow families it comes out about 1 in 14, with the broad ones 1 in 4.

The link between a₀ and the ratio is not multiplied again, because it is not independent of the ratio. And it must be said that these products assume independent numbers, and windows chosen by us. They show how much the calculation depends on those choices. They are not the probability that MM is a coincidence.

The rivals. Numbers written before the measurement do not have this cost. A prediction is a single form. This holds for the Higgs mass at 126 GeV with a few GeV of uncertainty, and for the weak angle of grand unification.

What follows. The weight of the links lies in the economy of the inputs: one single ℓ, one single R. That is what the razor's count rewards.

Counted as coincidences, MM's numbers weigh little, taken one at a time with their family. And the link between a₀ and 3√3 would hold by chance about one time in three, among fifteen simple numbers. The decisive weight will come from the registered tests, which are predictions with a single form.

13. What the razor says, all told

On the five structural mysteries MM gives one single ontological explanation. Quantum behaviour, entanglement, the speed limit, dark matter and dark energy become aspects of one single structure of relations. Inside it there is a non-local phase, present everywhere. Standard physics instead keeps the first three as postulates, and the two entities without explanation. In the sources read, no rival puts non-locality at the foundation (section 5).

On the open themes MM is the framework that covers the most themes with own content that holds today, among those read. They are four numbers and two rules: six themes out of eighteen with P-ACT, five with Planck 2018 alone. The best rivals reach two. The price is declared in full (section 8). And it is paid with no new particles with fixed properties.

Two links cross the planes, and no rival read has them. The same √3 gives the galaxy scale and the dark ratio. The same R gives the neutron, quark mixing and the Higgs self-interaction. Their weight lies in the economy of the inputs; counted as coincidences they weigh little.

The formal criteria are in favour of MM where they can be computed. The fixed ratio is one parameter fewer, with the same quality: it holds with DESI and deuterium, with Planck and DESI, and with P-ACT. Its Bayes factor is about 15, and about 5 taking into account the choice of the form; with Planck 2018 alone it is in slight tension. The cosmic clock is one parameter more, repaid with the AIC in three samples, and with the BIC positively with Dovekie; against CPL it is repaid in all of them.

Two checks on data not used to choose. The cluster rule holds on the lensing of 70 clusters. On the 42 groups of Sun it predicts about three quarters of the mass. And 3√3 is consistent with DESI and deuterium, in a check that is not stringent.

On single numbers some rivals are more economical or further ahead. Asymptotic safety for the Higgs mass, with no new entities and written beforehand; on the condition λ = 0 at Planck, however, MM's form is closer to the datum. Grand unification for the weak angle, written beforehand. And Verlinde for a₀, outside the criterion.

The count holds when the rules change in three cases out of five, with both cosmic background data sets; in four with P-ACT. Counting only the numbers written beforehand, the rivals are ahead by one.

The razor does not validate, and does not prove uniqueness. It says which explanation to prefer today, while waiting for the measurements. The measurements that decide are registered. And the first one that can shift the whole comparison is BL3.

14. What this work does not claim

  • It does not claim that the ontological explanation of section 5 is proved. It rests on the premise of the first nexus between space and time. And where it touches the data, these also confirm known physics.
  • It does not claim that MM is validated. It claims that today, on the open themes and among the frameworks read, it is the explanation that asks for least for what it explains.
  • It does not claim that MM's numbers are derived without choices. The forms of a₀, 3√3, δ/3, λ(M_P) and α_s were chosen knowing the data. And this is said theme by theme.
  • It does not claim that no other framework, beyond those read, could do more. It claims that among the frameworks on several planes read at the sources none does. And the comparison can be repeated with the programs of the package, adding rows.
  • It does not claim that the Bayes factors and the coincidence probabilities are exact measures. They depend on the prior windows, which are declared.
  • It does not claim that the five counting rules were registered. They were written when the entries of the table were known. They serve to show how much the result depends on the way of counting.
  • It does not claim that the difference between the two lifetimes of the neutron is physical. That is what BL3 will decide.

Glossary

  • a₀: the acceleration below which the stars of galaxies turn faster than the visible matter would allow.
  • AIC and BIC: the χ² mismatch with a penalty for each free parameter. The penalty is 2 per parameter in the AIC, and the logarithm of the number of data in the BIC. The lowest value wins. For the BIC a difference below 2 is a weak clue, between 2 and 6 a positive clue, above 6 a strong preference: it is the Kass and Raftery scale. For the AIC the same scale is only an indication.
  • α_s: the strength of the strong force. The lattice value is α_s computed on a computer from the theory of strong forces; here it is the one from ALPHA 2026.
  • BAO: the baryon acoustic oscillations. They are a «line» printed in the distribution of galaxies, and they serve to measure distances. DESI measures them.
  • Bayes factor: how much the data make one hypothesis more credible than another. 1 means no preference; from 3 to 20 a positive preference; above 20 a strong one.
  • c: the speed of light.
  • χ² (chi squared): the overall mismatch between a model and a set of data. Lower is better. Δχ² is the difference between two models on the same data.
  • CKM: the table that says how quarks mix. The «deficit» is a small shortfall in a sum that should give 1.
  • Cosmic background (CMB): the light left over from the first 380,000 years.
  • δ = (R − 1)/(R + 1): the small relative difference between the two forces of the ratio R.
  • GCD: in school mathematics, the greatest common divisor. In MM, the unit used for counting: the multiples appear as time, the fractions as space.
  • h, ω_b, ω_c, Ω_m: H₀ in units of 100 km/s/Mpc; the density of ordinary matter (baryons) and of dark matter; the matter fraction.
  • H₀: the expansion rate of the universe today.
  • ΛCDM: standard cosmology, with cold dark matter and a cosmological constant. CPL: its variant, with a dark energy that changes in time, described by two extra parameters, w₀ and wₐ.
  • Mirror: in MM, a sector of matter separate from ours, normal in its laws, with constants read relative to ours.
  • Nexus: in MM, the starting link. The first nexus is the one between space and time.
  • Non-local phase: in MM, the part of the nexus that is in no place and in no instant. And it is present in every place and in every instant.
  • Occam's razor: between two explanations that hold with the data, we prefer the one that asks for less.
  • Premise: a starting sentence of the author, a fixed point like a postulate.
  • R = (1/α₃)/(1/α₂): the ratio between the inverses of the strengths of the strong and weak forces. They are carried to the Planck scale with the Standard Model equations, and the ratio is about 1.076: up there the strong force is a little weaker than the weak one. It is a quantity built from the measured values.
  • r: the ratio between primordial gravitational waves and fluctuations. n_s: the primordial spectral index.
  • Reading: in MM, an interpretation written so that it can be tested. A reading holds or falls; the whole framework does not fall.
  • Registered: written, dated and fixed with a hash before the calculation, or before the measurement exists.
  • σ (sigma): how far a number is from the datum, in units of the measurement error. Below 2 the number holds; between 2 and 3 it is in tension; above 3 the reading that gives it falls.
  • σ8: how lumpy matter is today.
  • Σm_ν: the sum of the masses of the three neutrinos.
