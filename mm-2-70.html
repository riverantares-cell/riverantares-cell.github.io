Abstract

This record gives the dynamics of Mathematical Materiality (MM). In the weak field it is linearised Einstein gravity, and the homogeneous model is Friedmann's. The junction to the a₀ = cH₀/6 layer is read as a boundary set by the Galaxy (the rule L*), and a premise of the author gives that boundary with two declared choices. The record also registers, before Gaia DR4 (planned for 2 December 2026), a test of this reading on wide binaries, with rules fixed before the data. It is one of MM's checks: the outcome concerns one reading of MM, not the framework. The dynamics: The prediction. In MM's reading L*, a pair of stars reads only its own field inside a boundary, where its own Newtonian field exceeds the Galactic one (7 773 AU for 1.5 solar masses near the Sun). The MOND-like boost is then the same in every direction. QUMOND with the external field predicts instead, inside that boundary, that pairs aligned with the Galactic field move more slowly. Newton predicts no difference.

Before we start: the words we need

Wide binaries. They are pairs of stars that orbit one around the other, very far from each other: thousands of times the distance between the Earth and the Sun. This distance is called the astronomical unit (au). The stars of a wide binary attract each other very weakly.

Gaia. It is a satellite that measures the position and the motion of a great many stars. Its data come out in blocks, called releases. DR3 is the release already published.

Newton's gravity and MOND. In galaxies the stars far from the centre orbit faster than expected. Visible matter alone, with Newton's law, is not enough. One answer is dark matter. Another is MOND, an idea of the physicist Milgrom: below a very small acceleration, a₀ (read a-zero), gravity is stronger than Newton's. The acceleration says how much the velocity changes every second.

The boost. It is the number by which Newton's gravity is multiplied. It is written ν (read nu). In MM the law is ν(y) = 1/(1 − e^(−√y)). Here y is Newton's acceleration divided by a₀. When y is large, ν is almost 1: Newton holds. When y is small, ν grows. In MM the scale is a₀ = cH₀/6: c is the speed of light and H₀ is the Hubble constant, which says how fast the universe is expanding today.

QUMOND and the external field. QUMOND is a version of MOND written with a field equation, which computes gravity at every point of space. In QUMOND a pair of stars also feels the gravity of the Galaxy around it: it is the external field. In English it is called EFE, external field effect.

Isotropic and anisotropic. Isotropic means the same in all directions. Anisotropic means that it changes with direction.

The words of the MM framework.

  • A premise is a starting sentence of the author: a fixed point, like a postulate.
  • A reading is an interpretation, written so that it can be put to the test. We also say that a body reads a field when its motion takes it into account.
  • Registered means written and fixed with its date before doing the calculations. So it cannot be changed afterwards.
  • The MCD is the greatest common divisor. An example: the MCD of 6 and 9 is 3. In the MM framework the MCD is the common unit that a relation counts.
  • The MM chain is the row of choices of MM 2.56 for wide binaries: a₀ = cH₀/6, the law ν, the field of the Galaxy and QUMOND.

Two measures of how well a hypothesis works.

  • The symbol σ (sigma) indicates the typical error. "2σ" means a distance of two typical errors.
  • The likelihood says how probable a hypothesis makes the observed data. Its logarithm is used, the log-likelihood. The higher it is, the better.

Part A: the prediction

This part is a registration record. It was deposited before Gaia DR4, the next release of Gaia data, planned for 2 December 2026.

A1. The question

Near the Sun, a wide binary sits in the gravitational field of the Galaxy. Two readings make different predictions for the internal motion of the pair, that is, for how the two stars move with respect to each other.

readinginside the boundary radius R_boutside
L* (MM)the pair reads only its own field: a MOND-like boost from its own field, the same in every directionQUMOND with the external field of the Galaxy
QUMOND + external field (MOND)the field of the Galaxy enters everywhere: the boost is weaker along the direction of the field of the Galaxythe same
Newtonno boost, no dependence on directionthe same

Let us read the table.

  • L*, the reading of MM. Inside a boundary radius R_b the pair reads only its own field. It has a MOND-like boost, the same in all directions. Outside the boundary QUMOND with the external field holds.
  • QUMOND with the external field. The field of the Galaxy enters everywhere. The boost is weaker along the direction of the field of the Galaxy.
  • Newton. No boost and no dependence on direction.

The boundary. R_b is the radius where the Newtonian field of the pair equals that of the Galaxy.

  • For a pair of 1.5 M☉ near the Sun, R_b = 7,773 au. M☉ is the mass of the Sun, used as a unit.
  • It is 0.861 r_M. Here r_M = √(GM/a₀) is the MOND radius: the distance at which the Newtonian acceleration of the pair drops to a₀. G is the gravitational constant, M the mass.
  • a₀ = cH₀/6 = 1.0914·10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². The unit m s⁻² means metres per second squared. 10⁻¹⁰ means divided by ten billion.

The field. The Newtonian field of the Galaxy near the Sun is e_N = 1.3488 a₀. It comes from the speed of the Sun around the centre, V₀ = 233 km/s, and from its distance from the centre, R₀ = 8.21 kpc. A kpc (kiloparsec) is a thousand parsecs; a parsec is a little more than three light years.

What is observed. Inside the boundary two kinds of pairs are compared.

  • The aligned pairs: the line between the two stars, seen on the sky, is close to the direction of the centre of the Galaxy (the exact rule is in section A2).
  • The pairs across.

With L* and with Newton the aligned pairs move as fast as those across. With QUMOND they move more slowly.

What the test can separate. This test separates QUMOND from L* and Newton. The amplitude test of MM 2.56, instead, separates Newton from the other readings.

A2. What is measured and with which rule (fixed before the data)

The sample.

  • They are the wide binaries of El-Badry, Rix and Heintz (2021). They had already been downloaded on 16 October 2026 for MM 2.56.
  • Only the well-measured pairs are kept. The quality cuts were frozen on 11 October 2026: R < 0.01, RUWE < 1.4, MSMS, d < 200 pc, ϖ/σ_ϖ > 20, compatible parallaxes, 3 < M_G < 12, proper-motion error on u < 0.1, depth term < 0.1, u < 3. What the main ones mean:
  • R is the probability that the two stars are close only by chance, without being a true pair;
  • RUWE is an index of how well Gaia measured the star;
  • MSMS means that both stars are normal stars, like the Sun;
  • d is the distance from us;
  • ϖ (read varpi) is the parallax, the small apparent shift that gives the distance; ϖ/σ_ϖ > 20 means that it is measured very well;
  • M_G says how bright the star is.
  • The masses are derived from the light of the stars (photometric masses).
  • x_p = s/r_M. Here s is the projected separation: the distance between the two stars as we see it on the sky.

The velocity. u = |v_⊥|/v_N.

  • v_⊥ is the part of the relative velocity on the sky perpendicular to the line between the two stars. There is no perspective correction.
  • v_N = √(GM/s) is the velocity that Newton would give.
  • In words: u says how fast the pair is compared with Newton's velocity.

The angle. φ (read phi) is the angle between the line of the pair and the direction of the centre of the Galaxy, both seen on the sky.

  • The pairs are aligned if |cos φ| ≥ 1/√2, that is, if the angle between the line of the pair and the direction of the centre is at most half a right angle, in one direction or the other. Otherwise they are across.
  • The pairs that on the sky lie within about 17° of the centre or of the anticentre, the direction opposite to the centre, are removed (|ê_p| < 0.3). Here |ê_p| is the length of the direction of the centre projected on the sky: it is small when the pair lies, on the sky, almost in front of the centre or of the anticentre.

The statistic. Δ_φ = ⟨u⟩_aligned / ⟨u⟩_across − 1. The brackets ⟨ ⟩ indicate the mean.

  • In words: the mean u of the aligned pairs is divided by that of the pairs across, and 1 is subtracted. If the two means are equal, Δ_φ = 0.
  • It is computed in five bands of x_p. The key band is 0.3-0.86, inside the boundary.

The uncertainty. The bootstrap over the pairs is used, with 2,000 resamples: the calculation is redone many times, each time drawing at random from the pairs. No star appears in more than one pair.

The reading rule, in the key band.

  • "No QUMOND anisotropy inside the boundary" (favours L* or Newton): if the data lie more than 2σ beyond the QUMOND interval, on the side of L*, and within 2σ of the L* interval.
  • "QUMOND-like anisotropy" in the opposite case.
  • "Not decidable" otherwise.

The control. In the control band (0.05-0.2) all readings predict from zero to 2 %. If the data there exceed 3σ, the reading carries the note "systematic in the control". A systematic is an error that always shifts in the same direction.

A3. The predictions (simulated before opening the data)

The models.

  • Newton.
  • QUMOND with the external field and the vector force: the part of the force along the line of the pair and the part across are computed. The calculation starts from the phantom density: in QUMOND the extra gravity behaves as if there were an invisible mass in empty space. A three-dimensional integral is used, for two equal masses.
  • L*, with a smooth junction at the boundary that conserves energy: the passage from inside to outside is gradual.

The simulated population.

  • 4,000 orbits, isotropic, that is, oriented at random.
  • The apocentre is the point of the orbit where the two stars are farthest apart. The apocentres are distributed log-uniformly between 0.15 and 5 r_M: every factor of distance has the same weight.
  • The eccentricity e says how flattened an orbit is. Its distribution is p(e) ∝ e^1.2.
  • The orbits are computed for 40 periods with an adaptive leapfrog, a calculation method with alternating steps that adapts by itself.
  • Each orbit is seen from 4 lines of sight chosen at random.

The simulated measurement. It is made like that of the real data: errors on mass and distance, noise on u, fake test pairs, the same cuts.

The runs. They were computed on AWS, an online computing service, before opening the data.

How to read the intervals.

  • There is a built-in check: Newton gives zero, as it must.
  • The paired difference L* − QUMOND in the key band is +1.1 % ± 0.4 % in periods 0-10 and +2.5 % ± 0.8 % in periods 30-40.
  • The expected power with DR3 is 0.8σ: the difference between the readings is smaller than the error. "Not decidable" was written as the most likely outcome before the data.

A4. A first look at today's data (DR3)

Key band. There are 4,140 pairs. Δ_φ = +0.018 ± 0.027.

  • It is 0.2σ from the L* interval, 0.4σ from Newton and 0.8σ from QUMOND.
  • Reading: not decidable with DR3. The data do not separate the readings.

Control band. There are 17,128 pairs. The value is +0.027 ± 0.013: 2.1σ, below the 3σ threshold.

  • The control band of an earlier test (F-46, August 2026, 300-2,000 au) also showed a small excess of the same sign.
  • The two tests are not fully independent.
  • There could be a systematic that depends on the orientation of the pairs. It has to be understood before DR4.

The spread of u. In the data u is more scattered than in the simulations. The coefficient of variation, that is, the spread divided by the mean, is 0.84-0.87 in the data against 0.69-0.70 in the simulations. So the real uncertainty is larger than the expected one.

A5. The rule for the new data: an exact replica

Same rule. The same pairs, re-measured in DR4, go through the same band, the same statistic, the same predictions and the same rule.

The steps. The data are downloaded; they are converted into the format of the catalogue; the pairs that in DR4 turn out to have a non-single star are removed; the sample is built; the analysis is done. The names of the programs are in the final section.

Validity. At least 3,000 pairs in the key band and 1,000 per class are needed.

The dry run on DR3. A dry run is a rehearsal on data already seen, to check that everything works.

  • The sample builder, copied, reproduces the DR3 sample bit for bit.
  • 3,000 pairs downloaded from the Gaia archive in October reproduce the same pairs, bands and classes, with u within 0.27 %.

The expected outcome. With this observable about ten times the DR3 pairs would be needed to reach 2σ. So even with DR4 the most likely outcome remains "not decidable". The record fixes the test in time.

What is not in this record. A more powerful analysis: the shape of the distribution, all the bands, the errors pair by pair. If it is done, it will be registered separately before 2 December.

A6. What the prediction rests on

The reading L*.

  • A pair is read as isolated inside a sharp boundary.
  • In MM this is a rule, not a derived law (Part B).
  • Part C shows that a premise of the author, with two declared choices, gives the boundary and the isotropy inside the boundary.

The junction at the boundary.

  • With a sharp switch, isotropic inside and anisotropic outside, the force does not conserve energy.
  • The junction used here is smooth and conserves energy: it is a shell from 7,773 to 8,550 au. It is a choice of the author.
  • How the result would change with other smooth junctions has not been measured.

An idealised population.

  • In the simulations the starting distribution is known, the direction of the external field is fixed, the masses are equal in the dynamics, and there are no tides or encounters with other stars.
  • In the real data the population has to be estimated, and part of the signal may be absorbed.
  • The simulated numbers are lower bounds: the pairs needed are at least those.

Part B: the dynamics of MM behind the prediction

The starting point. MM reads gravity from counts on a graph, that is, a network of connected points. The closure rule is ψ + Nψ = 2 (ψ is read psi).

How it was done. Since 26 October 2026 the dynamics has been developed level by level. Each step was registered before the calculation.

B1. What holds

  • Level 0, the inventory. The constraints of the dynamics are already in MM's Regge-type action. The Regge action is a way of writing Einstein's gravity on a network of flat pieces, like a ball made of many triangles.
  • Level 1.
  • The linearised Bianchi identity follows from the action on the lattice, with a binding stress. The Bianchi identity is a mathematical rule of Einstein's gravity, linked to the conservation of energy and momentum.
  • If the closure is read as a law in motion, it adds a scalar mode, an extra kind of vibration, when the trace varies. The trace is a sum of components of the field.
  • The weak field. When gravity is weak, the dynamics of MM gives linearised Einstein gravity: the simplified form of Einstein's equations for small deformations of space-time.
  • Level 3, cosmology. The local homogeneous model, that is, the same at every point, is exactly that of Friedmann. Friedmann's equations describe how the scale of a universe that is the same at every point changes in time, whether it expands or contracts. The density ρ(a) (ρ is read rho), that is, how the density changes with the expansion, is not fixed by the framework.

B2. Matter in motion (Level 2)

Compact stars. They are very dense stars, like neutron stars.

  • A distant reading held fixed (D1-0) does not admit generic motions.
  • A "breathing" distant reading (D1-a) gives:
  • a main mode of vibration at 6.68 kHz with the matter model SLy and at 7.14 kHz with APR4. SLy and APR4 are two models of how matter is made inside neutron stars. A kHz is a thousand vibrations per second;
  • a tidal deformability of +22 % compared with general relativity. It is how much a star is deformed under the gravity of another.
  • Open: whether D1-a is stable is not decided.

B3. Galaxy clusters (Level 3)

A galaxy cluster is a very large group of galaxies bound by gravity.

  • Without a₀, the dynamics of Levels 1-2 is that of Newton: a factor 6.7 of mass is missing.
  • With a₀ a factor 1.5-2.2 remains, as in MOND.

Some names, before reading the steps.

  • Cassini is the probe that orbited Saturn. Its radio tracking limits the quadrupole Q₂ of the field around the Sun: a small deformation of the Sun's gravity that would change the orbit of Saturn.
  • KiDS is a large survey of the sky that measures weak lensing: the gravity of galaxies bends a little the light of distant galaxies.
  • AQUAL, AeST and Galileon are other theories that modify gravity.
  • N₃ is the number of pairs that would be needed to see the effect at 3σ.

The steps, one by one.

  1. An algebraic hook, L = 4(ψ₀ − 1). It is not a derived junction. The reading K1 falls on KiDS. The realisation with QUMOND falls on Cassini.
  2. The rule L*: inside R_b each system reads its own field. It passes Cassini by construction. KiDS admits it. In binaries it gives a plateau, a flat stretch.
  3. A local dynamics: QUMOND, a ceiling on the boost, a curvature reading. QUMOND is at 18.5σ and the ceiling at 13.7σ on Cassini (Saturn's Q₂). The curvature falls on the test of granularity, another check of the series.
  4. AQUAL and a combination of field and curvature (D3). AQUAL is at 23.5σ on Cassini. D3 passes Cassini, but falls in the discs of galaxies poor in stars.
  5. An extra field (of AeST type) and the cubic Galileon. Not decidable at the threshold. The tidal quadrupole grows inwards.
  6. L* written as a non-local law, with a rule for systems. Non-local here means that it depends on the whole system, not on the point. The trees of the systems (who is inside whom) are consistent, and the two-body force is validated.
  7. Two-body QUMOND with the external field, in three dimensions. At the boundary there is a step of +6 % relative to the isotropic mean.
  8. A population of pairs with the mean, central force. N₃ = 1.3·10⁵ pairs would be needed to reach 3σ: the outcome is "uncertain".
  9. A population with the vector force. N₃ = 1-2.5·10⁴ pairs would be needed, in an idealised case. The main signature is the anisotropy.
  10. The test on DR3 (Part A): not decidable with DR3.
  11. The author's premise on the observation MCD (steps 11, 11b, 11c; Part C). It gives the boundary and the isotropy of L*. On DR3 its law fits best, but a posteriori, that is, after seeing the data. DR4 is not expected to separate it from the chain.

Step 9 in more detail.

  • The transverse part of the external field is not small. At the boundary U ≈ −0.16, against ν_e K = −0.385 in the linear limit.
  • It makes the orbits more eccentric over tens of periods. It is similar to the Kozai cycles, in which a distant third body gradually stretches the orbit of a pair.
  • Inside the boundary L* switches it off.
  • The registered corrections are six:
  • a non-commensurate sampling, that is, without regular repetitions;
  • a late time window;
  • a conservative QUMOND field, derived from a potential. The check V8: the computed transverse part agrees within 6 % with the one derived from the potential;
  • a smooth junction;
  • an adaptive integrator;
  • a convergence run, with substeps twice as fine and the energy checked over all 40 periods. It converges: N₃ = 2.6·10⁴ and 1.0·10⁴, within −5 % and +1 % of the official run.

B5. In one line

  • Where the dynamics coincides with Einstein, it holds.
  • The part proper to MM, that is, a₀ = cH₀/6 and the junction with it, is still a rule, L*. It is not a derived law.
  • The local versions of the junction tried so far fall on Cassini or on granularity.
  • L* makes a falsifiable prediction for wide binaries: it is Part A. Falsifiable means that the data can disprove it.

Part C: a premise that gives L*, and what it predicts for the amplitude

C1. The premise and its translation (step 11)

The premise is the author's, of 28 October 2026. The galaxy in which a system sits acts on the observation MCD of the system. And this MCD, in turn, determines the system's own field.

The translation into a formula is by the AI. It has two declared choices.

  • The field of the Galaxy enters as a scale, that is, a number, not as a vector with a direction. So the reading is isotropic.
  • The boost with which a system is read cannot exceed the boost with which the Galaxy reads the point where the system is. In a formula: ν_eff = min(ν(y), ν(e_N)), that is, y_eff = max(y, e_N), with ν(y) = 1/(1 − e^(−√y)). Here min means the smaller of the two, max the larger.

This translation is called T_max.

  • Two other translations, a sum and a quadrature (the square root of the sum of the squares), do not give a sharp boundary.
  • Other choices are possible. And the reason for the minimum is not derived: "why the minimum" remains open.

What follows from it.

  • The boundary and the isotropy of L*. The boundary falls where y = e_N: at 0.861 r_M, that is, 7,773 au for 1.5 M☉. Inside the boundary the pair reads only itself, the same in every direction. In L* boundary and isotropy were posited, that is, assumed; here they follow from the premise.
  • Outside the boundary T_max and L* part ways. T_max gives an isotropic boost ν(e_N) = 1.455 and no step at the boundary. L*, which outside is QUMOND with the external field, gives 1.26-1.33, with the anisotropy.
  • The Solar System. The anomalous acceleration is of the order of 10⁻³⁴¹ m s⁻² at 9.5 au. It is a very small number, far below any measurement. T_max passes.
  • The globular cluster NGC 2419. A globular cluster is a ball of a great many stars. Here T_max gives a boost ν ≈ 3.3-3.4 at 86 pc, while the data give at most about 1.1 (≲ 1.1). T_max falls, as the book finds for every law made of the internal and external accelerations alone. This outcome hits the translation T_max. The book's way out is the history of the cluster, which the premise does not replace.

Let us read the table. ΔlogL is the difference from the best hypothesis: 0 for the best, negative numbers for the others.

  • T_max is the best (0). Its two-body variant gives −0.6.
  • MOND in the simple form gives −1.1, in the standard form −1.9. They are two ways of writing the passage between Newton and MOND.
  • The MM chain gives −3.4 in the version called 674 and −5.3 in the one called 730. Chain 674 uses QUMOND with a₀ = cH₀/6, and L* outside the boundary.
  • MLS, that is, the law ν with a₀ = 1.2·10⁻¹⁰, gives −9.8.
  • Newton gives −11.1.

The outcome with the registered criterion.

  • T_max against the MM chain: +3.4, with subsets 0.2, 9.1 and 5.3. Not decidable.
  • T_max against Newton: +11.1, with subsets 7.8, 13.6 and 7.4. T_max favoured.
  • The registered two-body variant, with equal masses, gives the same classes. This does not show that the result does not depend on every other way of treating the two bodies.

Where the difference comes from. Almost all of it from the low-acceleration band. There T_max is an isotropic boost of 1.455.

A posteriori. The idea of T_max outside the boundary came partly from these same data. The best MOND hypothesis of MM 2.56 has a boost of about 1.45 there. On DR3 this is coherence, not a test. And the differences of 1-2 between the MOND-like hypotheses lie within the noise of about ±3 seen in MM 2.56: the noise is how much the result changes by pure chance in the simulations.

C3. The rule for the new data

The chain of steps. It is the same as for MM 2.56: the data are downloaded, they are converted, and the forward simulations of its six hypotheses are made. Then the simulations with the two registered libraries of T_max and their evaluation are added. The new rule is added to that of MM 2.56 without changing it. The names of the programs are in the final section.

Frozen with the rule.

  • 4,000 simulated pairs in the control band, as in MM 2.56.
  • The thresholds 5 and 2. They are a practical criterion registered beforehand. They are not calibrated with simulations of false positives, that is, of cases in which the test would favour the wrong hypothesis.

DR4 is not an independent replica of DR3. The pairs are largely the same, re-measured with more data. The test is prospective, that is, it looks ahead, because the rule is fixed before the data.

The expected outcome: not decidable (step 11c, registered before the calculation). Mock data were made with the same machinery: 40 mock experiments for each true hypothesis, with from 1 to 8 times the DR3 pairs of the low-acceleration band.

  • With the DR3 pairs, as in the DR4 replica, the rule picks the right reading with probability 0.12 (5/40) if T_max is true, and 0.03 (1/40) if the chain is true. In one sentence: "with the same pairs DR4 probably does not separate them".
  • With 8 times the pairs the probability is still only 0.38 and 0.05: the advantage does not grow. The targets of 50 % and 80 % are not reached within 7,304 simulated pairs. Beyond that number the threshold is not estimated.
  • When the chain is true, the criterion favours T_max by mistake in 3 experiments out of 40 at the size of DR3 (0.07-0.12 at the various sizes). It is a Monte Carlo estimate, that is, made with random simulations, very uncertain.

A compatible explanation. It is an a posteriori diagnostic, that is, a check to understand why, registered before running it.

  • T_max and the chain differ mainly in the amplitude of the boost.
  • A change of amplitude can be absorbed by the mass scale, which the rule leaves free and common to the two bands.
  • With 8 times the pairs each reading moves towards the other by changing the masses by about 7 %.
  • With the mass scale fixed exactly at the true value, 8 times the pairs give 0.82 and 0.65. It is a favourable case, not a requirement. Which precision on the masses would be enough is not simulated.
  • The diagnostic does not isolate the cause. Two other causes are not excluded: the Monte Carlo noise of the models of the control band, fixed at the three registered replicas, and the coarse calculation grids.

The +3.4 seen on DR3 does not choose between the two readings. In a Gaussian approximation of the simulated distributions (bell-shaped), the ratio of the densities is about 1.1. It is a description made a posteriori.

Two checks of T_max with DR4.

  • The amplitude, with this rule.
  • The direction, with Part A. Inside the boundary: no anisotropy, as for L*. Outside the boundary T_max predicts none, while L* and QUMOND predict from +0.7 % to +4.8 %. With this observable those bands are not decidable.

C4. What it means

  • The premise, with two declared choices, turns the rule L* into a consequence inside the boundary. And it adds a prediction outside the boundary.
  • On DR3, a posteriori, its law fits best of the eight hypotheses. But it is not distinguishable from the MM chain.
  • With DR4 and the registered rule the expected outcome is again "not decidable" (step 11c). The amplitude test separates the two MOND-like readings from Newton. It hardly separates them from each other.
  • The outcomes concern the translation T_max, not the premise nor MM.

Glossary

The words of the framework (MM)

  • L* (L-star). The reading of MM in which a pair, inside a boundary, reads only its own field, the same in every direction. In MM it is a rule, not a derived law.
  • MCD. The greatest common divisor. In the MM framework it is the common unit that a relation counts. The observation MCD is the one with which a system is observed.
  • MM chain. The row of choices of MM 2.56 for wide binaries: a₀ = cH₀/6, the law ν, the field of the Galaxy and QUMOND.
  • Premise. A starting sentence of the author, a fixed point like a postulate.
  • Reading. An interpretation, written so that it can be put to the test. A body reads a field when its motion takes it into account.
  • Registered. Written and fixed with its date before the calculations, so that it cannot be changed afterwards.
  • T_max. The translation into a formula of the premise of 28 October 2026: the boost of a system does not exceed the one with which the Galaxy reads the point where it is.

The words of physics

  • a₀. A very small acceleration: below a₀, in MOND, gravity is stronger than Newton's. In MM a₀ = cH₀/6.
  • A posteriori. Said of a check made after seeing the data.
  • Acceleration. How much the velocity changes every second.
  • Anisotropic, isotropic. That changes with direction; the same in all directions.
  • au (astronomical unit). The mean distance between the Earth and the Sun.
  • Boost (ν). The number by which Newton's gravity is multiplied.
  • Bootstrap. A way of estimating the uncertainty by redoing the calculation many times on samples drawn at random from the data.
  • Boundary (R_b). The radius where the Newtonian field of a pair equals that of the Galaxy.
  • Dry run. A rehearsal on data already seen, to check that everything works.
  • Eccentricity. How flattened an orbit is.
  • External field. The gravity that comes from outside a system; for binaries near the Sun it is that of the Galaxy.
  • Gaia, DR3, DR4. Gaia is a satellite that measures positions and motions of stars; DR3 is the data release already published, DR4 the next one.
  • Likelihood. How probable a hypothesis makes the observed data.
  • Log-likelihood. The logarithm of the likelihood: the higher it is, the better a hypothesis accounts for the data.
  • MOND, QUMOND, AQUAL. MOND is Milgrom's idea: below a₀ gravity is stronger. QUMOND and AQUAL are two versions written with a field equation.
  • MOND radius (r_M). The distance at which the Newtonian acceleration of a body drops to a₀.
  • Monte Carlo. A calculation made with many random simulations.
  • Phantom density. In QUMOND, the invisible mass that seems to sit in empty space.
  • Projected separation (s). The distance between the two stars of a pair as we see it on the sky.
  • Sigma (σ). The typical error of a measurement.
  • Systematic. An error that always shifts in the same direction.
  • Wide binaries. Pairs of stars very far from each other that orbit one around the other.
