Abstract

This record registers, before Gaia DR4 (planned for 2 December 2026), a test of wide binaries. It is one of the checks of Mathematical Materiality (MM): the outcome concerns MM's reading of wide binaries, not the framework. Two MM readings are compared. The main one is a chain of correspondences that takes no parameter from the binaries. It links three things that seem unrelated: The field equation (QUMOND) is borrowed. The declared alternative is the reading of MM 2.54, which says that wide binaries are Newtonian. MOND with the external field is reported as a reference. The data and the rules are fixed now. The pairs of El-Badry, Rix and Heintz (2021) will be re-measured in DR4, with the cuts frozen on 11 October 2026. Stars with a DR4 non-single-star solution are removed. A forward simulation models hidden companions. The decision rule compares the chain with Newton and has written validity conditions: the test can favour one reading, or come out inconclusive or not valid.

Version 2.56 of the Mathematical Materiality (MM) series, October 2026. Mathematical Materiality, MM for short, is the author's framework of ideas. Each work in the series has a number.

What this record is. It is a registration record: it contains the protocol, the code, the data sample and a dry run.

  • A protocol is the list of the steps of a test, written before doing it.
  • Registered means written and fixed with its date before seeing the new data. In this way it cannot be changed afterwards.
  • A dry run is a rehearsal on data already seen, to check that everything works.
  • The record refers to MM 2.54 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23418156). 2.54 contains a census, that is, a list of the entries of MM. There, wide binaries are among the entries with a future check.

What is put to the test. This is one of MM's checks. The test can favour one of two MM readings. Or it can come out inconclusive, or not valid. Whatever the outcome, it concerns the reading of wide binaries, not the framework.

At the end you find how the work was done and a glossary.

Before starting: the words you need

Wide binaries. They are pairs of stars that orbit each other. The two stars are very far apart: thousands of times the distance between the Earth and the Sun. The two stars attract each other very weakly. This is why they are a good test bench for gravity at very small accelerations.

Gaia. It is a satellite that measures the position and the motion of a great many stars. Its data come out in blocks, called releases. DR3 is the release already published.

Acceleration and the scale a₀. Acceleration says how much the velocity changes every second. It is measured in m s⁻², metres per second squared. In galaxies the stars far from the centre rotate too fast. Newton's gravity, with visible matter alone, predicts lower speeds. MOND is an idea of the physicist Milgrom. There is a very small acceleration, a₀ (read a-zero). Below a₀ gravity becomes stronger.

The boost. It is the number by which Newton's gravity is multiplied. The law is written ν(y) = 1/(1 − e^(−√y)) (ν is read nu). Here y is Newton's acceleration divided by a₀. When y is large, ν is almost 1 and Newton holds. When y is small, ν grows. The asymptotic boost of a reading, that is, the one of the widest pairs, is written η (read eta).

cH₀. c is the speed of light. H₀ (read H-zero) is the Hubble constant: it says how fast the universe is expanding today. The product cH₀ is an acceleration. Its value depends on the measurement of H₀: here 67.4 and 73.04 are used, in kilometres per second per megaparsec.

QUMOND and the external field. QUMOND is a version of MOND written with a field equation. The equation computes gravity at every point of space. A pair of stars near the Sun also feels the gravity of the Milky Way: it is the external field.

The words of the MM framework.

  • A reading is an interpretation, written so that it can be put to the test.
  • The chain is the main reading of this record: a row of choices linked like the links of a chain (section 1).

Likelihood. It says how probable a hypothesis makes the observed data. One uses its logarithm, the log-likelihood, written L. The higher it is, the better.

1. What is compared

The chain with H₀ = 67.4: the main MM reading. It is made of four links.

  1. a₀ = cH₀/6 = 1.091·10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². It comes from the two-mirror reading of MM 2.54. 10⁻¹⁰ means divided by ten billion.
  2. The boost law ν(y) = 1/(1 − e^(−√y)), called the reading-series law. It is the MLS form of the radial acceleration relation, the law observed in disc galaxies. MLS are the initials of McGaugh, Lelli and Schombert.
  3. The external field of the Galaxy, derived from the rotation of the Milky Way: V = 233 km/s is the rotation speed of the Galaxy at the distance of the Sun. R = 8.2 kpc is its distance from the centre. The field is worth 1.966 a₀. A kpc (kiloparsec) is a thousand parsecs; a parsec is a little more than three light years.
  4. The QUMOND field equation, with all the multipoles. The multipoles are all the details of the shape of the field. It is borrowed from MOND, not derived from MM.

The other hypotheses.

  • The chain with H₀ = 73.04. It is the same, with a₀ = 1.183·10⁻¹⁰. The external field is then worth 1.814 a₀. It serves to see how much the result depends on H₀: it is a sensitivity test.
  • Newton. Newton's gravity, without boost. It is MM's declared alternative: it is the reading that MM 2.54 gives for binaries.
  • MOND simple and MOND standard. QUMOND with the external field and a₀ = 1.2·10⁻¹⁰. They are two ways of writing the passage between Newton and MOND. They serve as reference.
  • The series, with a₀ = 1.2·10⁻¹⁰. It is the reading-series law, with the a₀ measured by SPARC. SPARC is a catalogue of galaxies. It was an earlier working reading. It serves as reference.

What is special about the chain. It takes no parameter from the binaries. It links three things that seem unrelated:

  • the expansion of the universe, through a₀ = cH₀/6;
  • the law read in galaxies;
  • the rotation of the Milky Way.

The expected boost. The asymptotic boost of the chain is the one of the widest pairs. It is η = 1.327 with H₀ = 67.4. It is 1.362 with H₀ = 73.04. In words: for the widest pairs gravity would be 1.327 times Newton's, with H₀ = 67.4.

2. The data

The pairs.

  • They are the wide binaries of the catalogue of El-Badry, Rix and Heintz (2021).
  • They were downloaded on 16 October 2026, with three conditions: ϖ₁ > 4 mas, sepAU > 200, R < 0.1. There are 167,301 pairs.
  • ϖ (read variant pi) is the parallax, the small apparent shift that gives the distance; the mas is a thousandth of an arcsecond, a very small angle;
  • sepAU is the separation between the two stars in astronomical units;
  • R is the probability that the two stars are close only by chance.
  • With the frozen cuts, the sample reproduces pair by pair the one used during development: 913 low-acceleration pairs and 21,489 control pairs.

The values of the new release. The protocol was deposited before Gaia DR4. It is the next release of Gaia data, planned for 2 December 2026. The same pairs will be re-measured in DR4.

  • The DR3→DR4 neighbourhood table is used. It says which star of DR4 corresponds to which star of DR3.
  • If that table is missing, the stars are searched for by position. The epoch J2017.5 is used, with a maximum distance of 0.3″ (0.3 arcseconds).
  • Only one-to-one links are kept: one star of DR3 with only one star of DR4.
  • The stars that in DR4 have a non-single-star solution are removed: Gaia does not describe them as simple, isolated stars. This is a new selection, not simulated.

The conversion. The DR4 values are put into the format used during development. Then they go through the same chain of programs, with the quality cuts frozen on 11 October 2026:

  • BinType = MSMS: both stars are on the main sequence, that is, stars that burn hydrogen in their centre, like the Sun;
  • d < 200 pc: the distance from us is below 200 parsecs;
  • ϖ/σ_ϖ > 20: the parallax is measured very well;
  • parallaxes of the two stars consistent within 3σ (σ is the typical error);
  • 3 < M_G < 12: M_G says how luminous the star is;
  • error on u < 0.1. Here u is the relative velocity of the two stars, seen on the sky, divided by the velocity of a Newtonian circular orbit at their separation seen on the sky;
  • depth term < 0.1;
  • u < 3.

3. How the comparison is made and how the decision is taken

The exact rules are in the protocol. Here we summarise them.

The forward simulation. One simulates on the computer what should be seen with each hypothesis.

  • The hidden companions are taken into account: third stars nearby that are not seen but change the measurements. These are simulated:
  • the fit of the position;
  • the quality index RUWE;
  • El-Badry's cut;
  • the 2+2 systems, that is, two pairs bound to each other;
  • the near-twin pairs, of two almost equal stars.
  • There are two bands. The control band has 4000 random pairs with x ≥ 10. The low-acceleration band has x < 1. Here x is the Newtonian acceleration of the pair divided by a₀.
  • There is a grid of cases. The twins are 4 % or 20 %. The companions per star go from 0 to 0.4 (0-0.4). The replicas are 3: three repetitions with different random numbers.
  • In all: 6 hypotheses, 60 files.

The likelihood. Poisson statistics is used on 26 classes per band. Each class has the same number of pairs.

  • The twin fraction is common to the two bands. The mass scale is also common: it goes from 0.85 to 1.15.
  • The companion fraction is separate for each band.

Validity. The test is not valid if one of these conditions is missing.

  • V1: at least 600 pairs in the low band and 15,000 in the control band.
  • V2: the libraries must have 200,000 computed orbits. The error on the energy must be below 2·10⁻² at the 99th percentile, that is, in 99 per cent of the orbits. No pair must start unbound. The grid must be complete. The fingerprints of the data must be equal.
  • V4: the best hypothesis must have a small deviance. The deviance measures how far a hypothesis departs from the data. In each band it must be at most 3 times the number of classes (≤ 3 ×).

The decision. D = L(chain 67.4) − L(Newton): the difference in log-likelihood between the chain and Newton. D_k is the same difference, redone removing one replica.

  • Chain favoured: D ≥ +5 and every D_k ≥ +2.
  • Newton favoured: D ≤ −5 and every D_k ≤ −2.
  • Otherwise: inconclusive.
  • E = L(chain) − L(best MOND) is also reported. The chain is "close to the best MOND" if E ≥ −5.

4. The dry run on today's data (DR3)

These data had already been seen during development.

Same data, same simulations.

  • The DR3 values were written in the format of the Gaia archive and then converted. They give the same pairs and the same quantities as the development files: the difference is 0.
  • The simulations for Newton and for the chain were redone, with the same row order. They are identical to the ones that already existed.

The numbers of the rule.

  • The test is valid.
  • D = +7.64, with the subsets +7.56, +4.50 and +2.07.

What the rule would say on DR3. The decision would be "chain favoured over Newton". But the minimum margin is only just met: the lowest subset, +2.07, exceeds the threshold +2 by a little.

The download. The passage from DR3 to DR4 was tried on DR3, both by identifier and by position.

What could not be tested before DR4.

  • The DR4 neighbourhood table itself.
  • The effect of the selection of non-single stars.
  • A full rerun of the grid on the new data.

5. Grade and declared limits

Grade B*. The DR3 data were partly seen before registration. And DR4 reuses DR3 stars and observations. So a favourable outcome will be a confirmation on non-independent data, not a blind prediction.

The limits.

  • The generator of the simulated pairs was not rewritten from scratch. The tails at 3σ, that is, the most extreme cases, were not studied.
  • The masses have a free common scale, not full distributions.
  • The eccentricity, that is, how flattened the orbits are, is fixed according to Hwang et al. 2022.
  • QUMOND is borrowed.
  • The Galactic V and R are fixed.
  • The selection of non-single stars is not simulated.
  • The correlation between the two proper motions, pmRA and pmDE, is not used. They are the motion on the sky in the two directions.

The scope of the test was reduced on purpose: this is one check among many.

The amendments. Any change after the deposit will be a dated addendum. It will have its reason and the outcome under both rules: the original one and the new one.

How the work was done

This section gathers the procedure. In the archived text it corresponds to sections 6 and 7 and to the file names of sections 2 and 4.

Who did the calculations. The author is not a professional physicist. The calculations were done with AI assistants (Claude, with two checks by Codex). No human referee reviewed them. A referee is an expert who checks a work before publication.

The verifications: two rounds of Codex/GPT-6, another AI assistant. The reports are in documenti/verifica_astra/.

  • The first round, on version 1 of the protocol, gave "not ready", with nine points. They were all addressed in version 2. The list is at the end of the protocol.
  • The second round, on version 2: eight points resolved, no new error. One point was partly open: an explicit check of unbound Newtonian orbits. And a clean "not valid" on an incomplete grid, instead of a crash of the program.
  • After the second round (valuta_dr4.py 2.1):
  • the Newtonian library is checked orbit by orbit: 0 unbound states out of 200,000;
  • an incomplete grid now gives "not valid". This was tested with a missing file and with a file of two replicas (risultati/prova_a_secco/prova_valutazione_casi.log).
  • There was no third round.

The registrations. Every file was registered before being run. The registration has a SHA-256 fingerprint and the UTC time (documenti/registri/). A SHA-256 fingerprint is a code computed from the content of a file: if the file changes even slightly, the fingerprint changes.

The reference text. The protocol is documenti/PROTOCOLLO_DR4_v2.md, in Italian. The archived text, in English, summarises it.

The files cited in the sections.

  • Section 2: the query and the SHA-256 of the download are in dati/. The selected sample, with the Gaia identifiers, is dati/campione_F.csv. El-Badry's catalogue is on VizieR, an archive of astronomical catalogues: J/MNRAS/506/2269. The download of the DR4 values is codice/registrazione/scarica_dr4.py; the conversion is converti_dr4.py, which puts the data in canonical order and with the SHA-256 fingerprints.
  • Section 4: the download tests are in risultati/scarico/.

What is in the package.

  • codice/registrazione/: the download, conversion, simulation, evaluation and dry-run programs (version 2; version 1 is in versione_1/).
  • codice/pipeline/: the programs of the development chain that they call, unchanged. The code contains the author's paths (E:...), to be adapted.
  • dati/: the query, the SHA-256 of the download, the selected sample with the Gaia identifiers.
  • risultati/:
  • the libraries: dynamic checks and SHA-256. The six libraries (217 MB each) are not included. They can be regenerated with orbite.py or orbite_mm.py. The seeds are 1, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16. FATT_PASSI is 1 for Newton and 4 for the others. The seed is the starting number of the random numbers;
  • the sample, the chain, the dry run, the download tests.
  • documenti/: the plan, the decision on the main reading, the protocol versions, the verification report, the registers.

Glossary

The words of the framework (MM)

  • Chain. The main reading of this record: a₀ = cH₀/6, the reading-series law, the field of the Milky Way and QUMOND.
  • Reading. An interpretation, written so that it can be put to the test.
  • Registered. Written and fixed with its date before seeing the new data.

The words of physics

  • a₀. A very small acceleration: below a₀, in MOND, gravity is stronger than Newton's. In MM a₀ = cH₀/6.
  • Acceleration. How much the velocity changes every second.
  • Boost (ν, η). The number by which Newton's gravity is multiplied; η is its asymptotic value, for the widest pairs.
  • Deviance. A number that measures how far a hypothesis departs from the data.
  • Dry run. A rehearsal on data already seen, to check that everything works.
  • External field. The gravity that comes from outside a system; for binaries near the Sun it is that of the Milky Way.
  • Gaia, DR3, DR4. Gaia is a satellite that measures positions and motions of stars; DR3 is the data release already published, DR4 the next one.
  • Hidden companions. Stars close to one of the two stars of the pair, which are not seen but change the measurements.
  • Log-likelihood (L). The logarithm of the likelihood: the higher it is, the better a hypothesis accounts for the data.
  • MOND, QUMOND. MOND is Milgrom's idea: below a₀ gravity is stronger. QUMOND is a version of it written with a field equation.
  • Parallax (ϖ). The small apparent shift of a star, which gives its distance.
  • Protocol. The list of the steps of a test, written before doing it.
  • Replicas. Repetitions of a simulation with different random numbers.
  • Sigma (σ). The typical error of a measurement.
  • Wide binaries. Pairs of stars very far apart that orbit each other.
